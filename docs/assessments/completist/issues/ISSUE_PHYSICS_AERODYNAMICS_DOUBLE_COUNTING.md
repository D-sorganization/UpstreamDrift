title: Critical Double Counting of Aerodynamic Lift Forces
labels: physics-error, critical
assignees: physics-team
---

## Description
The `AerodynamicsEngine` in `src/shared/python/physics/aerodynamics.py` appears to double-count the lift force generated by ball spin.

It implements both a `LiftModel` and a `MagnusModel`, which are enabled by default in `AerodynamicsConfig`.

- `LiftModel` calculates a lift force based on `spin_axis x velocity`.
- `MagnusModel` calculates a Magnus force based on `spin x velocity`.

Physically, the Magnus effect *is* the mechanism by which a spinning object generates lift in a fluid. By having both models active and summing their outputs (`total = drag + lift + magnus`), the simulation likely applies the same physical force twice (or 1.x times depending on coefficients).

## Expected Behavior
The simulation should use a single, unified model for spin-induced aerodynamic forces.
- Either remove `LiftModel` and rely solely on `MagnusModel` (which is more general as it handles side-spin/curvature naturally).
- Or explicitly document that `LiftModel` is for a different phenomenon (e.g., non-spin lift, which is negligible for spheres) and disable it by default for golf balls.

## Impact
- **Trajectory Distortion**: Lift forces are exaggerated, leading to "ballooning" trajectories that hang in the air longer than reality.
- **Carry Distance Error**: Overestimation of carry distance due to excessive lift.
- **Validation Failure**: Will not match TrackMan/Doppler radar trajectory curves.

## Recommended Fix
1. Disable `LiftModel` by default in `AerodynamicsConfig` if `MagnusModel` is active.
2. Refactor `AerodynamicsEngine` to prevent simultaneous activation of overlapping force models.
3. Verify `Cl` and `Cm` coefficients to ensure they correspond to the specific model being used (often `Cl` data in literature *is* the Magnus coefficient for a specific spin axis).
