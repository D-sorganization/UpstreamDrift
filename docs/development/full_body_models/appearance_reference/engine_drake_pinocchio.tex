\section{Drake and Pinocchio Draw the Shared Appearance Spec (R9a, \#12688)}

\subsection{Defect}
The Drake and Pinocchio MeshCat backends of \texttt{native\_viewer\_export}
drew grey capsules and the spec hint ellipsoids. Each carried its own copy of
the plain palette and a near-identical capsule/shape registration loop, so
neither showed the MuJoCo character's skin, garments or shoes.

\subsection{Design}
One engine-neutral builder, \texttt{backends/\_meshcat\_scene.py}, reads
\texttt{model\_appearance} and returns \texttt{SceneVisuals}, a tuple of
\texttt{VisualItem} records (name, spec body, kind, RGBA, pose in the spec
body frame, size or OBJ path, library material). Each backend is an adapter
that only translates items into its own geometry API.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Appearance mode} (the default document, as MuJoCo): the resolved
  skin, garment, blend, shoe and club meshes of
  \texttt{body\_meshes.resolve\_appearance} (written as OBJ by
  \texttt{\_body.body\_mesh\_files}) plus the parametric head replace every
  capsule and hint ellipsoid. Colours are the library materials; the floor is
  the document ground material. The trunk is sized from the spec hints
  (ML semi-axis, AP/ML aspect), trousers are charcoal hip to ankle, and the
  R7 head slot (\texttt{MeshReference}) is passed through unchanged.
\item \emph{Plain mode} (\texttt{plain=True}): the grey capsule skeleton in
  \texttt{library.PLAIN\_PALETTE}; head and club meshes remain.
\end{itemize}
Frames: items are in the spec body frame. Drake's URDF links are in that
frame, so the adapter registers the item pose directly; Pinocchio composes the
body placement ($T_{\mathrm{body}}\,T_{\mathrm{item}}$) explicitly. Visuals are
registered after the dynamics model exists and never enter the plant, so
physics and kinematics are unchanged.

\subsection{Contract}
\texttt{VisualItem} rejects an unknown kind, an RGBA outside $[0,1]$, a mesh
without a path (and vice versa) and non-positive sizes. \texttt{check\_bodies}
fails closed when an item sits on a body the engine does not have, and
\texttt{build\_scene\_visuals} fails closed when the document dresses no body
or names an unknown material (\texttt{ValueError}).

\subsection{Acceptance Evidence}
Engine-free tests (\texttt{test\_meshcat\_appearance.py}) check, for both
anthropometric specs and both backends: every dressed body has meshes; pelvis
wears the pants material, trunk the shirt, feet the shoe; trunk ML $>$ AP and
ML equals the spec hint semi-axis (5\,\%); RGBA equals the library; no local
RGBA literal remains. A cross-engine test exports the MuJoCo MJCF with the
same document and compares per-mesh colours (tolerance $10^{-5}$) and the
rigid-motion-invariant sorted centroid-distance signature of each mesh
(tolerance $10^{-5}$\,m; MJCF prints six significant digits).
Reproduce:
\texttt{python -m pytest tests/unit/tools/native\_viewer\_export/test\_meshcat\_appearance.py}.

\subsection{Limitations}
Native-render stills (address and impact, face-on and down-the-line, beside
MuJoCo) require pydrake, pinocchio, meshcat and headless Chromium together and
were not captured in this change; no rendering claim is made here.
