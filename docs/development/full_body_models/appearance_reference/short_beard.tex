\section*{Short Beard Visual Geometry}
The optional \texttt{short\_beard} style draws on the actual CC0 head skin,
not on the mass or collision model. An official LIV Golf team portrait of Jon
Rahm (\texttt{https://www.livgolf.com/legion-xiii}, accessed 2026-11-27)
shows a close-cropped beard and moustache; the procedural result is a stylized
interpretation, not a measured face or exact texture.

The head mesh uses dimensionless head-length coordinates, $x$ forward, $y$
lateral, $z$ upward. With $a=|y|$ and $t=\operatorname{clip}((a-0.09)/0.14,0,1)$,
the cheek boundary is $z<0.295+0.085t^2(3-2t)$. The skin is locally subdivided and clipped at the boundary, requiring
$x>0.13+0.10u^2(3-2u)$, $u=\operatorname{clip}((a-0.20)/0.12,0,1)$,
$z>0.075$ and an elliptical lip opening
$(y/0.09)^2+((z-0.27)/0.045)^2>1$. The shell's inward surface is offset
$0.0004$ head lengths along vertex normals; the outward surface adds $0.003$,
except the $0.001$ rim. Boundary walls and a reversed inner layer close the
mesh. Two existing tapered moustache strips sit above the lip. The shell uses an opaque swatch mixed from 75\% skin and 25\% hair color
to suggest closely cropped hair; the moustache uses the same shade. Skin, moustache, beard and cap are subject to
the same optional head morph, so the ornament follows the base head.

The coarse skin triangles limit boundary fidelity and the uniform material is
not individual stubble; facial hair is visual only. Rendering must be checked
for each selected face, hair color and pose. Reproduce the closedness,
orientation, lip-clearance, schema and native physics checks with:
\begin{verbatim}
PYTHONPATH=.:vendor/ud-tools/src python -m pytest tests/unit/model_appearance/test_short_beard.py
\end{verbatim}
