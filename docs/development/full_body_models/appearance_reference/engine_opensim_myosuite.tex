\section{OpenSim and MyoSuite Viewers Adopt the Shared Appearance (R9b, \#12689)}

\subsection{Defect}
The OpenSim native viewer
(\texttt{native\_viewer\_export.backends.opensim\_worker}) drew every capsule of
the visual skeleton as a cylinder plus two end spheres in a local grey
$(0.75, 0.78, 0.85)$, and the hint ellipsoids in a second local grey
$(0.7, 0.72, 0.8)$, with only the shared head and the exported club meshes in
colour. It had no skin, garments or shoes. The MyoSuite viewer exported its
MJCF with the legacy capsule layer plus the head
(\texttt{visual=True, with\_head=True}), so it was grey too, although the same
exporter can attach the full appearance layer. Side by side with the textured
MuJoCo export, both looked like a different golfer.

\subsection{Contract}
Both workers render the default shared appearance document
\texttt{\_body.default\_appearance()} (\texttt{appearance-v1}), the one source
that the MuJoCo worker now uses as well. Neither adds colours, sizes or
classification rules of its own: they translate R8's
\texttt{ResolvedAppearance} (\#12685).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{OpenSim.} \texttt{visual\_parts(spec, appearance, directory)}
  $\to$ \texttt{list[VisualPart(body, geometry, params, rotation, position,
  rgba)]} is the pure render plan; \texttt{build\_model} only turns each part
  into an OpenSim geometry on a \texttt{PhysicalOffsetFrame}. With a document,
  every resolved \texttt{BodyMesh} becomes one OBJ (\texttt{\_body.body\_mesh\_files})
  registered as \texttt{osim.Mesh} with its library RGBA; there are no
  cylinders, spheres or hint shapes. With \texttt{None} (plain mode) the
  capsule skeleton and hint shapes are drawn in
  \texttt{library.plain\_rgba("capsule")} and \texttt{("shape")}. In both modes
  the shared head OBJs come first, and the club body is left to the club
  meshes the exported \texttt{.osim} already carries, so the club is never
  drawn twice.
\item \textbf{MyoSuite.} \texttt{build\_spec\_xml(spec\_bytes, appearance)} calls
  \texttt{export\_full\_body\_mjcf(spec\_bytes, appearance=appearance)}; MyoSuite
  then renders exactly the MuJoCo appearance layer. Plain mode (\texttt{None})
  keeps the legacy \texttt{visual=True, with\_head=True} export. The MyoSuite
  arena merge is unchanged: it still replaces the exporter's
  \texttt{visual\_floor} plane with the arena floor and headlight.
\end{itemize}
Preconditions raise \texttt{ValueError}: an unknown visual geometry kind, and
everything \texttt{resolve\_appearance} and \texttt{body\_mesh\_files} already
check (unknown materials, a resolution outside the spec body frame).

\subsection{Frames}
R8 states its meshes in the \emph{spec body frame}. The MuJoCo exporter's body
is the joint follower frame, so its emitter maps every vertex through
$\mathrm{inv}(T_{\mathrm{child\to follower}})$. The OpenSim exporter
(\texttt{full\_body\_osim}) instead attaches each joint's
$T_{\mathrm{child\to follower}}$ as a separate \texttt{PhysicalOffsetFrame} on
the child body, so the OpenSim body frame \emph{is} the spec body frame. The
identity is therefore the correct placement:
\[
  x_{\mathrm{OpenSim\ body}} = x_{\mathrm{spec\ body}}, \qquad
  R = I_3,\ p = 0 .
\]
This is the same assumption the old worker made for the capsules, whose
endpoints were spec-frame coordinates. A unit test asserts the identity for
every mesh part and checks the trunk orientation independently: projected on
the spec landmark axes (\texttt{cross\_section.body\_sections}), each trunk skin
mesh is wider mediolaterally than it is deep.

\subsection{Results}
Default document, both anthropometric specs (identical counts):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
Viewer & Before & After (R9b) \\ \hline
OpenSim & 84 grey primitives + 13 head meshes & 57 mesh parts, 0 primitives, 23 bodies \\
MyoSuite & capsule layer + head & MuJoCo layer: 24 bodies, 62 material geoms \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The MyoSuite per-body material summary equals the MuJoCo export's for both
specs. Every OpenSim colour is a library material RGBA or a shared head RGBA.
Trunk skin spans (ML $\times$ AP, metres) in the OpenSim plan:
\texttt{COMRod} $0.316\times0.205$ and \texttt{LowerTorso}
$0.290\times0.196$, matching R8's spec-frame table.
\texttt{evidence/appearance/engines/opensim\_myosuite/r9b\_appearance\_receipt.json}
records these numbers, the bundle hash and the frames.

\subsection{Native Renders}
Address and impact stills, face-on and down-the-line, of the capture-A driver
(the canonical \texttt{--static-seeds --fix-club-markers} fit re-fitted at
\texttt{ceffab6c07} for \#12642), rendered through the real backends: OpenSim
4.6's simbody visualizer under \texttt{xvfb-run} and MyoSuite's
\texttt{MJRenderer} (mujoco 3.6.0). Both show skin, the white polo, charcoal
trousers, black shoes and the shared head. The OpenSim views keep both feet and
the clubhead in frame at address and impact (\#12313). OpenSim's flatter
shading is the simbody visualizer's own lighting, not an appearance
difference. Stills are visual evidence and do not qualify dynamics.

\subsection{Physics Unchanged}
Visual geometry is attached to offset frames that carry no mass, and the
MyoSuite layer is the exporter's massless, non-colliding \texttt{visual} class.
The same-input OpenSim and MyoSuite parity tests
(\texttt{tests/unit/motion\_matching/same\_input/}) pass unchanged.

\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item The OpenSim club stays the exported model's club meshes; the grip hands
  wrapped on the club (OSV-2) are drawn by the MuJoCo-family layer only.
\item No job-level switch selects plain mode yet; the workers always render
  the default document, and plain mode is reached through the functions.
\item Simbody's visualizer has no PBR materials or textures; only the
  material RGBA (and opacity) carries over.
\end{itemize}
