\chapterpage{Canonical Projection and Depth Compositing}
The exporter reuses the saved native binding and canonical forward kinematics.
Its supplied \texttt{CameraProjection} is unchanged. No alternative FK, camera,
estimator or renderer is introduced.
For an authored local vertex $v$ on native body $b$, column-vector notation gives
\[
X_w=R_b(q)v+t_b(q),\qquad X_c=R_cX_w+t_c,
\qquad \widetilde p=KX_c,\quad p=\left(\frac{\widetilde p_x}{\widetilde p_z},
\frac{\widetilde p_y}{\widetilde p_z}\right).
\]
The saved camera uses world-to-camera rotation and translation. Its numerical validity
is checked; historical calibration and metric scale are not established by this check.
Model depth is expressed in authored model metres, not measured scene distance.

Shared capsules and saved ellipsoid/box hints produce fixed local triangle surfaces.
The manifest pins the exact native definition, exported XML, derived visual description,
visual hints and tessellation (16 longitude / 8 latitude samples). Shared body shapes
are placed through public native body poses. Radius defaults and hints are model
assumptions; they are not fitted skin surfaces, measured girth or subject-specific anatomy.

Triangles are clipped at the fixed positive camera near plane $10^{-6}$ model metres.
The shared rasterizer evaluates integer pixel centers inside each triangle's bounded ROI.
For screen-space barycentric weights $\lambda_i$ and camera depths $z_i$, perspective-correct depth is
\[
z(p)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{\lambda_i(p)}{z_i}\right)^{-1}.
\]
The nearest model triangle supplies the pixel; uncovered pixels retain infinite depth.
The sealed surface layer contains BGR pixels, a Boolean coverage mask, positive finite
covered depth and semantic Unicode geometry identities. It is model-only occlusion:
no original-scene depth, human segmentation or environmental occluder is inferred.

For mask $M$ and proxy color layer $S$, compositing over source $I$ is
\[
I'(p)=\begin{cases}\operatorname{round}\bigl((1-\alpha)I(p)+\alpha S(p)\bigr),&p\in M,\\
I(p),&p\notin M,\end{cases}\qquad\alpha=0.35.
\]
Canonical rounding uses NumPy \code{rint}, including ties-to-even.
Canonical skeleton, landmark/residual and shaft annotations are then drawn on this
source-sized derivative. The original encoded source bytes are unchanged.
Opacity zero is a distinct source-identity preflight; no full zero-opacity export is claimed here.
