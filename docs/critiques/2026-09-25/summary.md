## Headline

The three most dangerous weaknesses are architectural, not cosmetic. Two sit
in the neural-motion checkpoint pipeline:

1. The "three-seed evidence" required for reproducibility claims is
   **formulaically derived from a single loss value** — it is not the product
   of three independent training runs.
2. Dataset, split, and weight provenance hashes in the checkpoint matrix are
   **derived from the model-ID string**, not from hashing actual data or
   checkpoint files. The cryptographic chain they appear to form is circular.

A third weakness is in the benchmark runner: the speedup baseline is
**manufactured from the candidate's own latency** when no real baseline is
supplied, guaranteeing favorable verdicts.

These three are load-bearing: reports generated by `build_checkpoint_matrix()`
and `build_reproduction_catalog()` — including model cards intended for board
consumption — inherit all three weaknesses simultaneously.
