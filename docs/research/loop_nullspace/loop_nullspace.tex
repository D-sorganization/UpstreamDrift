\begin{abstract}
A two-handed golf swing closes a kinematic loop through the club.  Motion
capture fixes the generalized load $M(q)a+h(q,v)$ and the net bare-club wrench,
but not how that load divides between the hands, between the feet, or between
joints inside the loop.  This reference pins the mathematics that the children
of epic \#12593 implement: the general allocation equation $Ax=b$ and its four
dimension counts, a three-case analysis of the zero-side solve, the exact
$6\times12$ grasp map with its $12\times6$ kernel, bare-club Newton--Euler, the
invariants and their validity gates, the gross-power identity, constrained
counterfactuals, envelope geometry, reaction-restriction semantics and the
canonical closure convention.  Every formula is reproduced by exact sympy
derivations whose golden values are checked in.  The document makes no
numerical claim about any golfer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Notation and Conventions}
\label{sec:notation}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item $q,v,a\in\R^n$ are generalized position, velocity and acceleration;
$M(q)\in\R^{n\times n}$ is symmetric positive definite; $h(q,v)$ collects
Coriolis, centrifugal and gravity terms.  The generalized load is
$b=M(q)a+h(q,v)$.
\item $S\in\R^{n_a\times n}$ selects the actuated coordinates, so $S^{\top}\tau$
is the generalized force of the actuated torques $\tau\in\R^{n_a}$.  Index sets
$a$ and $u$ denote actuated and unactuated coordinates.
\item $\Phi(q)=0$ is the loop closure with $m$ rows.  $J_c=\partial\Phi/\partial
q\in\R^{m\times n}$ and $\lambda\in\R^m$ is the closure multiplier.  The drift
term is $\dot J_c v$ with $J_c a+\dot J_c v=0$.
\item $J_g\in\R^{n_g\times n}$ is the Jacobian of external (ground) contacts and
$f_g\in\R^{n_g}$ their loads.  A fixed base has $n_g=0$.
\item The unknown is $x=(\tau,\lambda,f_g)$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Canonical Closure Convention (R8).}
$J_c$ and $\dot J_c v$ are stored in the grip frame with rows
$[f_x\ f_y\ f_z\ \tau_x\ \tau_y\ \tau_z]$.  The linear rows are the velocity of
the reference point $P_g$, the closing-hand grip point.  The term $+J_c^{\top}
\lambda$ acts on the human tree, so the closing hand exerts $-\lambda$ on the
club.  Hand wrenches follow ADR-0052: the loading exerted \emph{by the hand on
the club}.  Planar fixtures use rows $[f_x\ f_y]$ in the world frame.

\paragraph{Characteristic Scales.}
Rank decisions use scaled matrices.  Forces are divided by $F_c=100$~N and
moments by $M_c=5$~N\,m.  Numerical rank uses the relative singular-value
tolerance $10^{-10}$ of the largest singular value.  Exact fixtures use exact
rank.

\section{General Allocation Equation}
\label{sec:allocation}

Dynamics with the closure and contact loads, split into actuated and unactuated
rows, give
\begin{equation}
A x = b,\qquad A=\begin{bmatrix}S^{\top} & J_c^{\top} & J_g^{\top}\end{bmatrix},
\qquad x=(\tau,\lambda,f_g),\qquad b=M(q)a+h(q,v).
\label{eq:Ax}
\end{equation}
It is a statement about one instant for which $q,v,a$ are given.  The motion
must also satisfy $J_c v=0$ and $J_c a+\dot J_c v=0$; Section~\ref{sec:gates}
states the gates.

\begin{proposition}[Dimension Counts]\label{prop:counts}
Order the rows of \eqref{eq:Ax} as actuated rows then unactuated rows, so
$S^{\top}=\begin{bsmallmatrix}I\\0\end{bsmallmatrix}$ and
\[
A=\begin{bmatrix} I & J_{c,a}^{\top} & J_{g,a}^{\top}\\
0 & J_{c,u}^{\top} & J_{g,u}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}.
\]
With $r_u=\rank\begin{bmatrix}J_{c,u}^{\top}&J_{g,u}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\rank A=n_a+r_u$ and the equality nullity is
$\dim x-\rank A=m+n_g-r_u$.
\item The multiplier gauge, the directions of $\lambda$ that change no load, is
$\dim\kernel J_c^{\top}=m-\rank J_c$.
\item With $f_g$ fixed, the dimension of the set of attainable torque variations
is $\rank J_c^{\top}-\rank J_{c,u}^{\top}$.
\item With $f_g$ fixed and $n_g=0$, nullity $=$ gauge $+$ attainable torque
variation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) Column operations that subtract multiples of the identity block remove
$J_{c,a}^{\top}$ and $J_{g,a}^{\top}$ from the first block row.  The result is
block diagonal with $I_{n_a}$ and the second block row, so the rank is
$n_a+r_u$; subtract from $\dim x=n_a+m+n_g$.  (ii) is the kernel dimension.
(iii) Variations $\delta\tau=-J_{c,a}^{\top}\delta\lambda$ must keep the
unactuated rows unchanged, so $\delta\lambda\in\kernel J_{c,u}^{\top}$.  The
map $\delta\lambda\mapsto J_{c,a}^{\top}\delta\lambda$ on that subspace has
kernel $\kernel J_c^{\top}\subseteq\kernel J_{c,u}^{\top}$.  Hence the image
dimension is $(m-\rank J_{c,u}^{\top})-(m-\rank J_c^{\top})$.  (iv) With
$n_g=0$, $m-r_u=(m-\rank J_c)+(\rank J_c-\rank J_{c,u})$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Contact Loads Free.}
With $\hat J=\begin{bsmallmatrix}J_c\\J_g\end{bsmallmatrix}$ and
$\hat\lambda=(\lambda,f_g)$, the same argument gives the $f_g$-free versions:
gauge $m+n_g-\rank\hat J$ and attainable torque variation
$\rank\hat J^{\top}-\rank\hat J_u^{\top}$ (so nullity $=$ gauge $+$ attainable).
The goldens report both versions.

\paragraph{Consistency.}
The unactuated rows require $b_u\in\range\begin{bmatrix}J_{c,u}^{\top}&
J_{g,u}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}$ (with $f_g$ in the unknowns).  The number of
independent consistency conditions is $n_u-r_u$.  Unactuated rows can therefore
make $\lambda=0$ infeasible; they can also make the whole problem infeasible,
which is reported and never repaired by relaxing balance.

\paragraph{Feasible-Set Dimension.}
Bounds (torque capacity, friction cones, preload) cut the affine set of
nullity $d$ down to a convex set whose dimension is reported after bounds as a
fourth number.  It is at most the attainable variation plus the gauge, and is
computed by the solver, not here.

\subsection{Lower-Body Loop: Ground Contact and Observations}
\label{sec:lowerbody}

With rigid unilateral feet the balance is $A\,x=b$ with
$A=[S^{\top}\;J_c^{\top}\;J_g^{\top}]$ and
$x=(\tau,\lambda,f_g)$, where $f_g$ stacks a world-axis three-vector for every
contact point and $J_g$ is the stacked point Jacobian.  A point is a contact
when its signed gap to the ground is at most a declared tolerance; the load of
a contact lies in a Coulomb cone $\sqrt{f_{t1}^2+f_{t2}^2}\le\mu f_n$ in the
frame $(t_1,t_2,n)$ of its normal, with $\mu$ a declared coefficient
(hypothesis \texttt{H-FRICTION-CONE}), never a measurement.  The foot contact
cones share the constraint class of the grip cones.

A ground-reaction observation is a linear map of the contact loads,
$E\,f_g=g$, with a declared one-sigma uncertainty $\sigma_i$ for every row
(hypothesis \texttt{H-GRF-OBSERVATION}).  Embedding $E_x=[0\;0\;E]$ and writing
$x=x_p+Nz$,
\begin{equation}
\text{nullity after observation}=\text{nullity}(A)-\rank(E_xN),
\end{equation}
the exact limit of vanishing measurement uncertainty.  The rank counts the
null directions the data can see; a net ground wrench is already fixed by the
unactuated root rows, so it contributes rank $0$.  With finite uncertainty the
observation is the two-sided band
\begin{equation}
\bigl|E_x(x_p+Nz)-g\bigr|_i\le k\,\sigma_i,
\end{equation}
with coverage factor $k$ (default $3$), added to the feasible set as rows of
kind \texttt{grf}; the null dimension is unchanged.  The accounting reports the
singular values of $\operatorname{diag}(1/\sigma)E_xN$: a value below one means
a unit scaled null step moves the prediction by less than the declared
uncertainty, so that direction is not resolved by the data.  The restriction
routine moves $x_p$ to the weighted least-squares member of the family and
drops the visible directions; it refuses (never absorbs) data that disagree
with the balance by more than $k\sigma$.

\section{Zero-Side Three-Case Analysis}
\label{sec:zeroside}

A ``zero-side'' question asks what multiplier would be needed if a side of the
body supplied no torque.  For a side $s$ with coordinate set $I_s$, the
equations are
\begin{equation}
J_{c,s}^{\top}\lambda=\tau_{ID,s},\qquad J_{c,s}=J_c[:,I_s],\quad
\tau_{ID,s}=b_s .
\end{equation}
This has $n_s=|I_s|$ equations in $m$ unknowns.  When the side has more coordinates than the
closure has rows (the seven-coordinate arms of the spatial fixture against six
closure rows, or the three-coordinate lead side of the planar fixture against
two) it is overdetermined.  Let $r=\rank J_{c,s}$.  The
classification is
\begin{description}[leftmargin=*]
\item[CONSISTENT\_UNIQUE] $\tau_{ID,s}\in\range J_{c,s}^{\top}$ and
$r=m$ (nullity $0$).
\item[CONSISTENT\_NONUNIQUE] $\tau_{ID,s}\in\range J_{c,s}^{\top}$ and $r<m$;
the solution set is an affine space of dimension $m-r$.
\item[INCONSISTENT] $\tau_{ID,s}\notin\range J_{c,s}^{\top}$.  The reported
residual is
\begin{equation}
\rho_s=\left\lVert\bigl(I-J_{c,s}^{\top}(J_{c,s}^{\top})^{+}\bigr)
\tau_{ID,s}\right\rVert,
\end{equation}
in scaled units, and the minimum-residual multiplier
$(J_{c,s}^{\top})^{+}\tau_{ID,s}$ is returned with the label
\emph{minimum-residual side effort}.
\end{description}
The label ``zero trail'' is never attached to an inconsistent case, and balance
is never relaxed to obtain it.  The conditioning of the retained spectrum is
reported with the case.

\subsection{Minimum-Metric Torque}

For a declared positive-definite metric $W$ on the actuated torques, the
minimum-$W$-norm torque that produces the measured motion minimises
$\tau^{\top}W\tau$ over the affine set of Section~\ref{sec:allocation}.  The
choice $W=M^{-1}$ restricted to the actuated coordinates is one allowed
\emph{labelled metric}.  It is not Gauss's principle of least constraint, which
concerns the constraint force at fixed torque.  The golden files give the
minimiser for $W=I$ and $W=M^{-1}$ together with the objective and the
residual of the equality.

\section{Grip Frame, Grasp Map and Kernel}
\label{sec:grasp}

\subsection{Grip Frame}
Let $r_L,r_R$ be the grip points of the hands, defined as the projections of the
hand centres onto the shaft axis.  The grip frame has origin
$o=\tfrac12(r_L+r_R)$ and axes
\begin{equation}
\hat u=\frac{\text{shaft axis, toward the clubhead}}{\lVert\cdot\rVert},\quad
\hat v=\frac{n-(n\cdot\hat u)\hat u}{\lVert n-(n\cdot\hat u)\hat u\rVert},\quad
\hat w=\hat u\times\hat v,
\end{equation}
where $n$ is the clubface normal.  The frame is undefined when $n\parallel\hat
u$; the code must report that as unavailable.  In this frame $r_L=o-\delta
\hat u$ and $r_R=o+\delta\hat u$, with $\delta$ half the grip separation, so
with $d=(\delta,0,0)$ the hand offsets are $\rho_L=-d$ and $\rho_R=+d$.

\subsection{The Maps}
Each hand $h$ exerts on the club a force $f_h$ at its grip point and a free
couple $c_h$.  Stack $y=(f_L,c_L,f_R,c_R)\in\R^{12}$ and the net wrench about
$o$ as $w=(F,M)\in\R^6$.  The grasp map is
\begin{equation}
G=\begin{bmatrix}I&0&I&0\\ -\skewm{d}&I&\skewm{d}&I\end{bmatrix}\in\R^{6\times12},
\qquad w=Gy.
\end{equation}
Define the split coordinates $s=\Phi y$ with
\begin{equation}
\Phi=\begin{bmatrix}-\tfrac12 I&0&\tfrac12 I&0\\
0&-\tfrac12 I&0&\tfrac12 I\end{bmatrix},\qquad
s=\Bigl(\tfrac12(f_R-f_L),\ \tfrac12(c_R-c_L)\Bigr),
\end{equation}
the differential force and differential free couple.  The kernel basis and the
equal-split right inverse are
\begin{equation}
K=\begin{bmatrix}-I&0\\-\skewm{d}&-I\\ I&0\\-\skewm{d}&I\end{bmatrix}\in\R^{12\times6},
\qquad
G^{\#}=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac12 I&0\\0&\tfrac12 I\\ \tfrac12 I&0\\0&\tfrac12 I
\end{bmatrix}\in\R^{12\times6}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact Split Map]\label{prop:grasp}
$GK=0$, $\Phi K=I_6$, $GG^{\#}=I_6$, $\Phi G^{\#}=0$, $\rank G=\rank K=6$ and
\begin{equation}
y=G^{\#}w+Ks,\qquad
\begin{bmatrix}G\\ \Phi\end{bmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}G^{\#}&K\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Shifting the reference point to $o+t$ replaces $G$ by $T(t)G$ with the wrench
transport $T(t)=\begin{bsmallmatrix}I&0\\-\skewm{t}&I\end{bsmallmatrix}$, so
$K$ and $s$ are unchanged by the choice of origin.
\end{proposition}
All identities are verified symbolically for a general vector $d$ and are
recorded as Booleans in the golden file.

\paragraph{Named Columns.}
For $d=(\delta,0,0)$ with $\hat u$ the first axis:
$s_1$ is the axial internal force (pull positive, squeeze negative);
$s_2,s_3$ are the two transverse internal forces along $\hat v,\hat w$, each
paired with the compensating free couple $-\skewm{d}s_f$ about the other
transverse axis; $s_4$ is the differential couple about the shaft (twist);
$s_5,s_6$ are the differential bending couples about $\hat v,\hat w$.  The
exact $12\times6$ matrix with row labels
$f_{Lx},\dots,c_{Rz}$ is in the golden file (\texttt{axial\_matrices}).

\subsection{Closing-Hand Map}
With the right hand closing the loop, $\lambda=-y_R$ at its grip point, and
\begin{equation}
s=-Q(d)\lambda+s_0,\qquad Q(d)=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\ \skewm{d}&I\end{bmatrix},
\quad s_0=\Bigl(-\tfrac{F}{2},\ -\tfrac12(M+d\times F)\Bigr),
\end{equation}
and $Q(d)^{-1}=Q(-d)$.  For the left hand closing the loop, $s=-Q(-d)\,y_L+s_0'$ with
$s_0'=(F/2,\tfrac12(M-d\times F))$.  Thus a closure multiplier determines the
split coordinates exactly; the unconstrained freedom is the part of $\lambda$
not fixed by the balance rows.

\subsection{Numeric Worked Example}
Frame: $\hat u=(3/5,0,4/5)$, face normal $n=(1,0,0)$ so $\hat v=(4/5,0,-3/5)$,
$\hat w=(0,1,0)$, $\delta=0.06$~m, $o=(0.1,0.2,1.0)$~m.  Bare club of mass
$0.313$~kg, centre of mass $0.75$~m from $o$ along $\hat u$, inertia about the

\[

M=(0.0030,\ 2.3435,\ 4.2893)\ \text{N\,m}.
\]
Choose $s=(40,-15,25,0.8,-0.5,0.3)$ (N and N\,m).  Then the hand loads in the

\section{Bare-Club Newton--Euler}
\label{sec:club}

The net wrench the hands exert on the club comes from Newton--Euler on the
\emph{bare} club only.  With bare-club mass $m$, centre of mass $c$ (offset
$c-o$ from the grip origin), inertia $I_c$ about the centre of mass,
accelerations in the grip frame and $g$ gravity:
\begin{align}
F&=m\,(a_c-g),\\
M_o&=I_c\alpha+\omega\times(I_c\omega)+(c-o)\times F .
\end{align}

\section{Invariants and Validity Gates}
\label{sec:gates}

\section{The Asymmetric Power-Cost Identity}
\label{sec:cpm}

Let $p_i=\tau_iv_i$ and $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$.  Since $[p]_+=\tfrac12(|p|+p)$ and
$[-p]_+=\tfrac12(|p|-p)$,
\begin{equation}
c_+[p]_+ + c_-[-p]_+=\tfrac12(c_++c_-)\,|p|+\tfrac12(c_+-c_-)\,p .
\end{equation}
Summed over joints with \emph{uniform} $c_\pm$, the second term is
$\tfrac12(c_+-c_-)P_{net}$ with $P_{net}=\sum p_i=(Sv)^{\top}\tau$, which by
Table~\ref{tab:invariants}(b) is independent of $\lambda$ on $J_cv=0$ (with
$f_g$ fixed).  The objective is then $\tfrac12(c_++c_-)\sum|p_i|$ plus a
constant, so the minimiser does not depend on the ratio $c_-/c_+$: a uniform
ratio is a dead parameter.  Only per-joint heterogeneous weights change the
optimum.

\paragraph{Why a Strictly Convex Partner Is Needed.}
$\sum|p_i|$ is convex and piecewise linear, so its minimisers can form an edge
or a face.  An $\ell_1$ term alone therefore gives a non-unique optimum.  A
strictly convex term on the retained space restores uniqueness.

\paragraph{Curvature Condition.}
Let $N$ be a basis of the null space and $N_\tau$ its torque rows.  The
quadratic $z^{\top}N_\tau^{\top}WN_\tau z$ is positive definite on the
\emph{retained} space (the null space modulo the multiplier gauge) iff $N_\tau$
has full column rank there.  The golden files report the Gram eigenvalues for
the identity metric: for the full-actuation fixture $(0.0682,\,0.9141)$ on a
two-dimensional family (strictly convex); for the straight-arm fixture
$(0,\,1.2936)$, where the zero eigenvalue is the gauge direction and the
retained dimension is $1$.

\paragraph{Numeric Check.}
On the planar fixture, with rates chosen so that negative power cannot be
avoided, the uniform-weight minimiser is the unique point $(-2.4428,\,-14.1078)$
for every tested $c_-/c_+$; heterogeneous weights move it to
$(27.55,\,-25.77)$.  $P_{net}=-18.4285$ for all tested $\lambda$ on the
velocity manifold, and $\min\sum|p|=18.676$.

\section{Constrained Counterfactuals}
\label{sec:cf}

Zero-torque counterfactuals (ZTCF) remove the control torque, and zero-velocity
counterfactuals (ZVCF) set $v=0$ \emph{and} $u=0$.  The repository name
\texttt{simulation\_backends/ztcf\_zvcf.py} implements these for an
unconstrained, pointwise system: it uses only $M$ and $h$.  On a closed loop
that is wrong, because it lets the reaction vanish or stay fixed.

\paragraph{KKT Projector.}
With $J_ca+\dot J_cv=0$, eliminate $\lambda$:
\begin{align}
\Lambda_c&=(J_cM^{-1}J_c^{\top})^{+},\\
P_c&=M^{-1}\bigl(I-J_c^{\top}\Lambda_cJ_cM^{-1}\bigr),\\
a&=P_c(S^{\top}\tau-h)-M^{-1}J_c^{\top}\Lambda_c\,\dot J_cv,\\
\lambda&=-\Lambda_c\bigl(J_cM^{-1}(S^{\top}\tau-h)+\dot J_cv\bigr).
\end{align}
The reactions are re-solved for each counterfactual; $\lambda$ is never held
fixed.  The acceleration is unique even when $J_c$ is rank deficient, although
$\lambda$ is not.

\paragraph{What Is $\lambda$-Invariant.}
With the same state, the constrained ZTCF acceleration (set $\tau=0$ above)
depends only on $q,v$; the \emph{total} control-induced acceleration is the
difference $a-a_{ZTCF}=P_cS^{\top}\tau$, and it is also determined by the
measured motion, so it is the same for every admissible allocation.  The
per-actuator attribution $P_cS_i^{\top}\tau_i$ depends on the individual
$\tau_i$, which vary across the family, and is \texttt{FAMILY\_DEPENDENT}.
Measured-state diagnostics, replay-state diagnostics and counterfactual
rollouts are three different things and are labelled separately.

\paragraph{Implementation and Reproduction.}
\texttt{loop\_nullspace/constrained\_dynamics.py} evaluates the projector with a
Cholesky factor of $M$ and a Hermitian pseudo-inverse of $J_cM^{-1}J_c^{\top}$
(relative cut-off $10^{-12}$); the Baumgarte terms are declared, replacing
$\dot J_cv$ by $\dot J_cv+2\alpha\dot e+\beta^2e$ with $e=\Phi(q)$ and
$\dot e=J_cv$.  The legacy pendulum golfer has a massless club coordinate, so
its $M$ is only semi-definite and $J_c$ is rank deficient; it uses the
equivalent augmented block system $[[M,-J_c^{\top}],[J_c,0]]$ (least squares if
singular), and its multiplier has the opposite sign convention.  A rollout
integrates $\dot q=v$, $\dot v=a(q,v,\tau)$ with one RK4 step per sample and the
load held over the step; a null-space torque is a family member only at the
state it was sampled for, so later samples drift by $O(\Delta t)$.  Evidence
against Pinocchio \texttt{constraintDynamics} on the 41-coordinate anthro model
(zero torques for ZTCF, zero velocities and torques for ZVCF) gives scaled
agreement $8.4\times10^{-12}$ and $3.9\times10^{-13}$.  Reproduce with
\texttt{python3 -m pytest tests/unit/simulation\_backends/test\_constrained\_ztcf\_zvcf.py
tests/unit/motion\_matching/test\_counterfactual\_constrained.py -n 0}.
This is software-correctness evidence only; it is not scientific qualification.

\section{Envelope Geometry}
\label{sec:envelope}

\subsection{Static Versus Trajectory-Coupled}
After eliminating the equality, the admissible set per frame is the polyhedron
$\{z: Gz\le h\}$ in null-space coordinates.  \emph{Static} envelopes use each
frame's set alone: for a quantity $\ell^{\top}z$, two linear programs give the
lower and upper bound.  \emph{Trajectory-coupled} sets add constraints linking
frames (torque-rate bounds, smoothness) and are subsets of the static ones;
the static envelope over-approximates whenever coupling is active.

\subsection{Statuses and Sign Classes}
A bound is \texttt{BOUNDED}, \texttt{UNBOUNDED} (a recession ray $r$ with
$Gr\le0$ and $\ell^{\top}r\neq0$, reported with the ray certificate) or
\texttt{INFEASIBLE}.  An unbounded bound is NaN with its status, never a large
number.  The sign class is: ZERO iff $\text{lower}=\text{upper}=0$; POSITIVE iff
lower $>0$; NEGATIVE iff upper $<0$; otherwise INDETERMINATE (a bound that
touches zero is INDETERMINATE).

\subsection{Chebyshev Centre}
The Chebyshev centre is the centre of the largest ball inside the polyhedron in
a \emph{declared} metric $\lVert z\rVert_W$.  The radius is the margin.  It is
generally not unique; the golden fixture is chosen so it is unique, and the
code must report non-uniqueness explicitly.  The centre depends on the metric,
so the metric is a convention, not physiology.

\subsection{Analytic Case}
The golden \texttt{envelope\_analytic\_lp} uses a two-dimensional multiplier
polygon with vertices $(0,\tfrac12)$, $(1,-\tfrac12)$, $(1,\tfrac12)$ and area
$\tfrac12$, capacities $(\tfrac12,\tfrac34,\tfrac12)$ and
$\tau_{ID}=(\tfrac12,-\tfrac14,1)$.  The envelopes are $\lambda_1\in[0,1]$,
$\lambda_2\in[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$, $\tau_1\in[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$,
$\tau_2\in[-\tfrac34,\tfrac14]$, $\tau_3\in[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$, all
INDETERMINATE.  The Euclidean Chebyshev centre is $(\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},
-\tfrac12+\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2})$ with radius $1-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$.  A trajectory
coupling with rate bound $\delta=\tfrac18$ narrows $\lambda_1$ in frame one from
$[0,1]$ to $[0,\tfrac58]$.  A ray $(0,1)$ makes $\lambda_2$ and $\tau_3$
unbounded in the unbounded case.

\subsection{Inclusion-Minimal Conflict Sets}
If the set is empty, a Farkas multiplier $y\ge0$ with $y^{\top}G=0$ and
$y^{\top}h<0$ certifies it.  An inclusion-minimal subset of rows (an
irreducible infeasible subsystem) is returned and mapped to hypothesis ids.  In
the analytic infeasible case the conflict set is
\{\texttt{H-TORQUE-CAPACITY:joint1:lower}, \texttt{H-GRIP-PRELOAD:axial\_nonpositive}\}
with certificate $y=(0,1,1,0,0)$ and $y^{\top}h=-\tfrac14$.

\subsection{Polyhedral Approximation of Cones}
A circular friction cone approximated by a regular $k$-gon is an inner
approximation (polygon inside the circle) or outer one.  With inner scale
$\cos(\pi/k)$ and outer scale $1/\cos(\pi/k)$, the relative error is
$1/\cos(\pi/k)-1$; the code declares inner or outer, $k$ and the error.

\section{Reaction-Restriction Sensitivity}
\label{sec:restriction}

On a \emph{fixed welded trajectory} the closure rows may be restricted:
\texttt{WELD6} allows all six reaction components; \texttt{SHAFT\_FREE5} drops
the twist moment about the shaft axis $\hat u$ (the $\tau_x$ row in the grip
frame); \texttt{BALL3} keeps only the three force rows.  Dropping a row removes
that reaction component, so the admissible reaction set shrinks and the nesting
is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F(\text{WELD6})\supseteq\mathcal F(\text{SHAFT\_FREE5})\supseteq
\mathcal F(\text{BALL3}).
\end{equation}
Envelopes of the restricted kinds are contained in the \texttt{WELD6}
envelope, and a restricted set can be empty.  This is a \emph{restriction of
the reaction set on the welded kinematics}.  It is not a different closure
model: a true alternative grip changes the kinematics themselves and has to be
re-derived (research item NSX-72).  Outputs carry the label
\texttt{REACTION\_RESTRICTION\_SENSITIVITY}.

\subsection{Calculation Reference}
\paragraph{Row Selection and Shaft Axis.}
In the canonical grip frame the closure rows are $(f_x,f_y,f_z,\tau_x,\tau_y,
\tau_z)$.  \texttt{restrict} keeps the rows $R$ of $J_c$ and $\dot J_c v$ and
changes nothing else ($q,v,a,M,h,S,J_g$ and the loop support are unchanged).
Row $\tau_x$ is the twist about the shaft only if the grip $x$ axis is the
shaft.  This holds for the anthro model (bare-club centre of mass on $x$) and
not for the spatial fixture, whose club lies along grip $z$.  \texttt{restrict}
therefore refuses \texttt{SHAFT\_FREE5} unless the shaft axis lies within
$0.1$ of the grip $x$ axis.  Dropping a row fixes that multiplier at zero, so
the restricted allocation set is $\{x:\ A_R x=b,\ \lambda_{\bar R}=0\}$.

\paragraph{Hand-Split Quantities.}
The closure multiplier is at the grip origin $P$ (the closing hand's weld
frame).  With hand coordinates $x_L,x_R$ on the shaft, the closing hand's
wrench about its own grip point is $-(f,\ t-x_c\hat e_x\times f)$ and the net
wrench $(F,M_P)$ moves to the grip midpoint $o$ as $(F,\ M_P-o\times F)$.  The
split coordinates are then the affine closing-hand map of the grip-split
section evaluated on the embedded multiplier.

\paragraph{Acceptance Evidence.}
Exact sympy goldens (\texttt{restriction\_counts}) fix the dimension counts per
kind; the minimum-norm torque on the exact chain equals the residual
$(I-P_r)b$; the $J_c=I$ closed form gives the envelope.  Containment of every
restricted envelope in the \texttt{WELD6} envelope is checked to
$10^{-6}(\text{scale}+|\text{bound}|)$ on the anthro record.

\paragraph{Anthro Result (199 Frames, Declared Prior).}
Under the \emph{assumed} capacity prior, 75 of 199 welded frames are
infeasible with conflict set \texttt{H-TORQUE-CAPACITY} alone.
\texttt{SHAFT\_FREE5} leaves the feasible count unchanged (124) and narrows
envelopes; \texttt{BALL3} loses six further frames (118 feasible, 81
infeasible).  No frame was balance-inconsistent: the foot rows balance the
unactuated root rows for every kind.  Sign-class changes are all
indeterminate-to-determined, as nesting requires.  The prior is not relaxed.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The left grip point is the left-hand solid's centre of mass, a model
parameter and not a measurement.  The result describes the reaction set on
welded kinematics; it is not evidence about the true grip mechanics.  The
position level of admission was skipped (no closure plant).

\texttt{LOOP\_NULLSPACE\_RESTRICTION\_STRIDE=1 LOOP\_NULLSPACE\_RESTRICTION\_JSON=out.json
python3 -m pytest tests/integration/test\_loop\_nullspace\_restriction\_anthro.py}
writes the record kept at
\texttt{docs/research/loop\_nullspace/records/restriction\_anthro\_stride1.json}.

\section{Audit of the Pendulum Constraint Rank}
\label{sec:audit}

\texttt{pendulum\_simulator/golfer\_constraints.py} returns four closure rows
for an eight-coordinate model.  Reviewers reported that the last two rows are a
projection of the first two (rank 2) and that the wrist angles have zero
columns.  Evaluating the analytic Jacobian with the calibrated geometry
(hub 0.15~m, arms 0.30 and 0.27~m, club 1.02~m, grips 0.02 and 0.12~m) at
random states and at 400 solutions of $\Phi=0$ gives:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item the rank is $3$ (singular values e.g.\ $1.23,0.61,0.37,0$), not $2$ and
not $4$; one of the four rows is redundant;
\item the two wrist columns are exactly zero, as reported;
\item every numerically found solution of $\Phi=0$ has club angle
$\theta_{club}=\pm\pi/2$; the Python forward kinematics is internally
inconsistent about the sign of the $y$ axis, which restricts the closed set to
that angle.  The native backend was not built in the audited environment, so
this finding covers only the Python path.
\end{itemize}
Any child that builds on this module must use only independent rows and must
not assume the closure is generically satisfiable.

\section{Variability Sensitivity}
\label{sec:variability}

This section fixes the mathematics that every variability child of epic
\#12593 (section 5A, rules V1--V8) implements.  Rule R2's allocation family
$x=x_p+Nz$ leaves the \emph{nominal} motion unchanged.  What $z$ can change is
the \emph{variance} of the outcome under perturbation, and it can do so only
through the three declared mechanisms of Section~\ref{sec:var-mechanisms}.
Reproduction: \texttt{derivations/variability\_goldens.py} (golden
\texttt{variability\_sensitivity}); it uses exact rational data and imports no
repository production code.

\subsection{Tangent-Bundle Linearization With Reactions Re-Solved}
\label{sec:var-lin}

Take $M(q)a+h(q,v)=S^{\!\top}u+J_c(q)^{\!\top}\lambda$ with closure
$\Phi(q)=0$ and its acceleration form $\Phi_{qq}[v,v]+J_c a=0$.  Fix the
feed-forward $(u,\lambda)=x_p+Nz$ of the nominal swing and perturb the state on
the tangent bundle, $J_c\,\delta q=0$ and $J_c\,\delta v+\Phi_{qq}[v,\delta q]=0$.
Differentiating both equations and eliminating $\delta\lambda$ gives the
\emph{KKT linearization}
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix} M & -J_c^{\!\top}\\ J_c & 0\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\delta a\\ \delta\lambda\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}
S^{\!\top}\delta u-(\partial_q h)\,\delta q-(\partial_v h)\,\delta v
-(\partial_q M\,a)\,\delta q+K_{\mathrm{geom}}(\lambda)\,\delta q\\[2pt]
-\Phi_{qq}[a,\delta q]-\Phi_{qqq}[v,v,\delta q]-2\Phi_{qq}[v,\delta v]
\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:var-kkt}
\end{equation}
where the \emph{geometric preload term} is
\begin{equation}
K_{\mathrm{geom}}(\lambda)=\sum_{r=1}^{m}\lambda_r\,\nabla_q^2\Phi_r(q).
\label{eq:var-kgeom}
\end{equation}
Holding $J_c$ fixed while differentiating $J_c^{\!\top}\lambda$ drops
$K_{\mathrm{geom}}$; that is an error, because $\lambda=\lambda_p+K_\lambda z$
varies over the allocation family.  Reading the tangent acceleration off the
solution yields $\delta\dot x_t=F_t(z)\,\delta x_t+G_t\,\delta u$, with
$\delta x_t=(\delta q_t,\delta v_t)$ in tangent coordinates.  Two facts follow:
$G_t N=0$ (the nominal motion does not move with $z$) and $F_t$ depends on $z$
through $K_{\mathrm{geom}}(\lambda(z))$ \emph{only}.

\paragraph{Worked Case.}  A point mass on the circle
$\Phi=\tfrac12(\lvert q\rvert^2-L^2)$ with two actuator forces and one reaction
has $H=\nabla^2\Phi=I$ and a one-parameter family $N=(-q,\,1)$.  In the angle
$\theta$ and rate $\omega$, with $a=L\alpha\,\hat t-L\omega^2\hat e_r$,
\begin{equation}
F_t=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\ \omega^2+\lambda(z)/m & 0\end{bmatrix},\qquad
G_t=\frac{1}{mL}\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\ \hat t^{\!\top}\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Without $K_{\mathrm{geom}}$ the entry is $\omega^2$ for every $z$.  An
independent reduced-coordinate derivation
$\alpha=(\hat t\cdot u-mg\sin\theta)/(mL)$ at fixed $u$ gives the same $F_t$,
so the golden fails if the term is dropped.  The planar two-arm oracle supplies
$P^{\!\top}K_{\mathrm{geom}}(\lambda)P$, with $P$ spanning $\ker J_c$, for two
reaction allocations; it differs between them (basis-free projector form is
stored).

\paragraph{Implementation (NSX-84).}  Perturbations are expressed in an
orthonormal basis $P$ of $\ker J_c$ (SVD), $\delta q=P\xi$ and
$\delta v=P\eta+O\xi$ with $O\xi=-J_c^{+}(\partial_qJ_c[P\xi])v$ the
minimum-norm velocity offset, so $J_c\,\delta q=0$ and the velocity-level
tangency hold by construction; the basis-free object is $\Pi=PP^{\!\top}$ with
$J_c\Pi J_c^{\!\top}=0$.  The full-state Jacobian of the index-3 DAE is never
formed.  Two routes yield the same derivatives: central differences of the
single KKT solve along retractions ($q+\epsilon\,dq$ in $\mathbb R^n$,
$\mathrm{quat}\otimes\exp$ for free joints, so $n_q\neq n_v$ is handled), and
the assembled system of Eq.~\eqref{eq:var-kkt} with $K_{\mathrm{geom}}$ taken
from $\partial_q(J_c^{\!\top}\lambda)$ at fixed $\lambda$.  The moving-frame
connection turns $F_t$ into the angle coordinates of the worked case; for
general $k$ it is specified in Section~\ref{sec:var-lin-k}.  The
differencing refuses, rather than extrapolates, when the closure rank or the
contact mode changes inside the step.  Engines declare analytic, autodiff,
finite-difference or no derivatives (capability
\texttt{closed\_loop\_derivatives}); an undeclared capability fails closed with
its reason.  Failed experiments: none recorded.  Reproduction:
\texttt{pytest tests/unit/loop\_nullspace/test\_tangent\_linearization.py}.

\paragraph{Pinocchio Analytic Derivatives (NSX-85).}  The Pinocchio adapter
returns the same tangent-bundle object without differencing.  The partials
$(\partial_qa,\partial_va,\partial_\tau a)$ and
$(\partial_q\lambda,\partial_v\lambda,\partial_\tau\lambda)$ are those of
\texttt{computeConstraintDynamicsDerivatives}, total derivatives of
\texttt{constraintDynamics} with $\lambda$ re-solved, hence they contain
$K_{\mathrm{geom}}$; they enter as
$\partial a/\partial\xi=\partial_qa\,P+\partial_va\,O$ and
$\partial a/\partial\eta=\partial_va\,P$.  The offset $O$ needs
$\partial_qJ_c[w]\,v$, obtained from the analytic LOCAL joint-velocity partials
of the two weld frames (hand frame minus club frame, constant offset
$\mathrm{d}v=R^{\!\top}(\mathrm{d}v_j+\mathrm{d}\omega_j\times p)$); the pose-rate
and transport terms multiply $J_cw$ or $J_cv$ and vanish for tangent $w$ and
admissible $v$.  Convention (rule R8): the native rows are the constraint
LOCAL frame, linear then angular, sign $+1$, and the canonical grip frame is
that frame, so $C=I$ and neither $a$ nor $\lambda$ needs conversion; the identity
is checked at every evaluation and a model that broke it is refused because the
$\partial_qC$ term of $\lambda_c=C^{-\top}\lambda_n$ is not implemented.
Provenance is \texttt{derivative\_method=analytic} with no step.  Evidence: on
the anthro fixture at projected states the result equals the NSX-84
finite-difference reference to a scaled $10^{-6}$, with the reference step chosen
per block.  The default step $10^{-5}$ cannot serve as oracle: accelerations are
about $6\times10^{4}$ and the native drift solve carries about $10^{-7}$ relative
noise, so the default-step reference has a round-off floor of about
$10^{-4}$ scaled.  The $\eta$ blocks are exactly quadratic in $v$ (large
step, no truncation); the $\xi$ blocks use a Richardson combination of central
differences at $3.2\times10^{-3}$.  Failed experiments: evaluating the
world-aligned joint-velocity partials instead of the LOCAL ones gave linear
rows that disagree with finite differences of the twist (cause not
investigated) and was discarded.  Reproduction:
\texttt{pytest tests/unit/loop\_nullspace/test\_pinocchio\_derivatives.py -m requires\_pinocchio}.

\subsection{Tangent State Space for Several Degrees of Freedom}
\label{sec:var-lin-k}

Let $P(q)\in\R^{n_v\times k}$ be a smooth orthonormal frame of $\ker J_c$,
$N=I-PP^{\!\top}$, $\omega=P^{\!\top}v$ (so $v=P\omega$) and
$\Gamma_w=P^{\!\top}\partial_qP[w]$.  Because $P^{\!\top}P=I$, $\Gamma_w$ is
skew.  Write $\delta q=P\xi$ and $\delta v=P\eta+O\xi$ with $O\xi\in
\range J_c^{\!\top}$ (minimum norm), so $P^{\!\top}O=0$ and
$\xi=P^{\!\top}\delta q$, $\eta=P^{\!\top}\delta v$.  A perturbed trajectory
obeys $\delta q'=\delta v$ and $\delta v'=\delta a=A_\xi\xi+A_\eta\eta+A_\tau\delta u$,
where $A_\bullet$ are the derivatives of Section~\ref{sec:var-lin}.

\paragraph{Derivation.}  Differentiating $\xi=P^{\!\top}\delta q$ along the
nominal motion ($P'=\partial_qP[v]$) gives
$\xi'=\partial_qP[v]^{\!\top}P\,\xi+P^{\!\top}\delta v=\eta-\Gamma_v\xi$,
since $\partial_qP[v]^{\!\top}P=\Gamma_v^{\!\top}=-\Gamma_v$ and $P^{\!\top}O=0$.
Likewise $\eta'=\partial_qP[v]^{\!\top}(P\eta+O\xi)+P^{\!\top}\delta a$.  The
offset is not stored: a tangent velocity keeps its frame components, so
$v(q+\varepsilon P\xi)=P(q+\varepsilon P\xi)\,\omega$ and its normal part gives
$O\,e_j=N\,\partial_qP[Pe_j]\,\omega$.  Collecting terms,
\begin{equation}
F_t=\begin{bmatrix}-\Gamma_v & I_k\\[2pt]
P^{\!\top}A_\xi+K & P^{\!\top}A_\eta-\Gamma_v\end{bmatrix},\quad
G_t=\begin{bmatrix}0\\ P^{\!\top}A_\tau S^{\!\top}\end{bmatrix},\quad
K_{ij}=\partial_qP_i[v]^{\!\top}\,N\,\partial_qP[Pe_j]\,\omega .
\label{eq:var-lin-k}
\end{equation}
$K$ is the $k\times k$ block ``$\partial_qP^{\!\top}$ contracted with
$Pe_j$'' of the specification, taken in the \emph{normal} projection: with
$B(x,y)=\Phi_{qq}[x,y]$, $W=(J_cJ_c^{\!\top})^{-1}$ and $J_c\,\partial_qP_i[w]=
-B(w,P_i)$ one finds $K_{ij}=u_i^{\!\top}Wu_j$ with $u_i=B(v,P_i)$, a Gram
matrix: symmetric and positive semidefinite for any $k$ (rank at most the
number of closure rows).  $\Gamma_v$, in contrast, is skew; the two never
mix.

\paragraph{Reduction to $k=1$.}  For $k=1$, $\Gamma_v=0$ and $N\partial_qP$
is the whole of $\partial_qP$.  Using $a=P\alpha+a_n$ with
$a_n=-J_c^{\!\top}WB(v,v)$ and $\partial_qP[P]^{\!\top}P=0$, the earlier
scalar connection $\partial_qP[P]^{\!\top}a$ equals
$\omega^2B(P,P)^{\!\top}WB(P,P)=K$; the pendulum golden
$F_{10}=\omega^2+\lambda/m$ is reproduced exactly.  The literal extension
$C_{ij}=(\partial_qP_i[Pe_j])\cdot a$ is \emph{not} the $k>1$ connection: it
adds the tangential term $\sum_l\alpha_l(\Gamma_{Pe_j})_{li}$, replaces the
Gram matrix by $B(P_j,P_i)^{\!\top}WB(v,v)$ (not symmetric in general) and
omits $-\Gamma_v$ on the diagonal blocks.  The five-bar golden shows an
entry error of $8.1$ against entries of size up to $53$ (failed experiment,
kept as a regression test).

\paragraph{Frame Invariance.}  Let $P\to PR$.  For a constant orthogonal $R$
and $T=\mathrm{blkdiag}(R,R)$, $\Gamma_v\to R^{\!\top}\Gamma_vR$,
$K\to R^{\!\top}KR$ and $A_\bullet P\to A_\bullet PR$, so
$F_t\to T^{\!\top}F_tT$ and $G_t\to T^{\!\top}G_t$: a similarity transform,
with identical eigenvalues.  For a position-dependent $R(q)$ the coordinates
rotate in time and
$F_t\to T^{\!\top}F_tT+\mathrm{blkdiag}(\dot R^{\!\top}R,\dot R^{\!\top}R)$,
$\dot R=\partial_qR[v]$ (a gauge law, not a similarity); eigenvalues of the
\emph{frozen} matrix then change by the frame's rotation rate, which is a
property of the chosen frame, not of the dynamics.  Both laws are tested.

\paragraph{Golden and Reproduction.}  A planar five-bar ($n_v=4$, two closure
rows, $k=2$, point masses, all four joints actuated) is derived by an
independent route: reduced Euler--Lagrange equations in the actuated angles
$s=(a_1,a_2)$, closed-form two-circle intersection for the passive angles,
linearisation in $(\delta s,\delta s')$ and the time-dependent map
$\zeta=T(s,s')(\delta s,\delta s')$, $\zeta'=(TF_rT^{-1}+\dot TT^{-1})\zeta$ in
40-digit arithmetic (outer derivatives by \texttt{mpmath}; truncation below
$10^{-25}$).  No KKT system and no repository code are used.  Golden:
\texttt{planar\_five\_bar\_tangent\_state\_space}; script:
\texttt{derivations/five\_bar\_goldens.py}.  Non-claims: this is a local
linearisation at one configuration; a smooth frame field is required, and
the frame $P(q)$ is the Gram--Schmidt frame of
$(\Pi e_1,\Pi e_3)$, $\Pi=I-J_c^{\!\top}(J_cJ_c^{\!\top})^{-1}J_c$, which is
singular where $\operatorname{rank}J_c$ drops.  Reproduction:
\texttt{pytest tests/unit/loop\_nullspace/test\_tangent\_state\_space\_k2.py}.

\subsection{Event Section and Saltation Correction}
\label{sec:var-salt}

Outcomes are evaluated at the physical impact event $g(x)=0$ (V2).  Let
$f^-=f(x^-)$ be the flow just before the event, $f^+$ just after, and
$R$ the reset map ($R=\mathrm{id}$ for a crossing without state jump).  The
perturbation of the state \emph{at the event} is
\begin{equation}
\delta x_{\mathrm{ev}}=\Pi\,\Phi(t^*,0)\,\delta x_0,\qquad
\Pi=I-\frac{f^-\nabla g^{\!\top}}{\nabla g^{\!\top}f^-},
\end{equation}
with $\nabla g^{\!\top}\Pi=0$ and $\Pi f^-=0$; evaluating at a fixed time
instead omits the event-time shift $\delta t^*=-\nabla g^{\!\top}\delta x/
(\nabla g^{\!\top}f^-)$.  The perturbation \emph{after} a reset is propagated by
the saltation matrix
\begin{equation}
S=R_x+\frac{(f^+-R_xf^-)\,\nabla g^{\!\top}}{\nabla g^{\!\top}f^-},\qquad
S f^-=f^+ .
\end{equation}
The golden verifies both on a solvable constant-acceleration flow:
$\partial x(T)/\partial x_0=\Phi^+(T-t^*)\,S\,\Phi^-(t^*)$ holds, and fails if
the timing term is removed.  Fixed-time and peak-speed-proxy outcomes are
diagnostics only.

\subsection{Covariance Propagation and Adjoint}
\label{sec:var-cov}

For discrete steps $\delta x_{k+1}=A_k(z)\delta x_k+B_k w_k$, with
$A_k=I+\Delta t\,F_t$ (explicit Euler, a declared discretization) and
$B_k=\Delta t\,G_t$,
\begin{equation}
P_{k+1}=A_kP_kA_k^{\!\top}+B_k\Sigma_w(z)B_k^{\!\top},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(y)=c^{\!\top}P_{\mathrm{ev}}c .
\end{equation}
The adjoint form gives all input sensitivities in one backward sweep:
$\psi_N=c$, $\psi_k=A_k^{\!\top}\psi_{k+1}$ and
$b_k=B_k^{\!\top}\psi_{k+1}$, so
$\operatorname{Var}(y)=\psi_0^{\!\top}P_0\psi_0+\sum_k b_k^{\!\top}\Sigma_{w,k}(z)b_k$.
Noise amplitudes are per-step variances and must be timestep-consistent (V6):
a continuous white spectral density $S_w$ becomes $\Sigma_w=S_w/\Delta t$.

\paragraph{Implementation (NSX-90).}  The recursion and the adjoint are written
for matrices of general shape, $A_k\in\R^{n_x\times n_x}$ with $n_x=2k$ for
tangent dimension $k$; they do not inspect $k$, and the frame connection for
$k>1$ (issue \#12817) stays in the tangent linearization.  The adjoint is
$S_x[N]=C$, $S_x[t]=S_x[t{+}1]A_t$, $S_{\mathrm{in}}[t]=S_x[t{+}1]B_t$; then
$S_{\mathrm{in}}[t]_{ij}$ is the sensitivity of outcome $i$ to input channel $j$
at step $t$ (``which joint, when'') and $S_x[0]$ is $S_{y,x_0}$.  Persistent
parameter states $\theta$ (per-trial constants, rule R14) are appended with
$A_{\mathrm{aug}}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}A&E\\0&I\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ and
enter through $P_0$; one draw acts at every step, so their variance is
$S_\theta\Sigma_\theta S_\theta^{\!\top}$ with $S_\theta=\sum_t C\,\Phi(N,t{+}1)E_t$.
The variance budget splits $\operatorname{Var}(y)$ by input class and step and
requires the classes to be uncorrelated, so the parts add exactly.  Outcomes at
the physical event use $C_{\mathrm{ev}}=C_y\Pi$ (Section~\ref{sec:var-salt}).
Evidence typing follows NSX-81: kernels are \texttt{INVARIANT} given the model
while $A_t$ does not depend on the allocation (neither M2 nor M3 declared);
variance under additive noise and no M2/M3 is \texttt{INVARIANT} with the noise
hypothesis on the \texttt{conditioning} axis; any declared M1, M2 or M3 makes
the variance \texttt{FAMILY\_DEPENDENT}.  Reproduction:
\texttt{pytest tests/unit/loop\_nullspace/test\_variance\_propagation.py
tests/unit/loop\_nullspace/test\_variance\_records.py}.

\paragraph{Nonlinearity Gate (NSX-90).}  The specification requires a gate but
fixes no statistic and no threshold.  The implementation declares the statistic
$r=\lvert D(2\epsilon)-2D(\epsilon)\rvert/\max(\lvert2D(\epsilon)\rvert,\rho)$ per
outcome component and probe direction, with $D(a)=y(a\,d)-y(0)$ from nonlinear
rollouts at amplitudes $\epsilon$ and $2\epsilon$ and a numerical floor $\rho$.
Probes are the adjoint worst-case direction $d=\Sigma s^{\!\top}/\sqrt{s\Sigma
s^{\!\top}}$ of each outcome and one standard deviation along each input
coordinate.  The threshold is a required argument with no default: no value is
recorded here because none has been specified.  A record whose maximum ratio
exceeds it, or whose rollout is non-finite, is \texttt{NOT\_LINEAR\_VALID}; a
record that never ran the gate is \texttt{NOT\_ASSESSED}.  Failed experiments:
none recorded.

\subsection{Mechanisms by Which the Allocation Changes Variance}
\label{sec:var-mechanisms}

With the nominal motion fixed, $\operatorname{Var}(y)$ depends on $z$ only
through
\begin{description}[leftmargin=*]
\item[M1] signal-dependent noise
$\sigma_i^2=\sigma_{0,i}^2+k_i^2u_i(z)^2$ (hypothesis
\texttt{H-NOISE-SIGNAL-DEPENDENT});
\item[M2] geometric preload stiffness $K_{\mathrm{geom}}(\lambda(z))$ in
$A_k(z)$ (\texttt{H-GEOMETRIC-PRELOAD});
\item[M3] activation-dependent muscle impedance (\texttt{H-MUSCLE-SHORT-RANGE-STIFFNESS};
MyoSuite and OpenSim only).
\end{description}
Declared impedance in torque-driven models is the hypothesis
\texttt{H-DECLARED-JOINT-IMPEDANCE} with its own feedback budget; it is never
inferred from net torque.  If none of M1--M3 is declared the objective is
constant in $z$ and the optimizer must return \texttt{OBJECTIVE\_FLAT\_INVARIANT}.

\paragraph{Additive Noise (Flat Case).}  With $k_i=0$ and no M2, $A$ and
$\Sigma_w$ are independent of $z$, so $\nabla_z\operatorname{Var}=0$
\emph{symbolically}; the golden stores the exact polynomial derivative $0$.
With the same additive noise \emph{and} M2 the gradient is not zero (stored),
so flatness is a statement about the declared mechanisms.

\paragraph{Closed-Form Minimum Variance.}  With frozen $A$ (M1 only) the
variance is a convex quadratic in $z$,
$\operatorname{Var}(z)=\mathrm{const}+\sum_ib_i^2k_i^2(u_{p,i}-q_iz)^2$ for
$N_u=-q$, so
\begin{equation}
z^*=\frac{\sum_ib_i^2k_i^2\,u_{p,i}\,q_i}{\sum_ib_i^2k_i^2\,q_i^2},
\qquad \operatorname{Var}''(z)=2\sum_ib_i^2k_i^2q_i^2>0 .
\end{equation}
The golden gives $z^*$ in exact rationals for the declared small case and
checks it against a grid minimum.  With M2 the derivative is a polynomial in
$z$ of higher degree and has no such closed form; its coefficients are stored.
No claim is made that any golfer uses a minimum-variance allocation.

\subsection{Face-Angle Statistics}
\label{sec:var-face}

Angles are compared through the wrapped error
$e=\operatorname{atan2}(\sin(\phi-\phi_{\mathrm{tgt}}),\cos(\phi-\phi_{\mathrm{tgt}}))\in(-\pi,\pi]$.
Reported: bias (circular mean of $e$), standard deviation, interquartile range,
tolerance exceedance $P(\lvert e\rvert>\epsilon)$ and miss probability.  The
coefficient of variation $\mathrm{SD}/\lvert\mathrm{mean}\rvert$ is banned for
zero-centred outcomes, where it is undefined or arbitrarily large.  Face rows
are \texttt{UNAVAILABLE} when axial club rotation is not observed.

\subsection{Non-Claims for Variability Sensitivity}
\label{sec:var-nonclaims}

Noise amplitudes, delays and jitter are \emph{declared hypotheses}, not
measurements.  Nothing here says that a golfer minimizes variance, that
co-contraction helps, or that any ranking of strategies is coaching advice
(V8).  Linear predictions are conditional on a nonlinearity gate and a paired
Monte Carlo check (V7).
