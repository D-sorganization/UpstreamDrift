\section{Scope and Assumptions}

The calculation is implemented in
\path{src/shared/python/force_plates/readings.py},
\path{interpolation.py} and \path{alignment.py}. It reads a
\texttt{VerticalPlateTrial} (OF-01) placed in the world by a yaw-only
\texttt{PlatePlacement} at an arbitrary model time and returns, per foot,
the normal load $N$ [N] and the centre of pressure (CoP) [m] on the
force-sensing plane, each with its uncertainty components. Only vertical
loads are measured: shear and free moment are not observed and are not
produced here.

Assumptions: the two plates are rigid and level; the plate clock and the
model clock differ by a common-mode offset; channels within one sample may
be skewed by at most a declared $s$; loads and CoPs are linearly
interpolated on the actual, possibly non-uniform, sample times.

\section{Frames, Units and Data Validity}

Stance coordinates $(x, y)$ are the plate's own axes in metres; the world
point is $p = o + R\,(x, y, 0)^\top$ with $R$ a proper rotation about world
$z$, and the CoP is reported on the sensing plane, a depth $h$ below the
plate surface. Times are seconds. A time outside the trial span is refused,
never extrapolated. A CoP is unavailable (\texttt{None}) when either
bracketing sample is unloaded; it is never interpolated across an unloaded
sample.

\section{Equations}

\paragraph{Time.} With alignment offset $\delta$ and profiled shift
$\Delta$, the trial time is $t = t_{\mathrm{model}} - \delta - \Delta$. A
value within $4\,\mathrm{ulp}(\max(|t_{\mathrm{model}}|, |\delta|,
|\Delta|, |t|))$ of a sample is snapped onto that sample, so the rounding of
the subtraction is never read as an extrapolation or as a spurious bias.

\paragraph{Interpolation and Bias Estimate.} In interval
$[t_i, t_{i+1}]$ the reading is $y(t) = y_i + m\,(t - t_i)$ with slope
$m = (y_{i+1} - y_i)/(t_{i+1} - t_i)$. The interpolation bias is estimated
as
\begin{equation}
  b(t) = \tfrac12\,|(t - t_i)(t_{i+1} - t)|\;M_2,\qquad
  M_2 = \max\bigl(2|f[t_{i-1}, t_i, t_{i+1}]|,\;2|f[t_i, t_{i+1}, t_{i+2}]|,\;c\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $f[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]$ is the non-uniform second divided difference
and $c$ an optional declared curvature floor. $b$ is zero at a sample and
at most $h_i^2 M_2/8$. Where neither divided difference exists (two
samples, NaN neighbours) a floor must be declared; a missing curvature is
never read as zero bias.

\paragraph{Skew.} The skew term is $s\,|\dot y|$ with $|\dot y| = |m|$; at
an interior sample the steeper adjacent interval is used.

\paragraph{CoP in World Axes.} The stance-axis bias $b_s$ and rate
magnitude $r_s$ (each a 2-vector) are carried to world axes by
$|R_{xy}|$, the element-wise absolute value of the horizontal block of
$R$: a stance error $e$ becomes $R e$, so $|R_{xy}|\,|e|$ bounds each world
component. The reported CoP bias and rate are the worst world axis,
$\max_j (|R_{xy}|\,b_s)_j$ and $\max_j (|R_{xy}|\,r_s)_j$. Under a $45^\circ$
yaw a CoP moving at $r$ along both stance axes thus has world rate
$\sqrt2\,r$. The reading also keeps the random sigma of each world axis,
$\sigma_{\mathrm{random},j} = \operatorname{hypot}\bigl(\sigma_m,
s\,(|R_{xy}|\,r_s)_j\bigr)$ for $j \in \{x, y\}$
(\texttt{cop\_sigma\_random\_xy\_m}), whose maximum is the worst-axis
random sigma; an unloaded CoP carries $\sigma_m$ on both axes. These are
per-axis bounds, not a covariance.

\paragraph{CoP Covariance.} Each stance CoP channel has its own skew,
independent of the other, so the stance random covariance is diagonal,
$\Sigma_s = \operatorname{diag}\bigl(\sigma_m^2 + s^2 r_{s,x}^2,\;
\sigma_m^2 + s^2 r_{s,y}^2\bigr)$, and the yaw carries it to world axes
exactly:
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma_w = R_{xy}\,\Sigma_s\,R_{xy}^{\mathsf T}
\end{equation}
(\texttt{cop\_cov\_random\_xy\_m2}, reported as
$(\Sigma_{xx}, \Sigma_{yy}, \Sigma_{xy})$). Its principal axes are the
plate's axes and its eigenvalues the stance variances, whatever the yaw; an
unloaded CoP has $\Sigma_w = \sigma_m^2 I$. Because
$\sum_j R_{ij}^2 = 1$ and $\sum_j R_{ij}^2 r_j^2 \le (\sum_j |R_{ij}|\,r_j)^2$,
$\sqrt{\Sigma_{ii}} \le \sigma_{\mathrm{random},i}$: the exact world-axis
sigma never exceeds the per-axis bound above. The overlay draws the 1-sigma
ellipse of $\Sigma_w$, the points at Mahalanobis distance one, with
semi-axes $\sqrt{\lambda_\pm}$,
$\lambda_\pm = \tfrac12(\Sigma_{xx}+\Sigma_{yy}) \pm
\operatorname{hypot}\bigl(\tfrac12(\Sigma_{xx}-\Sigma_{yy}), \Sigma_{xy}\bigr)$,
tilted by $\theta = \tfrac12\operatorname{atan2}(2\Sigma_{xy},
\Sigma_{xx}-\Sigma_{yy})$ from $+x$. It reaches $\pm\sqrt{\Sigma_{ii}}$ along
each world axis; for a Gaussian error it holds $1 - e^{-1/2} \approx 39\,\%$
of the probability in two dimensions, not the one-dimensional $68\,\%$.
Assumption: the two channels' skews are independent. A skew common to both
channels (one sample clock for the whole CoP) would add the rank-one term
$s^2\,(R_{xy}\dot c)(R_{xy}\dot c)^{\mathsf T}$, which needs the signed
rate $\dot c$; the reading keeps only rate magnitudes, so that case is not
modelled here.

\paragraph{Composition.} With declared measurement sigma $\sigma_m$ and
band factor $k$,
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{\mathrm{random}} = \operatorname{hypot}(\sigma_m, s\,|\dot y|),
  \qquad
  \sigma = \sigma_{\mathrm{random}} + b/k,
\end{equation}
so $k\sigma = k\sigma_{\mathrm{random}} + b$ exactly, and only for that
$k$. $\sigma$ is a band equivalent, not a one-sigma. The common-mode clock
offset is not folded in: it is shared by every frame and is profiled
through $\Delta$ by the OF-12 screen.

\section{Acceptance Criteria and Evidence}

Unit tests in \path{tests/unit/force_plates/test_readings.py} and
\path{test_readings_budget.py} check hand-worked oracles: exact sample hits
with zero bias; exact linear interpolation on non-uniform samples; the
quadratic bias estimate equal to the true interpolation error; the literal
composition of measurement, skew and bias; refusal of extrapolation;
snapping of clock rounding at the last and at an interior sample; and the
world-axis CoP rate and bias under $45^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ yaw.
\path{test_readings_cop_axes.py} checks the per-world-axis CoP random sigma,
its axis swap under a $90^\circ$ yaw and that its maximum equals the
worst-axis value. \path{test_readings_cop_covariance.py} checks that the
covariance is $R_{xy}\Sigma_sR_{xy}^{\mathsf T}$ at five yaws, that its
eigenvectors turn with the plate, that it never exceeds the per-axis bound,
the unloaded case and its validation;
\path{tests/unit/biomechanics/test_cop_covariance_ellipse.py} checks the
ellipse geometry against a table shared with the web chart's tests.

\section{Limitations}

The bias is an \emph{estimate, not a bound}. It holds when the signal is
band-limited well below the sample rate, so that $y''$ is bounded by its
neighbouring second divided differences. It is not valid across an
under-sampled impact transient: a 1~ms pulse between samples 4~ms and 10~ms
apart is invisible to every divided difference, and the test
\texttt{test\_limitation\_bias\_estimate\_is\_blind\_to\_an\_undersampled\_impact\_pulse}
pins that failure. Callers must exclude or separately bound such intervals.
The world-axis propagation is conservative, not exact, because rates are
carried as magnitudes.

\section{Model-Versus-Measured Comparison (OF-08, \#13086)}

\path{src/shared/python/force_plates/comparison.py} compares a model
ground-reaction series with a placed plate trial on observed components only.
It is a diagnostic, not an acceptance test: eliminating candidates is the
family-wise screen of OF-12, and qualification is OF-HW (\#13082). At every
model frame that the trial covers (\texttt{covers}, which uses the same
mapping and snap as \texttt{reading\_at}) the plates are read as above, and
each residual is divided by the band-equivalent sigma, so $|z| \le k$ means
exactly ``inside the $k$-band''. Frames outside the trial are counted and
skipped.

\paragraph{Load.} $r = F_{z,\mathrm{model}} - N$, $z = r/\sigma_N$.

\paragraph{CoP on the Sensing Plane.} The plate balances moments about its
sensing plane, a depth $h$ below the foot. For a foot resultant $F$ applied at
$c$ on the surface, the point $q$ on the sensing plane with no tangential
moment satisfies $(c-q)\times F \cdot t_i = 0$, which gives
\begin{equation}
  q_{xy} = c_{xy} - h\,\frac{F_{xy}}{F_z}.
\end{equation}
The model CoP is projected this way before comparison, with the model's own
shear, and the per-axis sigma is
$\operatorname{hypot}(\sigma_c, |F_{\mathrm{axis}}/F_z|\,\sigma_h)$. The model's
ground height must equal the plate surface within 1~mm.

\paragraph{Lead-Fraction Crossing.} $f = N_\ell/(N_\ell + N_t)$, NaN below the
CoP load threshold, with
$\sigma_f = \operatorname{hypot}(N_t\sigma_\ell, N_\ell\sigma_t)/(N_\ell+N_t)^2$.
The first upward crossing of $0.5$ is located by linear interpolation between
frames for model and measurement. The measured crossing has
$\sigma_\tau = \sigma_f/|\mathrm{d}f/\mathrm{d}t|$ ($\sigma_f$ interpolated
between the bracketing frames), combined in quadrature with the alignment's
common-mode $\sigma_\Delta$. A delta with $|\delta| \le k\sigma$ is reported as
unresolved, without a value.

\paragraph{Refusals and Labels.} A validation-purpose comparison refuses an
alignment cross-correlated against the same model. Every result carries the
trial's qualification (``unqualified measurement'' until OF-HW), the
alignment kind and the world frame id.
