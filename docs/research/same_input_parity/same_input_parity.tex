\begin{abstract}
The same full-body model, driven by the same initial state and the same joint
torques, must produce the same motion in every physics engine.  This reference
records the model definition, the constrained-dynamics formulation, the
closure projection that makes engine comparisons convention free, the
acceptance levels, and the measured pointwise (L1) result for MuJoCo, Drake and
Pinocchio.  The full-swing results (L2 open-loop and L3 closed-loop) for a driver
and a 7-iron swing in MuJoCo, Drake, Pinocchio, OpenSim and MyoSuite are
reported in Sections~\ref{sec:bundle} and~\ref{sec:fullswing}.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Document status.}  This is an editable standalone research
source, separate from the engineering design manual.  The sole editable design

\section{Purpose and Scope}
The same inputs (model specification, initial state $\bq_0,\bv_0$, and per-step
torques) must yield the same motion in MuJoCo, Drake and Pinocchio, and later in
OpenSim, MyoSuite and Simscape.  Agreement establishes that the spec-built
models are dynamically identical and that residual differences come from
documented numerical policy.  It says nothing about whether the model matches a
human golfer.  Inverse kinematics, feedback-assisted tracking and independently
replayed open-loop dynamics are kept distinct; all three are reported and labelled below.

\section{Shared Model Definition}
One full-body specification JSON builds every engine adapter.  The model has
$44$ generalized coordinates in spec order.  The root is a floating base
parameterised by \texttt{TranslationInputX/Y/Z} and \texttt{HipInputX/Y/Z},
which are unactuated.  The two hands are welded to the club by a dual-grip
closure: a 6D weld constraint between the body \texttt{RHandStandoff} and the
\texttt{Clubface Vector} frame, with the left grip closing the second loop.
Ground contact uses one shared Hunt--Crossley law with common parameters in all
engines.  Units are SI throughout: metres, radians, seconds, newton-metres.

Baseline probe at the recorded states (issue \#11605): total mass is identical
($79.358$~kg), $\max|\bM_{\mathrm{MuJoCo}}-\bM_{\mathrm{Pinocchio}}|\le
9\times10^{-13}$ for the $44\times44$ mass matrix, and the bias
$\bh=\mathbf{C}(\bq,\bv)\bv+\mathbf{g}(\bq)$ agrees to $\le 8\times10^{-10}$
N\,m.  The models therefore pass L0 and differ only in numerical policy.

\paragraph{Three-Tier Cross-Engine Equivalency Contract (Issue \#11709, PR \#12468).}
\texttt{src/engines/tiers.py} defines \texttt{ENGINE\_EQUIVALENCY\_TIERS} and
\texttt{PRIMARY\_EQUIVALENCY\_TIER} across three mathematical tiers:
\texttt{tier\_1\_rigid\_body} (\texttt{mujoco}, \texttt{drake},
\texttt{pinocchio}, \texttt{simscape}, \texttt{opensim}, \texttt{myosuite},
\texttt{jaxsim}), \texttt{tier\_2\_soft\_contact} (\texttt{mujoco},
\texttt{putting\_green}, \texttt{simscape}), and
\texttt{tier\_3\_musculoskeletal} (\texttt{opensim}, \texttt{myosuite}).
The \texttt{same-input-bundle/v1} operates on the shared
\texttt{tier\_1\_rigid\_body} torque-actuated specification while
\texttt{are\_engines\_tier\_compatible} prevents invalid cross-tier parity
claims against unstripped muscle-actuated \texttt{tier\_3\_musculoskeletal}
models.

\paragraph{Subject-Mass Hand-Scaling Policy (Issue \#12300, PR \#12486).}
\texttt{build\_full\_body\_spec(..., match\_subject\_mass=True)} in
\texttt{src/shared/python/full\_body/native\_spec.py} scales \texttt{LHand} and
\texttt{RHand} segment masses uniformly with all other human segments
(\texttt{scale = subject\_mass\_kg / default\_body\_mass}, updating the
default spec hash from \texttt{2bba2beb} to \texttt{a656a18e}), whereas
Simscape reference-capture builds intentionally pass
\texttt{match\_subject\_mass=False} until the R2025b host recalibrates the
capture bundle.

\section{Constrained Dynamics}
With rate map $\bD(\bq)$ ($\bD\bv$ is the weld rate residual), the
accelerations $\ba$ and constraint forces $\bl$ solve the KKT system
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}\bM & \bD^{\top}\\ \bD & 0\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\ba\\ \bl\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}\bt-\bh\\ -\dot{\bD}\bv\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:kkt}
\end{equation}
Drake and Pinocchio solve Eq.~\eqref{eq:kkt} exactly.  MuJoCo adds a Tikhonov
term of $10^{-6}$ to the constraint block by default
(\texttt{kkt\_regularization}); with large grip forces and small wrist inertias
this alone moves accelerations visibly, so parity runs set it to $0$.

\section{Why Raw States Are Formulation Dependent}
The recorded states violate the grip weld by $1.4$ to $190~\mu$m.  Each engine
expresses the weld residual in a different frame (world or local, body-a or
body-b point) and so linearises a violated weld differently: the row spaces of
$\bD$ differ by about $3\times10^{-5}$ off the manifold, and raw-state
accelerations differ by up to $42$~rad/s$^2$.

\paragraph{Failed First Comparison.}  Comparing engines at the raw recorded
states with MuJoCo's default regularisation gave MuJoCo versus Pinocchio
differences up to $0.62$~rad/s$^2$ at early frames, while Drake versus Pinocchio
agreed to $5\times10^{-8}$.  Switching off the regularisation alone was
insufficient; the closure violation had to be removed from the comparison.

\section{Convention-Free Closure Projection}
Two objects do not depend on the residual convention: the zero set of the pose
residual (the closure manifold) and the row space of $\bD$, since every
convention is $T(\bq)\bJ(\bq)$ for an invertible $T$.  The position correction
is the closest point on the manifold, $\bq^{*}$, defined by
\begin{equation}
\bq^{*}-\bq_0\in\row\bigl(\bD(\bq^{*})\bigr),\qquad r(\bq^{*})=0 .
\end{equation}
It is solved as a fixed point.  With $B_k$ the row-normalised $\bD(\bq_k)^{\top}$
as columns, an inner Newton iteration finds $\alpha$ such that
$r(\bq_0+B_k\alpha)=0$, using a central-difference residual Jacobian with step
$10^{-7}$; the outer update sets $\bq_{k+1}=\bq_0+B_k\alpha$ and recomputes
$\bD$.  The rate map $\bD$ is extracted exactly from the linear rate residual
using unit velocities.  The velocity correction removes the row-space
component at $\bq^{*}$,
\begin{equation}
\bv^{*}=\bv-\bD^{+}\bD\bv .
\end{equation}
The Newton search stops at $\max|r|\le10^{-13}$ (cap 25 iterations, otherwise
\texttt{ArithmeticError}).

\paragraph{Failed First Version.}  Projecting along $\bD(\bq_0)$ only, evaluated
at the violated state, left a residual engine spread of $6\times10^{-8}$ in the
projected state.  The closest-point version evaluates $\bD$ on the manifold,
where every engine agrees, and reduces the spread to $3\times10^{-14}$.

\section{Pointwise Parity Result (Level L1)}
Every 20th frame of the 606-frame dynamics record ($31$ frames) is projected
onto the closure manifold and the constrained accelerations of MuJoCo (exact
KKT), Drake and Pinocchio are compared under the recorded efforts.  The gate is
\begin{equation}
|\Delta a|\le 10^{-6}+10^{-8}\max|a| .
\end{equation}
Results (all $31$ frames pass):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item worst state spread across engines $\le 3.0\times10^{-14}$;
\item worst projected acceleration difference $1.4\times10^{-4}$~rad/s$^2$ at
  frame 420, where $\max|a|\approx1.3\times10^{5}$ (contact spike); relative
  $1.1\times10^{-9}$;
\item $\operatorname{cond}(\bM)\approx6.1\times10^{8}$ at frame 420
  ($4.6\times10^{7}$ at frame 400), so $\epsilon\operatorname{cond}(\bM)\approx
  1.4\times10^{-7}$ bounds round-off, and the relative tolerance sits $14\times$
  inside that bound;
\item worst off-manifold difference $41.8$~rad/s$^2$ (MuJoCo, frame 420), which
  is the disagreement the projection removes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Acceptance Levels}
\begin{description}[nosep]
\item[L0, model identity.] Masses, inertias, frames, $\bM(\bq)$ and bias agree to
  $\le10^{-9}$ relative at reference states.  \emph{Passed.}
\item[L1, pointwise dynamics.] Constrained accelerations at closure-consistent
  states agree to the gate above.  \emph{Passed for MuJoCo, Drake, Pinocchio.}
\item[L2, open-loop same-input trajectory.] Whole-swing joint RMS against the
  MuJoCo reference $\le10^{-6}$~rad, or the divergence-growth exponent and
  agreement horizon recorded; marker RMSE $\le1$~mm.  \emph{Passed in the segmented
  form; for a single full-horizon run the growth rate and agreement horizon are
  recorded (Section~\ref{sec:fullswing}).}
\item[L3, closed-loop same controller.] The identical tracking controller
  reproduces the MuJoCo match metrics to $\le1$~mm per engine.  \emph{Passed for Drake,
  Pinocchio, OpenSim and MyoSuite (Section~\ref{sec:fullswing}).}
\end{description}
The input bundle \texttt{same-input-bundle/v1} is described in
Section~\ref{sec:bundle}.


\section{Same-Input Bundle and Integration Policy}\label{sec:bundle}
Schema \texttt{same-input-bundle/v1} is exported from a pipeline run
directory by the MuJoCo reference and replayed by every other engine.  It holds
the spec hash, closure-consistent $\bq_0,\bv_0$, the per-step joint efforts
$\bt_k$, contact parameters and marker attachments.  The policy is identical in
every engine:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item classical RK4 with efforts held zero-order over each hold (by default one
  hold per step; see the effort-hold paragraph below);
\item $1$~ms step with $8$ substeps (effective $0.125$~ms);
\item exact weld KKT solve (Eq.~\eqref{eq:kkt}, no regularisation);
\item closest-point closure projection after each step, tolerance
  $10^{-13}$;
\item root (floating-base) efforts set to zero.
\end{itemize}
The substeps are necessary.  Contact introduces stiff modes near
$-9.9\times10^{3}$~s$^{-1}$; a single $1$~ms RK4 step has amplification
$|R|\approx274$ for such a mode, whereas $8$ substeps bring $h\lambda$ inside
the RK4 stability region.  Two bundles were generated on 2026-10-06 on host
\texttt{brick}, from the pipeline specs
\texttt{full\_body\_spec\_anthro\_driver.json} and
\texttt{full\_body\_spec\_anthro\_iron7.json}.  The zero-order-hold reference
tracks the capture about $4\times$ more loosely than the canonical pipeline
(joint RMS $0.017$ versus $0.004$~rad), so the bundle is a dynamics-parity
input and not a capture-matching result.

\paragraph{Effort Hold (Issue \#12279).}  The reference export evaluates the
pipeline computed-torque controller and holds each sample.  The hold length
$h_e$ is a manifest parameter: with step $\Delta t=1$~ms, the number of holds
per step is $n_h=\Delta t/h_e$, which must be an integer that divides the $8$
RK4 substeps (so $n_h\in\{1,2,4,8\}$); \texttt{effort\_holds\_for} raises
\texttt{ValueError} otherwise.  Hold $kn_h+i$ (hold $i$ of step $k$) is
sampled at its start state and integrated with $8/n_h$ RK4 substeps of size
$h_e/(8/n_h)$.  The closure projection still runs once per $1$~ms step.  The
manifest records \texttt{policy.effort\_holds\_per\_step} and
\texttt{policy.effort\_hold\_s}; for $n_h>1$ the bundle also stores
\texttt{hold\_efforts} ($\text{steps}\times n_h$ rows), and \texttt{efforts}
keeps each step's first hold.  Replays call \texttt{replay\_efforts()} with
\texttt{step\_policy()} so every engine uses the recorded hold, and bundles
without the keys load as one hold.  The default stays $1$~ms (owner ruling
2026-10-11), which is bit-identical to the earlier integrator.  Every
cross-engine receipt in this document (bundles and receipts of 2026-10-06) was
produced with the default hold; \emph{no cross-engine receipt exists yet for a
finer hold.}  A bundle may also carry a latched ball-impact plan
(\texttt{provenance.ball\_impact}, GCV-20, \#11767) that every engine applies
identically; this reference reports no impact-bearing parity result.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}  The open-loop swing is physically unstable, with
measured perturbation growth $39$--$41$~s$^{-1}$.  A round-off difference of
$10^{-15}$ therefore reaches $10^{-6}$ after $\ln(10^{9})/40\approx0.52$~s for
any pair of engines.  The L2 acceptance for a single full-horizon run is hence
the documented growth rate and agreement horizon (Drake and Pinocchio stay
within $10^{-6}$~rad to $0.51$--$0.60$~s, OpenSim to about $0.5$~s), after which
the runs diverge and eventually become nonfinite or singular; the replay stops
gracefully (\texttt{stop\_on\_failure}).  This is a property of the motion, not
a disagreement between engines.  The segmented lane (restart from the MuJoCo
state every $50$~ms) and the closed-loop lane (identical controller) cover the
whole swing and agree to round-off.

\paragraph{MyoSuite.}  MyoSuite wraps the MuJoCo core, so identical operations
give identical results.  It was run with MyoSuite 2.12.2 on MuJoCo 3.6.0 while
the reference was generated with MuJoCo 3.8, and was still bit-identical on
every lane, including the full-horizon open-loop run (observed).

\paragraph{Failed First Full-Swing Comparison.}  Before the fix below, MuJoCo
versus Drake/Pinocchio wrist accelerations differed by about
$2\times10^{-2}$~rad/s$^2$, the closed-loop iron error was $3.3\times10^{-5}$~rad
and the $50$~ms segments reached $6\times10^{-5}$~rad and $1.3$~mm.  The cause
was MuJoCo's own eigen-decomposition of an MJCF \texttt{fullinertia} attribute,
accurate only to about $1.1\times10^{-6}$ relative on the iron club inertia.
The native MJCF exporter (\texttt{native\_mjcf.py}, \texttt{\_principal\_inertia})
now emits \texttt{diaginertia} and \texttt{quat} from numpy \texttt{eigh}, with a
determinant correction that keeps the frame right-handed.  Pointwise agreement
then became $\le5.5\times10^{-9}$ and the results above followed.

\paragraph{Simscape Same-Input Replay Contract (P-8, Issue \#11613).}
Simscape Multibody operates under MATLAB R2025b (asserted via
\texttt{strcmp(version('-release'), '2025b')}; non-2025b releases such as R2026a
fail closed).  The harness \texttt{scripts/matlab/run\_simscape\_same\_input\_replay.m}
imports the spec-exported URDF (\texttt{golfer.urdf}) into Simscape Multibody
via \texttt{smimport}.  The torque bundle \texttt{same-input-bundle/v1} supplies
joint actuation torques $\bt_k$ held zero-order (ZOH) at \texttt{effort\_hold\_rate\_hz}
(default $1000$~Hz) initialized from closure-consistent $(\bq_0, \bv_0)$.
The harness evaluates L0 (mass/inertia topology and coordinate inventory via
\texttt{simscape\_model\_inventory.m}), L1 (inverse dynamics torques against the
Pinocchio RNEA reference), and L2 ($50$~ms windowed restarts with coordinate
error $\le 10^{-6}$~rad and marker RMSE $\le 1$~mm, alongside open-loop divergence
horizon $\ge 0.5$~s and exponential growth rate $\approx 40$~s$^{-1}$), outputting
the structured receipt \texttt{simscape-same-input-replay/v1}.

\section{Reproduction}
From the repository root:
\begin{verbatim}
MUJOCO_GL=egl PYTHONPATH=.:src python3 -m scripts.same_input_pointwise_parity
python3 -m pytest tests/unit/motion_matching/same_input -m ""
\end{verbatim}
The first command rewrites the pointwise receipt; the second runs the
behavioural tests.  The full-swing results are reproduced with
\begin{verbatim}
PYTHONPATH=.:src python -m scripts.same_input_bundle export \
    --run-dir RUN --out bundle.npz [--effort-hold-ms 0.125]
PYTHONPATH=.:src python -m scripts.same_input_bundle replay \
    --bundle bundle.npz --engine {mujoco,drake,pinocchio,opensim,myosuite} \
    --out r.json [--segment-ms 50]
PYTHONPATH=.:src python -m scripts.same_input_bundle closed-loop \
    --run-dir RUN --bundle bundle.npz --engine E --out r.json
\end{verbatim}
where the pipeline runs used
\texttt{--spec} \url{docs/development/full_body_models/full_body_spec_anthro_driver.json} (and the \texttt{iron7} counterpart).
Simscape R2025b same-input replay (SIP P-8, \#11613) is executed on the MATLAB host with:
\begin{verbatim}
matlab -batch "addpath('scripts/matlab'); receipt = run_simscape_same_input_replay('.', 'out');"
\end{verbatim}
This document compiles with \texttt{tectonic same\_input\_parity.tex}.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Simscape (P-8, \#11613) replay harness is wired and validated for the MATLAB R2025b host with fail-closed rejection of R2026a, consuming \texttt{same-input-bundle/v1} via \texttt{smimport} and ZOH torque actuation; final numerical evidence qualification remains bounded to the designated R2025b host (\texttt{DeskComputer} or \texttt{ControlTower}).
\item The effort-hold measurements (\#12279) are single-host numbers from the
  issue thread and were not re-run; the club-face fixtures are not regenerated
  with the finer hold, and no cross-engine parity receipt exists at a finer
  hold or with a ball-impact plan.
\item Two bundles (one driver, one 7-iron swing) were compared, each from one
  anthropometric spec.
\item Single full-horizon open loop cannot meet $10^{-6}$~rad beyond about
  $0.5$~s for any engine pair because of the swing instability; only the
  growth rate and horizon are asserted.
\item The OpenSim adapter works around two exporter defects: an invalid XML
  element name (\texttt{<HuntCrossleyForce::ContactParametersSet>}, emitted
  escaped) and a marker set referencing a nonexistent \texttt{Hub} body.
\item The zero-order-hold reference tracks the capture more loosely than the
  canonical pipeline; it is not a capture-matching result.
\item Contact spikes are ill conditioned ($\operatorname{cond}(\bM)\approx6\times10^{8}$),
  so the relative tolerance is bounded by round-off, not by physics.
\item The grip closure is projected, so the compared states are not the raw
  recorded states.
\item This is a software consistency result, not scientific qualification;
  that remains with the design-manual governance pathway.
\end{itemize}
