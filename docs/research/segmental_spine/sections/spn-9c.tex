\subsection{Full-Body Driving of the Bruno Model}
\label{sec:spn-9c}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
SPN-9a drives only the pelvis and the intervertebral levels. This work package
(\#12758) maps the rest of the matched swing onto a full-body target model: the
legs and elbows by coordinate, the shoulders by segment orientation, and the club
and the ground as external loads. The target is the Bruno full-body model
(\texttt{bruno2015\_fullbody\_os4\_v2}); a synthetic identity map on the in-repo
golf humanoid exercises the same code path without any licensed file. The pelvis
residual of the inverse dynamics with these loads is a software check, never a
qualification of the swing.

\paragraph{Frames and Units.}
Ground axes: $X$ anterior, $Y$ up, $Z$ right. Rotations are proper matrices
$R_{\mathrm{world,body}}$. Angles are in radians, lengths in metres, forces in
newtons, moments in newton metres, time in seconds. Both the golf humanoid and the
Bruno model use the same ground-pelvis joint encoding (tilt $Z$, list $X$,
rotation $Y$, then the three translations), so the pelvis pose of the golf input
is carried unchanged.

\paragraph{Coordinate Map Policy.}
\texttt{src/config/spine\_fullbody\_coordinate\_maps.json} classifies every
coordinate of both models; \texttt{fullbody\_map.py} validates it and refuses an
unclassified coordinate. Golf coordinates are \texttt{direct}, \texttt{pelvis\_pose},
\texttt{orientation\_match}, \texttt{spine\_drive} or \texttt{excluded} (with a
recorded reason: knee-translation helper, subtalar, MTP, forearm pronation and
wrist). Target coordinates are driven by the pelvis pose, the spine drive, a
direct transfer, an orientation match, held at zero (\texttt{held\_neutral}) or
\texttt{locked\_in\_asset}. For the Bruno file this is 6 pelvis, 29 spine-drive,
12 direct, 6 orientation-matched, 7 held-neutral and 105 locked coordinates; the
lateral-bending and axial-rotation coordinates of the thoracic levels $T_1$--$T_2$
to $T_{11}$--$T_{12}$ are locked in the asset, so the drive cannot move them. The
file stores names, topology and signs only, no licensed value.

Signs are observed, not assumed. For every direct entry the native probe
(\texttt{probe\_axis\_dots}) moves the golf and the target coordinate by a small
step at the all-zero pose and takes the cosine between the two child-segment
motions; the map records the sign and a floor on $\mathrm{sign}\cdot\cos$, and the
asset-gated test fails if the observed value falls below the floor. The hip,
ankle and elbow signs are $+1$ and the knee sign is $-1$; the knee axes are not
collinear (about $17^\circ$ apart), so a knee flexion carries a small cross term
that is not modelled.

\paragraph{Shoulder Orientation Match.}
The golf shoulder is an intrinsic ZXY sequence (flexion, adduction, rotation) and
the Bruno shoulder a different sequence, so the coordinates cannot be copied.
With $R_g(t)$ the world rotation of the golf humerus and
$K=R_g(0)^{\!\top}R_T(0)$ the neutral alignment of the two humerus frames (both
models at the all-zero pose), the target humerus is asked to reach
\begin{equation}
  R_T^{\star}(t)=R_g(t)\,K ,
\end{equation}
and the three target shoulder coordinates solve
$\min_{q}\bigl\|\log\bigl(R_T(q)^{\top}R_T^{\star}\bigr)\bigr\|$ per frame by
bounded least squares inside the coordinate ranges (warm start from the previous
frame plus deterministic restarts). The geodesic residual is reported per frame
and never repaired; an out-of-range request gives the closest in-range solution and
a positive residual. The solve assumes the two zero poses are anatomically
equivalent; any posture difference there becomes a constant orientation offset.

\paragraph{Club and Ground Loads.}
The target has no club. Two options were weighed: add a club body to the licensed
model, or apply the club's reaction as external loads on the hand bodies. The
second leaves the licensed file untouched (its hash stays valid), needs no
grip-closure model and carries the quantity spinal loading needs, the wrench the
club exchanges with the hands, so the loads are applied to the hands rather than to
a club body. For the rigid club of mass $m$, centre of mass $c$ and inertia $I$,
\begin{align}
  F &= m\,(a_c - g), & g&=(0,-9.80665,0)^{\top},\\
  M_c &= I_w\alpha + \omega\times(I_w\omega), & I_w &= R\,I\,R^{\top},
\end{align}
where $F$ and $M_c$ are the force and the moment about the centre of mass that the
hands exert on the club. The derivatives come from cubic splines of $c(t)$ and of
the entries of $R(t)$ with $\omega^{\wedge}=\dot R R^{\top}$ and
$\alpha^{\wedge}=\ddot R R^{\top}-(\omega^{\wedge})^{2}$, after the same optional
zero-phase low-pass as the inverse dynamics. The split between the hands is
statically indeterminate; equal weights $w_i=1/2$ are an assumption. Hand $i$
exerts $w_iF$ at its grip point $p_i$ and the couple
$\tau_i=w_i\bigl(M_c-\sum_j(p_j-c)\times w_jF\bigr)$, which reproduces $M_c$
exactly. The club acts on the hand with the opposite wrench, transported to the
hand body origin $o_i$ as the force $-w_iF$ and the torque
$-\tau_i-(p_i-o_i)\times w_iF$, both expressed in ground. The lever arm $p_i-o_i$
comes from the golf geometry (the shared grip point in the golf hand frame), not
from the target.

The ground reaction reuses the force-free estimator: inverse dynamics with every
force element off but the hand loads gives the six pelvis generalised forces the
ground must supply, and non-negative friction-limited forces on the sole points of
the mapped foot bodies reproduce them (regularised non-negative least squares).
The foot and heel distribution is an assumption, not a measurement. The sole point
offsets are those of the golf humanoid applied in each foot frame.

\paragraph{Study Option and Receipt.}
\texttt{SpineStudyRequest} accepts \texttt{fullbody\_map} and
\texttt{external\_loads\_policy} (\texttt{none}, \texttt{supplied},
\texttt{grf\_only}, \texttt{club\_and\_grf}); the default policy is
\texttt{supplied} with an \texttt{external\_loads} file and \texttt{none}
otherwise. The option cannot be combined with a movement variant. The
\texttt{fullbody} stage follows the drive; its merged coordinates feed the inverse
dynamics, the static optimisation and the joint reactions. The receipt gains an
optional \texttt{fullbody} record (omitted without the option, so the schema is
unchanged) that lists every included load by name, body, kind and frame, lists the
excluded classes with their reasons, states in words when the club or the ground
reaction is excluded or only gravity acts, and carries the map summary and the
orientation residual.

\paragraph{Assumptions and Limitations.}
Forearm pronation and wrist are locked in the Bruno file and excluded on the golf
side, so the hands follow the forearm only. The head and neck are held neutral.
Equal hand weights, the foot distribution, the zero-pose equivalence and the
unmodelled knee cross term are assumptions. The club wrench is a rigid-body
reaction, not a grip model. Shoulder elevation is bounded below by its range, so
swings that ask for a negative elevation show a positive residual. Real-capture
acceptance on the owner workstation (anonymised capture identifier) is still
pending; a synthetic swing proves only that the software path runs. Pelvis
residuals are reported by the run and committed nowhere.

\paragraph{Reproduction.}
\begin{verbatim}
python3 -m pytest tests/opensim/spine/test_fullbody_map.py \
  tests/opensim/spine/test_fullbody_loads.py \
  tests/opensim/spine/test_fullbody_orientation.py \
  tests/opensim/spine/test_study_fullbody.py -q
UD_SPINE_BRUNO_SOURCE=<local MaleFullBody.osim> \
  python3 -m pytest tests/opensim/spine/test_fullbody_licensed.py -q -s
\end{verbatim}
Without the environment variable the licensed tests skip with the reason
\texttt{UNQUALIFIED: bruno2015\_fullbody\_os4\_v2}; a skip is never acceptance.
