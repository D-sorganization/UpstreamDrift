\begin{abstract}
Epic \#13160 asks that the best tour-average driver fit stay inside human
joint ranges without losing its marker fit.  Before any model change can be
judged, the 44 coordinates of the full-body v1 model need a pinned definition:
which axis each one turns about, and what a positive value means anatomically.
This reference records that definition as a table measured from compiled
geometry (not from comments), the basis map to ISB, the baseline violations of
the human ranges by the current best fit, and the exact reproduction commands.
Each later HRG child appends its own section here, including failed
experiments.  Nothing in this document qualifies the model scientifically.
\end{abstract}

\section{Coordinate Conventions and Sign Map}
\label{sec:signmap}

\paragraph{Frames and Method.}
The model frame at $q=0$ is the pelvis frame: $x$ forward, $y$ left, $z$ up
(right-handed).  For each coordinate the test compiles the specification to
MuJoCo, sets that coordinate to $+10^{\circ}$ (or $+10$\,mm for the slides) with
all others zero, and records (i) the rotation axis of the child body, from
$R_1R_0^{\top}$ expressed in the pelvis frame, and (ii) the motion of a probe
point fixed in the child body $0.1$\,m from the joint anchor along the first
of forward, up, left that is not within $60^{\circ}$ of the axis.  The
perturbation is a single hinge, so the result is the axis and the sense of a
positive increment.  Drake and Pinocchio forward kinematics give the same
axes to within $0.5^{\circ}$.  The table is
\path{src/shared/python/motion_matching/coordinate_sign_map.py}.

\subsection{Wrist Labels and Forearm Pronation}
\label{sec:wrist}

The panel disagreed on \texttt{LWInputX} versus \texttt{LWInputY}.  The probe
answers it, in the rolled (\texttt{WRIST\_ROLL}) family:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \texttt{*WInputX} turns about $-y$, exactly parallel to the elbow axis
    ($|\hat a_{\mathrm{wrist}}\cdot\hat a_{\mathrm{elbow}}|=1$).  A positive
    value lifts the forward hand, the way elbow flexion does (elbow $+$ lowers
    it).  It is therefore cock: radial deviation, with ulnar negative.  This
    matches \texttt{HUMAN\_RANGES\_DEG} ($-40..25$).
  \item \texttt{*WInputY} turns about $+z$, perpendicular to the elbow axis and
    to the forearm.  It is flexion/extension.  The axis and the probe motion
    ($+y$, left) are the same on both sides, which is opposite on the two
    hands: extension on the left (dorsum faces $+y$) and flexion on the right
    (palm faces $+y$).  The symmetric range $\pm70$ hides this.
  \item \texttt{*FInput} turns about $-x$, the proximal direction of both
    forearms.  A positive value is supination on the left and pronation on the
    right.
\end{itemize}
These labels assume that the forearm is neutral (thumb side toward the elbow
flexion side) at $q=0$.  That is the convention \texttt{WRIST\_ROLL} and the
range table are written for; the geometry confirms the axis relations but not
the convention, because the specification carries no palm landmark.  If the
zero forearm were supinated, \texttt{*WInputX} would be flexion and
\texttt{*WInputY} deviation.

\subsection{Trunk Order}
Order, proximal to distal: \texttt{TorsoInput} (about pelvis $z$), then
\texttt{SpineInputX}, \texttt{SpineInputY}.  The test twists by $30^{\circ}$ and
finds the tilt axes yawed to $(\cos30^{\circ},\sin30^{\circ},0)$ and
$(-\sin30^{\circ},\cos30^{\circ},0)$, while the twist axis stays at the pelvis
$z$ axis for any tilt.  A positive twist turns the upper torso to the left
(counterclockwise seen from above); a right-handed backswing is negative.

\subsection{Spec Family: Was the Wrist Roll Applied?}
Two families exist.  Native specs (\path{full_body_spec_v1.json}, \path{full_body_spec_v2.json},
primitives Rx, Ry) keep the native wrist base, whose second primitive spins
about the forearm and is redundant with pronation.  Anthropometric specs
(primitives Rx, Rz) use $\mathrm{WRIST\_ROLL}\cdot R_{\mathrm{native}}$ with
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{WRIST\_ROLL}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\-1&0&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
\texttt{wrist\_roll\_applied(spec, native)} returns true, false, or raises
\texttt{ValueError} for any other basis.  The compiled geometry agrees: the
dot product of the second wrist axis with the forearm axis is 1 for native
specs and 0 for rolled ones.  Comparing the cock axis with the elbow axis does
not separate the families, because the native elbow is itself tilted.

\section{ISB Basis Map}
\label{sec:isb}

ISB (Wu et al.) uses $X$ anterior, $Y$ up, $Z$ right.  With the model basis
above, a vector $v=(x,y,z)$ becomes
\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{ISB}}=\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&0&1\\0&-1&0\end{pmatrix}}_{M}v,
\qquad M^{\top}M=I,\ \det M=+1,
\end{equation}
and a rotation $R$ becomes $MRM^{\top}$, which preserves angle and
handedness.  A left twist (positive about model $z$) is positive about ISB $Y$.
Implemented and tested as \texttt{model\_to\_isb}, \texttt{isb\_to\_model},
\texttt{model\_rotation\_to\_isb} in \texttt{coordinate\_sign\_map.py}.

\section{Grip}

\subsection{Subject Handedness and Lead-Trail Sides (HRG-GR0, \#13201)}

\paragraph{Definition.}
\texttt{subject.handedness} is \texttt{"right"} or \texttt{"left"}: the hand that
is \emph{lead} (nearer the target) is the anatomical left hand of a right-handed
golfer and the right hand of a left-handed golfer. \texttt{core.handedness} is the
single resolver: \texttt{lead\_side} yields \texttt{"L"} for right and \texttt{"R"}
for left, \texttt{trail\_side} the opposite, and any other value raises \texttt{ValueError}.
A specification without the field resolves to \texttt{"right"} through
\texttt{subject\_handedness}; this is a declared default, not an inference from data.
The builder writes the key only for a non-default value, so every specification and
receipt committed before this change remains byte-identical. The receipt schema records
an optional \texttt{handedness} field that is \texttt{null} on legacy receipts.

\paragraph{Sign Conventions and Sagittal Reflection.}
\texttt{GripPose.landmarks} uses the golfer body frame with $x$ anterior, $y$ toward
the golfer's anatomical left, and $z$ up. The sagittal plane is $x$--$z$, so the lateral
axis is $y$ and the sagittal reflection is $P = \mathrm{diag}(1, -1, 1)$. The target
lies toward $+y$ for a right-hander and $-y$ for a left-hander; the toe axis is $+x$;
the grip axis is idealized as vertical, down $-z$ from the butt. Every distance and
angle of the grip is expressed in lead/trail terms and is identical for both handednesses;
only the target direction, hence the azimuth and palm-normal vectors, changes sign in $y$.
Consequently, $P\,\ell_{\mathrm{right}} = \ell_{\mathrm{left}}$ for each landmark $\ell$,
asserted within $10^{-9}$~m.

\subsection{Cross-Model Grip Capability Inventory (HRG-GS-M0, \#13322)}
To support the cross-engine grip contact and hand-coupling objectives of epic
\#13297, a machine-readable capability manifest is established at
\texttt{src/config/grip\_capability\_inventory.json} under schema
\texttt{grip-capability-inventory/v1}. The inventory classifies every model asset
across four capabilities:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{visual\_style}: Rendering of hand-hand grip overlap, interlock, or ten-finger configurations.
\item \texttt{allocated\_hand\_hand}: Static or kinematic allocation of dual-hand wrenches onto bilateral hand-hand pairs.
\item \texttt{physical\_pad\_grip}: Dynamic contact between designated anatomical hand pads and the club grip cylinder.
\item \texttt{physical\_hand\_hand}: Dynamic inter-hand pad-to-pad contact dynamics.
\end{enumerate}

Every entry records its verified mechanical attachment mode
(such as \texttt{rigid\_branch\_kinematic}, \texttt{closure\_weld},
\texttt{bushing\_compliance}, \texttt{free\_plus\_bushings},
\texttt{site\_weld}, \texttt{mocap\_weld}, or \texttt{direct\_mid\_hands})
and finger kinematic fidelity (\texttt{none}, \texttt{rigid\_proxy}, or \texttt{articulated}).
By contract, no capability may claim \texttt{available = true} without an active
\texttt{evidence\_test} verifying the code path; currently, only the full-body-v1
spec export path (exercised in \texttt{test\_engine\_grip\_contact.py} and
\texttt{test\_opensim\_contact\_grip.py}) provides physical pad-grip contact
evidence. All other capabilities remain \texttt{available = false} with explicit
diagnostic reasons and tracking issues mapped to epic children (\#13326--\#13336).

Non-hand-bearing models (\texttt{putting\_green}, \texttt{putting\_green\_gui},
\texttt{pendulum\_simulator}, and \texttt{swing\_objective\_lab}) are explicitly
segregated in an \texttt{excluded} list with formal justifications.

Empirical verification against source assets revealed several divergences from
initial panel assumptions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{MyoSuite sim repository}: Panel notes assumed \texttt{shared/models/myosuite/myo\_sim}
held an active 23-DOF hand model; verification confirmed the path is an unpopulated checkout directory,
\texttt{myoarm\_simple.xml} lacks hands, and \texttt{golfer\_myobody\_driver.xml} utilizes rigid
ellipsoid hands with site-welds.
\item \textbf{MuJoCo golfer kinematics}: \texttt{golfer.xml} attaches the club shaft as a direct
kinematic child of \texttt{hand\_left}, leaving \texttt{hand\_right} entirely uncoupled without equality welds.
\item \textbf{Drake URDF topology}: The club shaft is rigidly fixed to \texttt{right\_hand} with no finger
links, leaving \texttt{left\_hand} disconnected.
\item \textbf{Pinocchio URDF topology}: The club shaft attaches to a \texttt{mid\_hands} frame connected
directly to \texttt{thorax3}, bypassing both left and right hand branches.
\end{itemize}

A Design-by-Contract loader at
\texttt{src/shared/python/grip\_contact/capability\_inventory.py}
strictly validates the manifest against disk paths, schema versioning, non-empty diagnostics,
and tracking issue conventions.

\subsection{Grip-Style Contact Topology (HRG-GS-G1, \#13299)}
\textbf{Contract.} \texttt{GripStyle} (overlap, interlock, ten-finger) and
\texttt{trigger\_finger} are now physical members of \texttt{GripSpec}; a
non-default value is written by \texttt{apply\_grip\_config} as a new spec
revision and changes the physics hash.  The default (overlap, no trigger) is
omitted from the physical JSON, so existing v1 spec bytes are unchanged.

\textbf{Frame.} Sites are expressed in the shared grip frame of
\texttt{grip\_contact.pad\_layout}: $+x$ along the grip from butt to head,
$\rho=\sqrt{y^2+z^2}$ radial.  Each site is a sphere or capsule
(centre, unit axis, half-length, radius).

\textbf{Pair table.} Every style has eight pad--grip pairs (palm and finger,
proximal and distal, per hand), the lead heel pad on the butt region and the
trail palm on the lead thumb (sphere--capsule).  Overlap adds the trail little
finger on the lead index (capsule--capsule); interlock adds the trail little
finger against the lead middle finger and the lead index wrapping the trail
little finger (two capsule--capsule pairs); ten-finger puts the trail little
finger on the grip beside the lead index (capsule--cylinder).  The optional
trail index trigger adds a capsule--cylinder pair.

\textbf{Anatomy.} Radii scale linearly with hand length $L$
($L_0=0.19$\,m, valid $0.14\le L\le0.24$\,m): little finger
$0.0075\,L/L_0$, index $0.008\,L/L_0$, thumb $0.0095\,L/L_0$, heel pad
$0.012\,L/L_0$\,m.  These are anthropometric assumptions, not measurements.

\textbf{Physics honesty.} In \texttt{full-body-v1} the lead hand is the club

\subsection{Allocated Hand--Hand Contact Reporting (HRG-GS-G3, \#13301)}
\textbf{Method.} We generalize the palm--thumb allocation (\texttt{palm\_thumb.py})
across every hand--hand pair in the contact topology $\mathcal{T}$ (HRG-GS-G1,
\#13299). Given the world-frame closure wrench $F_{\mathrm{closure}} =
(f_{\mathrm{lead}}, \tau_{\mathrm{lead}})$ from the trail-hand-to-club
constraint and hand--club reactions $(f_{\mathrm{hc}}, \tau_{\mathrm{hc}})$,
the residual wrench $F_{\mathrm{rem}} = (f_{\mathrm{lead}} -
f_{\mathrm{hc}}, \tau_{\mathrm{lead}} - \tau_{\mathrm{hc}})$ is
partitioned among active hand--hand pairs via weights $w_i \ge 0$ ($\sum_i w_i =
1$). The split across pairs is an explicit, labelled modelling prior
(\texttt{AllocationPrior}), not an identified physical quantity. Hand--club reactions
$(f_{\mathrm{hc}}, \tau_{\mathrm{hc}})$ default to zero when not supplied, which attributes
the entirety of the observable closure wrench to hand--hand pairs, exactly as HRG-H1 did.
\texttt{AllocatedHandContacts} records the active \texttt{prior\_name} and
\texttt{hand\_club\_source} (\texttt{"supplied"} vs \texttt{"not\_supplied"}) so this
modelling assumption is visible to all consumers. For each pair $i$, the patch point $p_i$
and unit normal $\hat{n}_i$ (pointing lead to trail) are evaluated via primitive distance
solvers. The force $f_i = w_i f_{\mathrm{rem}}$ is transported to $p_i$, resolving
couple $c_i = w_i \tau_{\mathrm{rem}} - (p_i - p_{\mathrm{ref}})
\times f_i$. Compressive normal force is $f_{n,i} = \max(0, -f_i \cdot
\hat{n}_i)$, tangential force $f_{t,i} = \|f_i - (f_i \cdot
\hat{n}_i)\hat{n}_i\|$, and friction ratio $\mu_{\mathrm{eff}} =
f_{t,i}/(\mu f_{n,i})$. Unloaded flags mark tensile configurations ($-f_i
\cdot \hat{n}_i \le 0$).

\textbf{Parameters.} Static friction coefficient $\mu = 0.9$; equal-split pair
weights prior (\texttt{name="equal\_split"}, $w_i = 1/N_{\mathrm{pairs}}$); normal tolerance $10^{-9}$; minimum
closure force $10^{-9}$\,N. Hand--club reactions default to zero (\texttt{hand\_club\_source="not\_supplied"}).

\textbf{Acceptance Criteria.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
\item The sum of allocated hand--hand forces and hand--club forces reproduces closure
$\lambda$ to $\le 10^{-9}$\,N.
\item Every allocated channel emits \texttt{method="allocation"}, user-visible
label \path{external:<pair>_allocated}, \path{prior_name}, and
\path{hand_club_source}; requesting physical mode on welded
topology raises \path{SchemaFeatureUnavailableError} via \path{require_allocated_mode}.
\item Native viewer overlay renders one arrow per allocated pair under the allocated
contact toggle.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Limitations.} Allocation partitions a single resultant closure wrench
using an assumed modelling prior rather than solving multi-body compliant contact; internal squeeze co-contraction
between opposing pairs is not observable from the net closure wrench. Hand--club forces default to zero when not supplied.

\subsection{Pinocchio Topology Contact Reference (HRG-GS-G6, \#13304)}
\textbf{Topology Contact Model.} The engine-agnostic contact model (\path{src/shared/python/grip_contact/topology_contact.py})
evaluates compliant physical contact between anatomical hand sites $\mathcal{S}$ defined in \texttt{ContactTopology} $\mathcal{T}$
(HRG-GS-G1, \#13299) and the club grip cylinder via the G2 pairwise kernels (\path{pair_contact.py}, \#13300).
Sites ride on the prescribed hand frame, while the club grip cylinder rides on the dynamic club frame.
Physical hand--grip contact pairs are evaluated using \texttt{sphere\_capsule} for spherical sites (palm and finger pads, heel pad)
and \texttt{capsule\_capsule} for cylindrical finger segments (such as the optional trail index trigger finger).

For each physical pair $i$, the signed penetration depth $\delta_i = \max(0, r_{A,i} + r_{B,i} - d(p_A, p_B))$ and relative contact point
velocity $v_{c,i}$ yield compressive normal force $f_{n,i}$ and regularized friction force $f_{t,i}$:
\begin{align}
f_{n,i} &= k \delta_i \left(1 + d \dot{\delta}_i\right) \hat{n}_i, \\
f_{t,i} &= -\mu f_{n,i} \, \mathrm{step}\left(\frac{\|v_{t,i}\|}{v_{\mathrm{trans}}}\right) \frac{v_{t,i}}{\|v_{t,i}\|},
\end{align}
where $\hat{n}_i$ is the outward unit normal on the club grip cylinder at the closest point, $v_{t,i} = v_{c,i} - (v_{c,i} \cdot \hat{n}_i)\hat{n}_i$,
and $\mathrm{step}(x) = \min(1, x)$ is the Stribeck transition function.
The total hand wrench on the club in world axes about the club grip-frame origin matches the \texttt{PadWrench} convention:
\begin{equation}
F_{\mathrm{club}} = \sum_{i} f_{b,i}, \quad
M_{\mathrm{club}} = \sum_{i} \left[ m_{b,i} + (p_{c,i} - p_{\mathrm{club\_orig}}) \times f_{b,i} \right].
\end{equation}

\textbf{Coordinate Frames.} Sites in \texttt{ContactTopology} are defined in the shared butt-origin grip frame ($+x$ axial from butt to head,
$y=z=0$ along the shaft axis). In \texttt{GripInterface}, each hand's local grip frame origin lies along the shaft axis at the hand centre
$x_{\mathrm{orig}} = \frac{1}{2}(x_{\mathrm{palm\_proximal}} + x_{\mathrm{palm\_distal}})$, with transverse offset $[0, 0, -r_{\mathrm{grip}}]$
for the lead hand (Left) and $[0, 0, +r_{\mathrm{grip}}]$ for the trail hand (Right).
The helper \texttt{map\_site\_to\_hand\_frame} transforms site primitives into the hand frame:
\begin{equation}
p_{\mathrm{hand}} = p_{\mathrm{axis\_offset}} + \begin{bmatrix} x_{\mathrm{site}} - x_{\mathrm{orig}} \\ y_{\mathrm{site}} \\ z_{\mathrm{site}} \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
At the weld pose, every physical pad--grip site's surface gap is exactly $-0.250$\,mm, satisfying the $\pm 0.5$\,mm neutral preload contract.

\textbf{Pinocchio Adapter.} \texttt{ClubInHands} in \path{src/engines/physics_engines/pinocchio/python/grip_contact_sim.py} accepts an optional
\texttt{topology\_model: TopologyContactModel}. When supplied, \texttt{wrenches()} evaluates \texttt{topology\_side\_wrench} and caches per-pair
wrenches in \texttt{last\_pads}. \texttt{simulate\_grip\_contact} integrates the club equations of motion using SciPy Radau implicit integration
and logs per-pair normal and tangential force traces in \texttt{ContactRun.pair\_normal\_force\_n} and \texttt{ContactRun.pair\_tangential\_force\_n}.
Unavailable channels (such as allocated hand--hand pairs) are recorded as \texttt{NaN} with explanatory reasons in \texttt{channel\_reasons}.

\textbf{Parameters.} Stiffness $k = 1.6 \times 10^5$\,N/m, dissipation $d = 0.5$\,s/m, static and dynamic friction coefficient $\mu = 0.90$,
velocity transition $v_{\mathrm{trans}} = 0.01$\,m/s, patch radius $r_{\mathrm{patch}} \approx 4.47$\,mm matched to $1100$\,N squeeze per hand.

\textbf{Acceptance Criteria.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
\item Zero force at separation; unilateral normal force ($f_n \ge 0$).
\item Friction cone constraint: $\|f_t\| \le \mu f_n$ (with regularized Stribeck allowance).
\item Action--reaction balance: $f_a + f_b = 0$ and net moment about arbitrary points $\le 10^{-12}$\,N$\cdot$m.
\item Resultant wrench matches sum of individual pair wrenches to $\le 10^{-12}$.
\item Per-pair force traces match \texttt{topology\_side\_wrench} re-evaluated at recorded states to $\le 10^{-9}$\,N across overlap, interlock, and ten-finger styles.
\item Timestep halving ($2 \times 10^{-4}$\,s vs $1 \times 10^{-4}$\,s) changes peak pair normal force by $< 2\%$ ($0.0000\%$ observed across all three styles).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Limitations.} Hand segments are treated as rigid bodies driven by prescribed kinematics without articulated finger degrees of freedom;
hand--hand pairs are allocated (reporting only, HRG-GS-G3) and do not exert physical forces on the rigid weld kinematics.

\subsection{MuJoCo Topology Contact Reference (HRG-GS-G8, \#13306)}
\textbf{Topology Geometry and Pair Generation.} The MuJoCo grip-contact adapter (\path{src/engines/physics_engines/mujoco/python/grip_contact_sim.py})
accepts an optional \texttt{topology\_model: TopologyContactModel} in \texttt{contact\_mjcf}, \texttt{ClubInHands}, \texttt{build\_run}, \texttt{hold\_run}, and \texttt{simulate\_grip\_contact}.
When \texttt{topology\_model} is supplied, the legacy symmetric 6+6 sphere \texttt{PadLayout} is replaced by one MuJoCo collision primitive per physical hand--grip pair in \texttt{topology\_model.physical\_grip\_pairs(side)}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
\item Spherical pad sites (\texttt{primitive="sphere"}) emit \texttt{<geom name="\{site\}" type="sphere" size="\{r\}" pos="\{p\}" contype="0" conaffinity="0" mass="0"/>}.
\item Cylindrical phalanx sites (\texttt{primitive="capsule"}) emit \texttt{<geom name="\{site\}" type="capsule" size="\{r\}" fromto="\{a\} \{b\}" contype="0" conaffinity="0" mass="0"/>}.
\end{itemize}
Each site primitive is placed on \texttt{hand\_L} (\texttt{lead}) or \texttt{hand\_R} (\texttt{trail}) by reusing \texttt{map\_site\_to\_hand\_frame(topology, pair.site\_a, side)} without introducing new coordinate frame transformations.
All contact geoms carry \texttt{contype="0" conaffinity="0"}, so broadphase parent--child and hand--hand collisions are disabled; in addition, \texttt{<exclude body1="hand\_L" body2="hand\_R"/>} is emitted in \texttt{<contact>} and one explicit \texttt{<pair geom1="\{site\}" geom2="grip" condim="6" .../>} is generated per physical hand--grip site.
Hand--hand pairs (\texttt{hand\_hand\_pairs()}) are never emitted as physical MuJoCo contact pairs; instead, \texttt{PairTraceRecorder} records their normal and tangential force channels as \texttt{NaN} with explanatory reasons in \texttt{ContactRun.channel\_reasons}.

\textbf{Adapter Diagnosis and Root-Cause Fixes.} Law-level and hold-equilibrium diagnostics identified four adapter root causes that previously produced $166.7\% / 317.9\%$ peak per-hand hold errors and required an artificial club-clamping workaround:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
\item \textit{Uncompensated hand gravity and missing prescribed hand acceleration in \texttt{place\_hands}}: Because \texttt{hand\_L} and \texttt{hand\_R} are \texttt{<freejoint/>} bodies ($m_{\mathrm{hand}} = 100$\,kg, $I_{\mathrm{hand}} = 10\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} I_3$) with prescribed state, leaving \texttt{qfrc\_applied} $= 0$ caused MuJoCo's Delassus solver to use unconstrained hand acceleration $\ddot{q}_{\mathrm{hand},0} = [0, 0, -g, 0, 0, 0]$ inside \texttt{mj\_forward} and \texttt{mj\_step}. On the $0.1$\,s hold, this drove the club $-259.9\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ along $-y_{\mathrm{grip}}$ (exceeding the $250\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ preload at $t = 12.1$\,ms so all four finger pads lost contact and slammed back at $114.1$\,N). On the swing, it omitted the prescribed kinematic acceleration $\ddot{q}_{\mathrm{hand}}(t) = (\dot{q}_{\mathrm{hand}}(t+\Delta t) - \dot{q}_{\mathrm{hand}}(t))/\Delta t$. Setting \texttt{qfrc\_applied} on \texttt{hand\_L} and \texttt{hand\_R} to $m_{\mathrm{hand}}(a_{\mathrm{lin}} - g)$ and $I_{\mathrm{hand}} a_{\mathrm{ang}}$ (with velocity evaluated at time $t$ consistent with \texttt{mj\_Euler}) reduces hold club drift to $84.85\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ vs.\ $69.61\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ in Pinocchio ($\max \|p_{\mathrm{mj}} - p_{\mathrm{pin}}\| = 15.34\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ for \texttt{overlap}/\texttt{interlock} and $2.38\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ for \texttt{ten\_finger}).
\item \textit{Parasitic normal constraint mass at $d_0 = 0.95$ and extra $d_0$ damping factor}: At \texttt{solimp} impedance $d_0 = 0.95$, the regularizer $R_{n,i} = \frac{1-d_0}{d_0}\hat{A}_0 = \frac{1}{19}\hat{A}_0$ added $53.4$\,kg ($\approx 170\,m_{\mathrm{club}}$) of artificial normal constraint mass, absorbing $99\%$ of preload acceleration at $t=0$ ($18.2\%$ static error without clamping) and slowing settling by $12\times$; in addition, \texttt{\_solref} multiplied damping by an extra factor of $d_0$. Using $d_0 = 0.01$ (\texttt{TOPOLOGY\_IMPEDANCE}) and removing both the extra $d_0$ damping factor and \texttt{\_forward\_clamped\_club} allows plain \texttt{mj\_forward} + \texttt{mj\_contactForce} to match \texttt{topology\_pair\_wrench} to $\le 0.035\%$ natively.
\item \textit{Missing analytical \texttt{mjc\_CapsuleCylinder} collider (\texttt{mjc\_Convex} \texttt{multiccd} contact jumping)}: MuJoCo dispatches \texttt{capsule}--\texttt{cylinder} pairs to \texttt{mjc\_Convex}, which with \texttt{multiccd} enabled emitted 3 contacts at $t=0$, dropped to 2 at $\theta \ge 5.6$\,nrad, and 1 at $\theta \ge 1.71$\,mrad on \texttt{ten\_finger\_little\_finger\_grip}. Emitting \texttt{<flag multiccd="disable" refsafe="disable"/>} on the topology path enforces a single closest-point contact across all orientations.
\item \textit{Tangential viscous friction scaling (\texttt{impratio} / \texttt{solreffriction})}: Tangential viscous damping $c_{\mathrm{fric}} = B_f/R_f = \frac{2\,\mathrm{impratio}}{(1-d_0)t_f \hat{A}_0}$ depends only on $\mathrm{impratio}/t_f$, whereas parasitic tangential mass $1/R_f \propto \mathrm{impratio}$. Using $\mathrm{impratio} = 0.095$ and $t_f = 1.9\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ for un-stepped static \texttt{mj\_forward} ($1/R_f = 0.27$\,g) and scaling both by $1000\times$ ($\mathrm{impratio} = 95$, $t_f = 1.9$\,ms) during \texttt{advance()} preserves $\mathrm{impratio}/t_f = 5.0 \times 10^4\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ while keeping explicit Euler stable ($d_0 t_f = 1.9\Delta t > \Delta t$).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Per-Site \texttt{solref} Calibration.} MuJoCo's negative-\texttt{solref} direct stiffness/damping parameterization is initialized per physical site $i$ on the topology path as
\begin{equation}
k_{0} = \frac{4 k}{d_0^2}, \quad b_i = k_{\mathrm{solref},i} \, \delta_0 \, d, \quad \mathrm{solref}_i = (-k_{\mathrm{solref},i}, -b_i),
\end{equation}
where $k = 1.6 \times 10^5$\,N/m is the shared penalty stiffness, $d = 0.5$\,s/m is the dissipation constant, $d_0 = 0.01$ is the topology \texttt{solimp} impedance at preload $\delta_0 = 0.25$\,mm, and $\mathrm{solimp} = (0.01, 0.01, 0.001, 0.5, 2)$.
\texttt{ClubInHands.calibrate(q, iterations=8, tol=1e-3, target\_force=...)} scales each site's stiffness against native \texttt{mj\_contactForce} after plain \texttt{mj\_forward}.

\subsection{OpenSim Topology Contact Reference (HRG-GS-G7, \#13305)}
\textbf{OpenSim Topology Contact Adapter.} The OpenSim contact adapter
(in \path{src/engines/physics_engines/opensim/python/} \\
\path{grip_contact_osim_sim.py} and \path{full_body_grip_contact.py}) connects the shared anatomical contact topology
$\mathcal{T}$ (\texttt{ContactTopology}, HRG-GS-G1, \#13299) to OpenSim's native
compliant contact subsystem via \texttt{ContactSphere}, \texttt{ContactMesh}, and
\texttt{ElasticFoundationForce}.
For each physical pad--grip contact pair in $\mathcal{T}$, contact spheres are placed on the
prescribed hand body (\texttt{LGrip} for both hands under the rigid-tie kinematic constraint),
using \texttt{map\_site\_to\_hand\_frame} without recomputing frames or modifying the shared law.

\textbf{Elastic Foundation Mapping Equations.} OpenSim implements a Winkler elastic foundation
model where force per unit area is proportional to penetration depth squared over a discretized
mesh:
\begin{equation}
f_{\mathrm{EF}}(\delta) = c_{\mathrm{geom}} k_{\mathrm{Winkler}} \delta^2 (1 + d \dot{\delta}),
\end{equation}
where $c_{\mathrm{geom}}$ is the Winkler geometric patch factor between a sphere of radius $r_{\mathrm{pad}}$
and a cylinder of radius $r_{\mathrm{grip}}$:
\begin{equation}
c_{\mathrm{geom}} = \frac{\pi}{2} \sqrt{\frac{r_{\mathrm{pad}} r_{\mathrm{grip}}}{r_{\mathrm{pad}} + r_{\mathrm{grip}}}}.
\end{equation}
To achieve exact parity with the linear contact law of the Pinocchio reference ($f_n = k_{\mathrm{lin}} \delta_0$
at neutral preload $\delta_0 = 0.25$\,mm), each unique site radius is calibrated via a static single-pad
equilibrium probe (\texttt{calibrated\_site\_foundation}):
\begin{equation}
k_{\mathrm{Winkler}} = \frac{k_{\mathrm{lin}} \delta_0}{c_{\mathrm{geom}} \delta_0^2} \cdot \frac{f_{\mathrm{target}}}{f_{\mathrm{measured}}}.
\end{equation}
This calibration ensures that at the neutral preload $\delta_0 = 0.25$\,mm, the foundation produces exactly
$f_{\mathrm{target}} = 42.7738$\,N with $0.0000\%$ numerical error against the shared linear law.

\textbf{Sphere-Chain Approximation of Capsule Sites.} Capsule (phalanx) sites, such as the trail little finger
in the ten-finger grip style ($r = 7.5$\,mm, half-length $L = 8$\,mm), are approximated by a discrete chain
of $N$ contact spheres along the capsule's medial axis with center spacing bounded by $r/2$:
\begin{equation}
N = \max\left(2, \mathrm{round}\left(\frac{2L}{0.5r}\right) + 1\right) = 5.
\end{equation}
The sphere centers are linearly spaced between capsule endpoints $p_0$ and $p_1$:
\begin{equation}
c_k = p_0 + \frac{k}{N - 1}(p_1 - p_0), \quad k \in \{0, \dots, N - 1\}.
\end{equation}
The target stiffness and force per sphere are scaled by $1/N$ ($k_k = k_{\mathrm{lin}}/N$, $f_k = f_{\mathrm{target}}/N$).
No empirical correction factor is applied: each sphere is calibrated only at the preload by the native-force
measurement above.

\textbf{Per-Pair Traces and Channel Accounting.} At each integration sample step, individual pair wrenches
are extracted from OpenSim's \texttt{ElasticFoundationForce} record labels (\texttt{force.X} and \texttt{torque.X}
on the club body) and decomposed into normal and tangential components via radial projection onto the grip cylinder axis.
Per-pair traces are assembled via \texttt{PairTraceRecorder}, logging finite positive normal forces for all physical
hand--grip pairs. Hand--hand pairs (such as overlap/interlock finger-on-hand sites) are reported as \texttt{NaN}
with the standard reason: \textit{``hand-hand pairs are allocated, not physical forces under prescribed hand motion''}.

\subsection{Drake Two-Hand Topology Contact (HRG-GS-G9, \#13307)}
\textbf{Body Topology.} \texttt{ClubInHands} in \path{src/engines/physics_engines/drake/python/grip_contact_sim.py} instantiates two distinct
prescribed hand bodies in the Drake \texttt{MultibodyPlant}: \texttt{lead\_hand} (side \texttt{L}) and \texttt{trail\_hand} (side \texttt{R}),
alongside the free \texttt{club} body. At every derivative evaluation, each hand body's pose and spatial velocity are set from its own
\texttt{GripInterface} hand frame via \texttt{hand\_frame\_states(weld, interface)[side]}. Each side's pads or \texttt{ContactTopology} site frames
are attached exclusively to that side's hand body in local hand-grip coordinates via \texttt{hand\_site\_attachments}. In native pad mode,
inter-hand and intra-hand pad--pad collisions are excluded in \texttt{SceneGraph} via \texttt{CollisionFilterDeclaration().ExcludeWithin(pad\_set)},
preserving exact numerical equivalence with the legacy pad contact baseline.

\textbf{Force Application Equations.} When \texttt{topology\_model: TopologyContactModel} is supplied, the adapter evaluates the shared G2/G6
contact law \texttt{topology\_side\_wrench(topology\_model, side, hand\_frame, club\_frame)} for each side $s \in \{\mathrm{L}, \mathrm{R}\}$
and applies an \texttt{ExternallyAppliedSpatialForce} to the club body at its side-$s$ grip-frame offset $p_{BoBq,B}^{(s)} = p_{\mathrm{offset},s}$:
\begin{equation}
F_{Bq,W}^{\mathrm{club},(s)} = \begin{bmatrix} M_{\mathrm{club},s} \\ F_{\mathrm{club},s} \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
together with the equal and opposite reaction spatial force on hand body $s$ about its origin $p_{HoHq,H}^{(s)} = 0$:
\begin{equation}
F_{Hq,W}^{\mathrm{hand},(s)} = \begin{bmatrix} -M_{\mathrm{club},s} - (p_{\mathrm{club},s} - p_{\mathrm{hand},s}) \times F_{\mathrm{club},s} \\ -F_{\mathrm{club},s} \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Using the shared contact law rather than Drake native point or hydroelastic capsule contact provides exact force-law parity (including torsional
patch friction) while keeping native capsule contact out of scope. Per-pair normal and tangential force traces are recorded via
\texttt{PairTraceRecorder}, and allocated hand--hand pairs are reported as \texttt{NaN} with explanatory reasons in \texttt{channel\_reasons}.

\textbf{Measured Parity vs.\ Pinocchio Topology Reference.} Across all 10 quantities in \texttt{PARITY\_QUANTITIES} on the driver fixture
($1100$\,N squeeze per hand, $0.04$\,s static hold and $0.04$\,s swing replay), worst-case peak and RMS errors remain well inside the binding
$5\%$ peak and $2\%$ RMS tolerances:

\item \texttt{interlock}: static hold peak $0.3333\%$, RMS $0.4670\%$; swing replay peak $0.3152\%$, RMS $1.7122\%$.
\item \texttt{ten\_finger}: static hold peak $0.4794\%$, RMS $0.2616\%$; swing replay peak $1.1222\%$, RMS $1.6485\%$.
\end{itemize}
Per-pair force traces match \texttt{topology\_side\_wrench} re-evaluated at the recorded states to $\le 10^{-9}$\,N.

\textbf{Limitations.} Hand bodies are prescribed from the weld trajectory, so the equal-and-opposite reaction spatial force applied to

\subsection{Cross-Engine Grip-Style Parity Report (HRG-GS-G10, \#13308)}
\textbf{Per-Pair Parity Extension and Contact-Point Drift.} \path{src/shared/python/grip_contact/parity.py} extends the cross-engine grip-contact comparison with per-pair normal force (\texttt{pair\_normal\_force}), per-pair tangential force (\texttt{pair\_tangential\_force}), and per-pair contact-point drift on the club grip (\texttt{contact\_point\_drift}) via \texttt{PAIR\_PARITY\_QUANTITIES} and \texttt{STYLE\_PARITY\_QUANTITIES}, reusing \texttt{QuantityError}, \texttt{parity\_errors}, \texttt{PEAK\_TOLERANCE} ($5.0\%$), and \texttt{RMS\_TOLERANCE} ($2.0\%$) with no duplicated metric code. \texttt{PairTraceRecorder} in \path{src/shared/python/grip_contact/topology_contact.py} records the Euclidean displacement $\|\mathbf{c}_i^{\mathrm{club}}(t) - \mathbf{c}_i^{\mathrm{club}}(0)\|$ of each physical hand--grip contact site projected onto the grip cylinder axis in the club frame in \texttt{ContactRun.pair\_contact\_point\_drift\_m}, while allocated hand--hand pairs record \texttt{NaN} with the reason \textit{``hand-hand pairs are allocated, not physical forces under prescribed hand motion''}.

\textbf{Classification Rationale (Determinate vs.\ Indeterminate).} Following \path{docs/development/full_body_models/GRIP_PARITY_DECISIONS.md} Section~19 (\textit{Indeterminacy Report}), every parity quantity is explicitly classified by \texttt{classify\_parity\_quantity}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
\item \texttt{DETERMINATE} (\texttt{net\_force}, \texttt{net\_moment\_com}): The total wrench acting on the rigid club about its centre of mass is uniquely identified by the club's translational and rotational Newton--Euler equations:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{net}} = m_{\mathrm{club}}(\ddot{\mathbf{p}}_{\mathrm{com}} - \mathbf{g}), \qquad
\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{com}} = \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{com}}\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}} + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times (\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{com}}\boldsymbol{\omega}).
\end{equation}
Determinate quantities must satisfy \texttt{PEAK\_TOLERANCE} ($\le 5.0\%$) and \texttt{RMS\_TOLERANCE} ($\le 2.0\%$); tolerances are never loosened.
\item \texttt{INDETERMINATE} (\texttt{force\_L}, \texttt{force\_R}, \texttt{internal\_force}, \texttt{squeeze}, \texttt{couple}, \texttt{deflection\_L}, \texttt{deflection\_R}, \texttt{rotation\_L}, \texttt{rotation\_R}, \texttt{pair\_normal\_force}, \texttt{pair\_tangential\_force}, \texttt{contact\_point\_drift}): The 6-DOF internal nullspace of the closed two-hand grip loop (opposition force $\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{int}} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{F}_L - \mathbf{F}_R)$, axial squeeze, internal couple about the grip midpoint, compliance deflections, and individual contact-pair forces) is hyperstatic and depends on each engine's contact compliance formulation (analytical penalty capsules in Pinocchio/Drake, calibrated \texttt{solref} capsules in MuJoCo, and discretized multi-sphere \texttt{ElasticFoundationForce} meshes in OpenSim). Every indeterminate quantity is reported with its measured peak and RMS error and labelled \textit{``indeterminate (hyperstatic two-hand grip; not identified by club kinematics)''}; it is never silently counted as a pass (\texttt{passes() == False}) and is never hidden.
\item \texttt{UNAVAILABLE}: Any engine whose runtime import fails reports \texttt{NaN} for all candidate peaks and errors together with the import failure reason, never zero and never a pass.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Separated Hands and Physical Hand--Hand Contact in \texttt{full-body-v2} (HRG-GS-G11, \#13309)}
\textbf{Model Topology and Opt-In Contract.} In the default \texttt{full-body-v1} and \texttt{full-body-v2} schemas, the lead and trail hand solids (\texttt{LHand}, \texttt{LHandStandoff}, \texttt{RHand}) are welded to the club body (\texttt{Clubface Vector}, parented by \texttt{Left Wrist Kinetically Driven}) and the right wrist (\texttt{RHandStandoff}, parented by \texttt{Right Wrist Kinetically Driven}) closes the loop through a 6-DOF bilateral weld constraint. Under that welded topology, hand--hand contact pairs have zero relative motion unless driven by an analytical proxy. HRG-GS-G11 (\path{src/shared/python/motion_matching/full_body_v2.py}, \texttt{derive\_full\_body\_v2\_spec(..., separate\_hands=True)} and \texttt{separate\_v2\_hands}) adds an explicit opt-in \texttt{full-body-v2} topology option (\texttt{mode="separated\_v2"}):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
\item \textbf{Lead Hand Body (\texttt{LHandBody}):} Parented by \texttt{Left Wrist Kinetically Driven} with the original wrist follower transform preserved so its body frame coincides with the welded club frame at $\mathbf{q} = \mathbf{0}$. Carries the \texttt{LHand} and \texttt{LHandStandoff} solids ($0.4945\text{ kg}$ total) and the \texttt{Grip\_LeftHand} frame.
\item \textbf{Trail Hand Body (\texttt{RHandStandoff}):} Parented by \texttt{Right Wrist Kinetically Driven}. Carries the \texttt{RHand} solid ($0.4600\text{ kg}$), re-expressed via $\mathbf{T}_{R \leftarrow C} = \mathbf{T}_{a}\,\mathbf{T}_{b}^{-1}$ so its neutral world pose and inertia are unchanged.
\item \textbf{Bare Club Body (\texttt{Clubface Vector}):} Rooted at \texttt{world} via the 6-DOF \texttt{ClubFreeJoint} (\texttt{ClubFreeRx..Rz}, \texttt{ClubFreePx..Pz}) with neutral \texttt{parent\_to\_base} equal to the welded neutral club pose. Retains only the bare club solids (\texttt{Clubhead}, \texttt{Rigid Shaft}, \texttt{Grip}; $0.3130\text{ kg}$).
\item \textbf{Immutability of Defaults:} When \texttt{separate\_hands=False} (default), \texttt{v1} and \texttt{v2} bytes and SHA256 digests are untouched. When \texttt{separate\_hands=True}, \texttt{FullBodyPinocchioModel} (\path{src/engines/physics_engines/pinocchio/python/native_model.py}) builds the tree with zero bilateral weld constraints (\texttt{len(constraints) == 0}) and couples both hands to the bare club through the shared retention law.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Frames, Equations, and Physical Hand--Hand Contact Law.} Let $\mathbf{T}_{W,L}(t) = (\mathbf{R}_L, \mathbf{p}_L)$ and $\mathbf{T}_{W,R}(t) = (\mathbf{R}_R, \mathbf{p}_R)$ denote the world poses of the lead and trail hand grip frames evaluated by Pinocchio forward kinematics along the left-wrist (\texttt{LHandBody}) and right-wrist (\texttt{RHandStandoff}) chains, with spatial velocities $(\mathbf{v}_L, \boldsymbol{\omega}_L)$ and $(\mathbf{v}_R, \boldsymbol{\omega}_R)$. Each hand applies a retention wrench $\mathbf{w}_s^{\mathrm{club}} = (\mathbf{F}_s^{\mathrm{club}}, \mathbf{M}_s^{\mathrm{club}})$ ($s \in \{L, R\}$) to the free bare club via the shared 6-axis compliance law. Simultaneously, in \texttt{mode="separated\_v2"} (\path{src/shared/python/grip_contact/topology_contact.py}, \texttt{topology\_hand\_hand\_wrenches} looping over the single per-pair evaluator \texttt{topology\_hand\_hand\_pair\_wrench}), every active hand--hand pair $p \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{HH}}(\text{style})$ (\texttt{trail\_palm\_lead\_thumb} in all styles; \texttt{overlap\_little\_finger\_lead\_index} in \texttt{overlap}; \texttt{interlock\_little\_finger\_lead\_middle} and \texttt{interlock\_lead\_index\_wrap} in \texttt{interlock}) is evaluated from the two independent hand states using the shared \texttt{pair\_contact} primitives (\texttt{sphere\_capsule}, \texttt{capsule\_capsule}, and oriented \texttt{capsule\_sphere}):
\begin{equation}
f_{n,p} = \max\!\bigl(0,\; k_n \delta_p^{3/2}(1 + c_n \dot{\delta}_p)\bigr), \qquad
\mathbf{f}_{t,p} = -\mu f_{n,p}\tanh\!\left(\frac{\|\mathbf{v}_{t,p}\|}{v_{\mathrm{tr}}}\right)\frac{\mathbf{v}_{t,p}}{\|\mathbf{v}_{t,p}\| + \epsilon}, \qquad
\|\mathbf{f}_{t,p}\| \le \mu f_{n,p}.
\end{equation}
For each pair $p$ with contact midpoint $\mathbf{c}_p$ and contact force $\mathbf{F}_p = f_{n,p}\mathbf{n}_p + \mathbf{f}_{t,p}$ acting on the lead hand, equal and opposite wrenches are applied to the lead and trail hands:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{lead},p} = +\mathbf{F}_p, \quad
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{trail},p} = -\mathbf{F}_p, \quad
\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{lead},p} = (\mathbf{c}_p - \mathbf{p}_L) \times \mathbf{F}_p, \quad
\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{trail},p} = (\mathbf{c}_p - \mathbf{p}_R) \times (-\mathbf{F}_p),
\end{equation}
satisfying exact action--reaction balance $\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{lead}} + \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{trail}} = \mathbf{0}$ and $\mathbf{M}_{O,\mathrm{lead}} + \mathbf{M}_{O,\mathrm{trail}} = \mathbf{0}$ about any common world point $O$ to $\le 10^{-15}\text{ N}$ ($\le 10^{-9}\text{ N}$ contract). To preserve exact right-wrist chain kinematics at every sample node $t_k$ while avoiding sub-sample Euclidean chord distortion during adaptive RK8 (\texttt{DOP853}) club integration, the right-hand state is interpolated via its $\mathrm{SE}(3)$ relative transform in the lead-hand frame, $\mathbf{R}_{L\to R}(t_k) = \mathbf{R}_L(t_k)^\top\mathbf{R}_R(t_k)$ and $\mathbf{p}_{L\to R}(t_k) = \mathbf{R}_L(t_k)^\top(\mathbf{p}_R(t_k) - \mathbf{p}_L(t_k))$.

\textbf{Identifiability Note and Constant Relative Hand Pose.} Standard full-body motion-capture marker sets carry wrist and forearm markers but no individual finger or phalanx markers (\texttt{HAND\_HAND\_IDENTIFIABILITY\_NOTE}). Consequently, individual finger and palm--thumb contact forces ($\mathbf{F}_p$ for $p \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{HH}}$) and the internal inter-hand squeeze are \emph{not observable from markers}. Only the net closure wrench $(\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{net}}, \mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{com}})$ applied by the two hands to the rigid club is dynamically identified by club kinematics (\texttt{QuantityClassification.DETERMINATE}); individual hand--club and hand--hand pair forces are classified per \texttt{GRIP\_PARITY\_DECISIONS.md} \S 19 as \texttt{INDETERMINATE} (\emph{``indeterminate (hyperstatic two-hand grip; not identified by club kinematics)''}). \subsection{Model Grip Binding Contract (HRG-GS-M1, \#13323)}
\textbf{Data Types and Protocol.} The engine-agnostic binding contract (\path{src/shared/python/grip_contact/binding.py})
decouples model-specific body hierarchies from the canonical \texttt{ContactTopology} $\mathcal{T}$ (HRG-GS-G1, \#13299),
the G6 physical contact evaluator (\texttt{topology\_side\_wrench}, \#13304), and the G3 allocated hand--hand reporter
(\texttt{allocate\_hand\_hand\_from\_closure}, \#13301). It defines three frozen records and a structural protocol:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
\item \texttt{SiteBinding}: maps a topology site identifier $s \in \mathcal{S}$ to a model body identifier $B$, a homogeneous
transform ${}^{B}T_{s} \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$ ($4\times 4$), and a representation tag $\mathrm{rep} \in \{\texttt{rigid\_proxy}, \texttt{phalange}\}$.
\item \texttt{HandClubBinding}: binds a model asset (\texttt{model\_id}, \texttt{model\_hash}, \texttt{handedness} $\in \{\texttt{right}, \texttt{left}\}$,
\texttt{attachment\_mode}, \texttt{club\_body} $B_c$, \texttt{lead\_hand\_body} $B_{\mathrm{lead}}$, \texttt{trail\_hand\_body} $B_{\mathrm{trail}}$)
to a tuple of \texttt{SiteBinding} entries, an explicit \texttt{unavailable\_sites} mapping ($s \mapsto \text{reason}$), and optional club grip frame
transforms ${}^{B_c}T_{F_{\mathrm{club},\sigma}}$ ($\sigma \in \{\mathrm{L}, \mathrm{R}\}$).
\item \texttt{GripCapabilities}: declares supported \texttt{attachment\_modes}, \texttt{supports\_physical\_pairs},
\texttt{supports\_wrench\_application}, and \texttt{native\_contact}.
\item \texttt{ModelGripAdapter} (and its optional re-export \texttt{GripProvider} in \path{src/engines/protocols.py}):
exposes \texttt{capabilities()}, \texttt{resolve\_binding(topology)}, \texttt{sample\_states(binding)} returning $4\times 4$ world poses
(\texttt{BodyStates}), and \texttt{apply\_wrenches(wrenches)}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Coordinate Frames.} Given body world pose ${}^{W}T_{B} = (R_{B}, p_{B}) \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$ and site binding
${}^{B}T_{s} = (R_{Bs}, p_{Bs})$, the world pose of site $s$ is ${}^{W}T_{s} = {}^{W}T_{B}\,{}^{B}T_{s}$.
With $p_{\mathrm{hand}}(s,\sigma)$ denoting the site's position in the hand grip frame $F_{\mathrm{hand},\sigma}$ from
\texttt{map\_site\_to\_hand\_frame}, the implied hand grip frame offset on body $B$ is:
\begin{equation}
{}^{B}T_{F_{\mathrm{hand},\sigma}}(s) = {}^{B}T_{s} \begin{bmatrix} I_3 & -p_{\mathrm{hand}}(s,\sigma) \\ 0^{\top} & 1 \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} R_{Bs} & p_{Bs} - R_{Bs}\,p_{\mathrm{hand}}(s,\sigma) \\ 0^{\top} & 1 \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
For rigid-proxy hands where ${}^{B}T_{s} = {}^{B}T_{F_{\mathrm{hand},\sigma}} \begin{bmatrix} I_3 & p_{\mathrm{hand}}(s,\sigma) \\ 0^{\top} & 1 \end{bmatrix}$,
all sites on hand $\sigma$ recover the identical frame ${}^{W}T_{F_{\mathrm{hand},\sigma}} = {}^{W}T_{B}\,{}^{B}T_{F_{\mathrm{hand},\sigma}}$
and are evaluated in a single call to \texttt{topology\_side\_wrench}.

\textbf{Equations: Body-Wrench Assembly and Virtual Work.} Let $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ be the set of active physical
hand--grip pairs. Each pair $i \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ connects site $s_i$ on hand body $B(s_i)$ to the club body $B_c$,
producing contact point $p_{c,i}$, hand force $f_{a,i}$ and moment $m_{a,i}$ about frame origin $p_{a,i}$, and club force
$f_{b,i} = -f_{a,i}$ and moment $m_{b,i}$ about frame origin $p_{b,i}$. \texttt{GripStepReport.body\_wrenches()} transports
each pair wrench to the respective body origins $p_{B(s_i)}$ and $p_{B_c}$, excluding all allocated pairs:
\begin{align}
F_{B} &= \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{phys}} : B(s_i) = B} f_{a,i}, \quad
M_{B} = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{phys}} : B(s_i) = B} \left[ m_{a,i} + (p_{a,i} - p_{B}) \times f_{a,i} \right], \\
F_{B_c} &= \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{phys}}} f_{b,i}, \quad
M_{B_c} = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{phys}}} \left[ m_{b,i} + (p_{b,i} - p_{B_c}) \times f_{b,i} \right].
\end{align}
By construction, action--reaction holds across all bound bodies:
\begin{equation}
F_{B_c} + \sum_{B \in \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hand}}} F_{B} = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{phys}}} (f_{b,i} + f_{a,i}) = 0.
\end{equation}
Under any virtual rigid twist $\mathcal{V}_c = (v_{B_c}, \omega_{B_c})$ applied to the club body (with contact point velocity
$v_{c,i} = v_{B_c} + \omega_{B_c} \times (p_{c,i} - p_{B_c})$ and zero spin couple at static evaluation), the virtual power
computed from the assembled club body wrench equals the sum of pairwise contact powers identically:
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{club}} = F_{B_c} \cdot v_{B_c} + M_{B_c} \cdot \omega_{B_c}
= \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{phys}}} \left[ f_{b,i} \cdot v_{B_c} + \left((p_{c,i} - p_{B_c}) \times f_{b,i}\right) \cdot \omega_{B_c} \right]
= \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{phys}}} f_{b,i} \cdot v_{c,i}.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Invariants and Acceptance Criteria.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
\item Pre-step validation (\texttt{validate\_binding}): $100\%$ of missing-body, unmapped physical site, and unsupported attachment-mode
cases raise \texttt{SchemaFeatureUnavailableError} before evaluation or stepping.
\item Rotation and transform invariants: $\|R^{\top} R - I_3\|_{\max} \le 10^{-9}$, $|\det(R) - 1| \le 10^{-9}$, bottom row $[0,0,0,1]$ within $10^{-9}$,
unique site IDs, and disjoint bound versus unavailable site sets.
\item Action--reaction residual $\left\|\sum_{B} F_{B}\right\|_2 \le 10^{-12}$\,N and virtual-work residual
$|P_{\mathrm{club}} - \sum_i f_{b,i} \cdot v_{c,i}| \le 10^{-12}$\,W.
\item Allocated hand--hand pairs are labelled \texttt{"allocated"}, never enter \texttt{body\_wrenches()} or \texttt{apply\_wrenches()},
and report \texttt{NaN} with a non-empty reason (never $0$) when no closure wrench is supplied; separated physical sites evaluate to true $0.0$.
\item \texttt{PinocchioGripAdapter} (\path{src/engines/physics_engines/pinocchio/python/grip_binding_adapter.py}) reproduces
\texttt{topology\_side\_wrench} to $\le 10^{-12}$\,N across overlap, interlock, and ten-finger styles.
\item Law of Demeter AST gate: maximum attribute chain depth $\le 2$ dots, function length $\le 100$ lines, and parameters $\le 8$.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Limitations.} Rigid hand bodies (\texttt{representation="rigid\_proxy"}) carry fixed finger-shaped contact proxies and cannot
represent active finger articulation or intrinsic muscle recruitment. Where hands are welded to the club or to each other, hand--hand
pairs remain allocated equivalents (reporting only, governed by an explicit prior) rather than identified biological forces.

\subsection{Grip Binding Manifests (HRG-GS-M2, \#13324)}
\textbf{Manifest Schema and Calibration-Time Resolution.}
To avoid duplicating contact site geometry across disparate model formats, versioned grip binding manifests use schema \texttt{grip-binding/v1} (\path{src/config/grip_bindings/<model_id>.json}). Each manifest specifies model identification and provenance (\texttt{model\_id}, \texttt{model\_hash}, \texttt{calibration\_pose}, \texttt{units} $\{ \texttt{"length"}\!: \texttt{"m"}, \texttt{"angle"}\!: \texttt{"rad"} \}$, \texttt{provenance}), kinematic roles (\texttt{handedness} $\in \{\texttt{right}, \texttt{left}\}$, \texttt{side\_roles} mapping anatomical side to lead/trail role, \texttt{attachment\_mode}, \texttt{club\_body}, \texttt{lead\_hand\_body}, \texttt{trail\_hand\_body}, optional \texttt{segment\_map}), and explicitly unavailable sites (\texttt{unavailable\_sites} $\{ s \mapsto \text{reason} \}$).
Site geometry is never stored statically in the manifest; instead, \texttt{binding\_from\_manifest(manifest, topology, interface)} resolves site positions dynamically from the canonical \texttt{ContactTopology} into body-local frames at the declared calibration pose via the model kinematics interface.

\textbf{Geometric Validation and Frame Bounds.}
The validator \texttt{validate\_resolved\_binding(binding, topology, interface)} enforces strict spatial and orientation consistency:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
\item \textit{Shaft Alignment}: The grip coordinate frame $+x$ axis points from the butt toward the clubhead. With unit direction $u_x = R_{F_{\mathrm{club}}} \hat{e}_x$ and expected axis $u_{\mathrm{exp}} = [1, 0, 0]^{\top}$, the directional alignment must satisfy:
\begin{equation}
u_x \cdot u_{\mathrm{exp}} \ge 0.999999.
\end{equation}
\item \textit{Radial and Axial Bounds}: For every bound site $s \in \mathcal{S}$ with body-local position mapped into the grip frame $p_{\mathrm{grip}}(s) = (x_s, y_s, z_s)$, the radial distance $r_s = \sqrt{y_s^2 + z_s^2}$ and axial position $x_s$ must satisfy:
\begin{equation}
0.005\,\mathrm{m} \le r_s \le 0.060\,\mathrm{m}, \quad -0.35\,\mathrm{m} \le x_s \le 0.05\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{equation}
\item \textit{Handedness Reversal}: In left-handed configurations (\texttt{handedness} = \texttt{"left"}), the lead/trail roles map to the mirrored anatomical bodies (right hand becomes lead, left hand becomes trail), and the resolved site positions mirror symmetrically along the local $z$-axis ($p_{\mathrm{grip},z} \mapsto -p_{\mathrm{grip},z}$).
\item \textit{Schema and Invariant Integrity}: Schema identifier must equal \texttt{"grip-binding/v1"}, length units must be metres (\texttt{"m"}), angle units must be radians (\texttt{"rad"}), every rotation matrix $R$ must be proper orthogonal ($\|R^{\top}R - I_3\|_{\max} \le 10^{-9}$, $|\det(R) - 1| \le 10^{-9}$), and bound sites and unavailable sites must be strictly disjoint with union equal to all topology sites.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Limitations.} Manifests configure kinematic body assignment and site resolution at static calibration poses; dynamic deformation of hand tissues during high-load impact or active finger re-gripping is not represented by the rigid-proxy attachment mode.

\subsection{Cross-Model Grip Qualification Harness (HRG-GS-M3, \#13325)}
\textbf{Series Definition and Resampling.}
The engine-agnostic qualification series \texttt{TopologyKineticsSeries} (\path{src/shared/python/grip_contact/topology_series.py}) records a topology grip run on the shared $\Delta t = 2\,\mathrm{ms}$ sample cadence (\texttt{FIXTURE\_DT\_S} $= 0.002\,\mathrm{s}$):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
\item Monotonic sample times $t \in \R^N$ ($t_{k+1} > t_k$, $N \ge 2$);
\item Per-pair normal force $f_{n,p}(t) \in \R^N$, tangential friction force $f_{t,p}(t) \in \R^N$, and torsional spin moment $m_{s,p}(t) \in \R^N$ for each topology pair $p \in \mathcal{P}$;
\item Per-hand resultant club wrench $F_{\sigma}(t) \in \R^{N\times 3}$ and $M_{\sigma}(t) \in \R^{N\times 3}$ for $\sigma \in \{\mathrm{L}, \mathrm{R}\}$;
\item Explicit pair labels $\ell_p \in \{\texttt{"physical"}, \texttt{"allocated"}\}$, provenance identifiers (\texttt{engine}, \texttt{model\_id}, \texttt{mapping\_hash}), and an unavailability reason map $\mathcal{R}$ (\texttt{channel\_reasons});
\item Physical guard \texttt{applied\_allocated\_force\_n} $= 0.0\,\mathrm{N}$, verifying via \texttt{GripStepReport.applied\_allocated\_force\_n()} and \texttt{apply\_report()} that allocated hand--hand channels never enter external body wrench application.
\end{itemize}
Both constructors---\texttt{from\_contact\_run(run, topology\_model, *, engine, mapping\_hash)} and \texttt{from\_step\_reports(times, reports, ...)}---resample trajectories recorded on arbitrary solver steps onto the uniform $2\,\mathrm{ms}$ grid $t_k = t_0 + k\Delta t$ via piecewise-linear interpolation while strictly preserving \texttt{NaN} unavailability markers. Design-by-Contract preconditions enforce a bijection between \texttt{NaN}-bearing channels and non-empty entries in \texttt{channel\_reasons}: no \texttt{NaN} channel may lack a reason, and no finite channel may carry a reason.

\textbf{Law-Level Reference Qualification.}
The helper \texttt{law\_level\_series(topology\_model, hand\_frames\_seq, club\_frames\_seq, times)} evaluates the shared contact law (\texttt{topology\_side\_wrench}) directly on identical prescribed hand and club site trajectories, providing the exact law-level reference against which the G6 Pinocchio topology contact simulation is qualified before forward-dynamics engine comparisons.

\textbf{Three-State Availability Envelope, Metrics, and Tolerances.}
In \path{src/shared/python/grip_contact/parity.py}, \texttt{compare\_topology\_series(reference, candidate)} evaluates each channel $x(t)$ against $x_{\mathrm{ref}}(t)$ using the shared peak and RMS relative error metrics (\texttt{compute\_quantity\_error}):
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{peak}} = \frac{\left|\max_t \|x(t)\| - \max_t \|x_{\mathrm{ref}}(t)\|\right|}{\max_t \|x_{\mathrm{ref}}(t)\|}, \qquad
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rms}} = \frac{\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N} \|x(t_k) - x_{\mathrm{ref}}(t_k)\|_2^2}}{\max_t \|x_{\mathrm{ref}}(t)\|},
\end{equation}
against the pinned tolerances \texttt{PEAK\_TOLERANCE} $= 0.05$ ($5\%$) and \texttt{RMS\_TOLERANCE} $= 0.02$ ($2\%$). Each channel and the overall \texttt{TopologyParityReport} take one of three states:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
\item \texttt{"unavailable"} (\texttt{passed = False}): emitted whenever \texttt{reference.mapping\_hash != candidate.mapping\_hash} (reason \texttt{"mapping hash mismatch"}), or when any required physical channel is missing or contains \texttt{NaN} on either side. A report with any unavailable required channel never passes;
\item \texttt{"failed"} (\texttt{passed = False}): all required physical channels are available, and at least one exceeds $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{peak}} > 0.05$ or $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rms}} > 0.02$;
\item \texttt{"passed"} (\texttt{passed = True}): all required physical channels are available and satisfy $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{peak}} \le 0.05$ and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rms}} \le 0.02$.
\end{enumerate}
Channels labelled \texttt{"allocated"} on both sides are compared and reported separately in \texttt{allocated\_channels}; they never influence the physical parity verdict.

\textbf{Limitations.}
Law-level qualification verifies that an engine's contact evaluation and integration pipeline reproduces the shared contact law at matched states; forward-dynamics trajectory divergence across distinct multi-body solvers (OpenSim, MuJoCo, Drake) is qualified separately by the per-engine adapters (G7--G9). Rigid-proxy hand bindings do not identify biological finger articulation or internal co-contraction squeeze.

\subsection{MuJoCo Humanoid Grip Adapter (HRG-GS-M5, \#13327)}
\textbf{Method and Admitted Models.}
In \path{src/engines/physics_engines/mujoco/python/humanoid_grip_adapter.py}, \texttt{MuJoCoHumanoidGripAdapter} implements the shared \texttt{ModelGripAdapter} contract across the five native MuJoCo humanoid golf MJCFs where the club is a child of one rigid hand body and linked to the other hand body by an equality \texttt{<weld>}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
\item \texttt{mujoco\_golfer} (\path{src/engines/physics_engines/mujoco/models/generated/golfer.xml}): \texttt{club\_shaft} parented to \texttt{hand\_left}, equality weld \texttt{right\_hand\_grip\_weld} on \texttt{hand\_right};
\item \texttt{mujoco\_upper\_body\_swing} (\path{src/engines/physics_engines/mujoco/_golf_swing_upper_body_xml.py}): \texttt{club} parented to \texttt{right\_hand} via \texttt{club\_wrist}, equality weld on \texttt{left\_hand};
\item \texttt{mujoco\_full\_body\_swing} (\path{src/engines/physics_engines/mujoco/_golf_swing_full_body_xml.py}): \texttt{club} parented to \texttt{right\_hand} via \texttt{club\_wrist}, equality weld on \texttt{left\_hand};
\item \texttt{mujoco\_advanced\_biomechanical\_swing} (\path{src/engines/physics_engines/mujoco/_golf_swing_advanced_xml.py}): \texttt{club\_grip} parented to \texttt{right\_hand}, equality weld on \texttt{left\_hand};
\item \texttt{mujoco\_canonical\_golf\_humanoid} (\path{src/engines/physics_engines/mujoco/_golf_swing_canonical_xml.py}): \texttt{club\_grip} parented to \texttt{left\_hand}, site equality weld (\texttt{right\_grip\_site} to \texttt{club\_grip\_site}) on \texttt{right\_hand}.
\end{enumerate}
Each admitted model has a committed \texttt{grip-binding/v1} manifest under \path{src/config/grip_bindings/<model_id>.json} resolving $100\%$ of canonical \texttt{ContactTopology} sites ($14$ sites across lead and trail rigid hand proxies, or $15$ with \texttt{trigger\_finger=True}) via \texttt{binding\_from\_manifest} and \texttt{validate\_resolved\_binding}. Visual style from \texttt{GripSpec} (\texttt{overlap}, \texttt{interlock}, \texttt{ten\_finger}) is rendered by attaching zero-mass, non-colliding visual proxy geoms (\texttt{contype="0" conaffinity="0" mass="0" group="1"}) at the mapped hand-frame site centres $\mathbf{p}_{s}^{\mathrm{hand}}$ and finger-link stations, leaving \texttt{body\_mass}, \texttt{body\_inertia}, \texttt{body\_ipos}, \texttt{qpos}, \texttt{qvel}, and \texttt{efc\_force} bit-for-bit invariant ($\le 0.0$ difference).

\textbf{Equations and Allocated Weld Constraint Extraction.}
For an equality weld $e$ connecting body $b_1$ (closing hand) and body $b_2$ (club) with active constraint row indices $\mathcal{I}_e = \{i : \texttt{efc\_type}[i] = \texttt{mjCNSTR\_EQUALITY} \wedge \texttt{efc\_id}[i] = e\}$, the generalized constraint force is:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\mathrm{weld}} = J_{\mathrm{efc}}[\mathcal{I}_e,:]^{\top} \mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{efc}}[\mathcal{I}_e] \in \R^{n_v}.
\end{equation}
Evaluating the 6D body Jacobians $J_{b_1}(\mathbf{p}_a), J_{b_2}(\mathbf{p}_a) \in \R^{6 \times n_v}$ at the club-side weld anchor $\mathbf{p}_a \in \R^3$ yields the relative weld Jacobian $J_{\Delta} = J_{b_2}(\mathbf{p}_a) - J_{b_1}(\mathbf{p}_a)$. When $\operatorname{rank}(J_{\Delta}, 10^{-3}) = 6$, \texttt{extract\_weld\_closure\_wrench} delegates directly to \texttt{weld\_wrench\_on\_club}; on planar/reduced-DOF arm chains (\texttt{mujoco\_upper\_body\_swing}, \texttt{mujoco\_full\_body\_swing}) where out-of-plane singular values satisfy $\sigma_{\min}(J_{\Delta}) < 10^{-3}$, it solves the truncated-SVD least-squares system:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{w}_{\mathrm{club}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{weld}} \\ \mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{weld}} \end{bmatrix} = \pm \left(J_{\Delta}^{\top}\right)^{+}_{\mathrm{rcond}=10^{-3}} \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\mathrm{weld}} \in \R^6,
\end{equation}
with sign $+1$ when the club is object 2 and $-1$ when the club is object 1. The extracted closure wrench $\mathbf{w}_{\mathrm{club}}$ is passed to \texttt{evaluate\_contacts(..., closure=closure, prior=prior)}, which partitions $\mathbf{w}_{\mathrm{club}}$ across the active hand--hand pairs $p \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{hh}}$ via \texttt{allocate\_hand\_hand\_from\_closure} under the named prior $\pi$ (default \texttt{prior\_name="equal\_split"}), labelling every hand--hand output \texttt{"allocated"} (\texttt{external:<pair>\_allocated}) and enforcing \texttt{applied\_allocated\_force\_n()} $= 0.0\,\mathrm{N}$.

\textbf{Physics Honesty and Optional Attachment Redesign.}
In the default welded mode (\texttt{attachment\_redesign="off"}), \texttt{capabilities()} declares \texttt{attachment\_modes=("weld",)}, \texttt{supports\_physical\_pairs=False}, \texttt{supports\_wrench\_application=False}, and a non-empty \texttt{unavailable\_reason} naming the welded club and hand bodies; both \texttt{apply\_report} and \texttt{apply\_wrenches} raise \texttt{WeldedGripBodyError} (\texttt{SchemaFeatureUnavailableError}, \texttt{ValueError}). Behind the explicit \texttt{attachment\_redesign} flag (off by default), \texttt{build\_humanoid\_grip\_mjcf} detaches the club body to \texttt{<worldbody>} with a \texttt{<freejoint/>}, removes the hand--club \texttt{<weld>} equality, adds \texttt{<exclude>} tags between both hand bodies and the club body, and supports two mutually exclusive physical modes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
\item \texttt{"free\_club\_shared"}: zero native grip collision pairs (\texttt{registered\_grip\_collision\_count() == 0}), \texttt{supports\_physical\_pairs=True}, \texttt{supports\_wrench\_application=True}, \texttt{native\_contact=False}. Physical body wrenches $(\mathbf{F}_b, \mathbf{M}_b)$ about $\mathbf{x}_b = \texttt{xpos}[b]$ are transferred to the body COM $\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{com},b} = \texttt{xipos}[b]$ and applied to \texttt{data.xfrc\_applied}:
\begin{equation}
\texttt{xfrc\_applied}[b, 0\!:\!3] = \mathbf{F}_b, \qquad \texttt{xfrc\_applied}[b, 3\!:\!6] = \mathbf{M}_b + (\mathbf{x}_b - \mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{com},b}) \times \mathbf{F}_b.
\end{equation}
\item \texttt{"free\_club\_native"}: attaches \texttt{contype="0" conaffinity="0" mass="0"} sphere pad geoms and a grip cylinder geom coupled by explicit \texttt{condim="6"} \texttt{<pair>} elements, declaring \texttt{native\_contact=True} and \texttt{supports\_wrench\_application=False}. Calling \texttt{apply\_report} or \texttt{apply\_wrenches} raises \texttt{SchemaFeatureUnavailableError}, ensuring native and shared contact are never applied twice to the same pair.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Friction-Cone Reading of the Allocation.} On \texttt{mujoco\_upper\_body\_swing} and \texttt{mujoco\_full\_body\_swing} the equal-split allocation of the weld wrench is almost purely tangential ($f_n \approx 0$\,N, $f_t \approx 1150$--$1208$\,N on \texttt{trail\_palm\_lead\_thumb} and \texttt{overlap\_little\_finger\_lead\_index}), so the reported friction-cone ratio $\|f_t\|/(\mu f_n)$ is far above 1. These allocated equivalents therefore could not be carried by the named finger pairs as unilateral Coulomb contact; this characterises the weld models' closure demand, not a contact force, and is reported rather than clipped.

\textbf{Limitations.}
All five admitted MuJoCo humanoid MJCFs represent each hand as a single rigid body (or fixed finger proxy group without articulated phalangeal DOFs), so site bindings use \texttt{representation="rigid\_proxy"}. In the default welded topology, hand--grip and hand--hand forces are reporting/allocated channels rather than dynamically independent contact forces unless \texttt{attachment\_redesign} is explicitly enabled.

\subsection{MuJoCo Grip Laboratory: Phalanx-Mapped Hand--Hand Contact (HRG-GS-M6, \#13328)}
\textbf{Method and Segment-Mapped Phalanx Manifests.}
The MuJoCo Shadow/Allegro grip laboratory (\path{src/engines/physics_engines/mujoco/python/grip_lab_adapter.py}) wraps the bilateral hand scene builder (\path{src/engines/physics_engines/mujoco/python/mujoco_humanoid_golf/_grip_modelling_xml.py}) into the shared \texttt{ModelGripAdapter} contract across two multi-fingered robotic hand pairs around a free $6$-DOF cylindrical handle (\texttt{cylinder\_object}) driven by mocap palm welds (\texttt{lh\_mocap}, \texttt{rh\_mocap}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
\item \texttt{mujoco\_shadow\_hand\_lab} (\path{src/config/grip_bindings/mujoco_shadow_hand_lab.json}): bilateral 24-DOF Shadow Dexterous Hand scene (\texttt{lh\_palm}, \texttt{rh\_palm});
\item \texttt{mujoco\_allegro\_hand\_lab} (\path{src/config/grip_bindings/mujoco_allegro_hand_lab.json}): bilateral 16-DOF Wonik Allegro Hand scene (\texttt{lh\_palm}, \texttt{rh\_palm}).
\end{enumerate}
Each laboratory model is explicitly designated as \emph{a contact fixture, not a qualified golfer}. If the local hand MJCF/mesh assets are absent, \texttt{check\_hand\_assets\_available} fails closed by raising \texttt{SchemaFeatureUnavailableError} without network downloads.
Unlike the rigid-proxy humanoid manifests (M5), both laboratory \texttt{grip-binding/v1} manifests declare \texttt{attachment\_mode = "separated\_v2"} and provide a bijection \texttt{segment\_map} mapping every canonical \texttt{ContactTopology} site ($14$ sites for \texttt{trigger\_finger=False}, $15$ sites for \texttt{trigger\_finger=True}) to a \textbf{distinct} articulated phalanx or palm sub-body (\texttt{representation="phalange"} on every \texttt{SiteBinding}). At calibration pose $\mathbf{q}_0$, \texttt{MuJoCoGripLabAdapter} records the hand-root-to-phalanx relative transform $T_{H_{\sigma} \to B_i}^{0} \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$ for each phalanx body $B_i$ on hand $\sigma \in \{\mathrm{L}, \mathrm{R}\}$. During articulated evaluation ($\mathbf{q} \neq \mathbf{q}_0$, \texttt{frozen\_fingers=False}), the live phalanx deflection $\Delta T_{B_i}(\mathbf{q}) = T_{H_{\sigma} \to B_i}(\mathbf{q})\,\left(T_{H_{\sigma} \to B_i}^{0}\right)^{-1}$ updates the world site frame:
\begin{equation}
T_{W \to s}(\mathbf{q}) = T_{W \to H_{\sigma}} \, \Delta T_{B_i}(\mathbf{q}) \, T_{B_i \to s}.
\end{equation}
In frozen-finger mode (\texttt{frozen\_fingers=True}), all finger joint coordinates are locked to $\mathbf{q} = \mathbf{q}_0$ ($\Delta T_{B_i}(\mathbf{q}_0) = I_4$), reducing the phalanx kinematics exactly to the rigid-proxy hand frame $T_{W \to H_{\sigma}}$.

\textbf{Equations for Physical Hand--Hand Contact on Phalanges.}
Because the two hands and their articulated phalanges are separate rigid bodies in \texttt{separated\_v2} mode, all active hand--hand topology pairs $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{hh}}$---namely \texttt{trail\_palm\_lead\_thumb} (all styles), \texttt{overlap\_little\_finger\_lead\_index} (\texttt{overlap}), \texttt{interlock\_little\_finger\_lead\_middle} and \texttt{interlock\_lead\_index\_wrap} (\texttt{interlock}), alongside the direct hand--grip pair \texttt{ten\_finger\_little\_finger\_grip} (\texttt{ten\_finger})---are evaluated as \textbf{physical} contacts (\texttt{pair\_labels[p] = "physical"}, \texttt{applied\_allocated\_force\_n() = 0.0}) via the shared G2 primitive kernels (\texttt{sphere\_capsule}, \texttt{capsule\_sphere}, and \texttt{capsule\_capsule} in \path{src/shared/python/grip_contact/pair_contact.py}) invoked through \texttt{topology\_hand\_hand\_pair\_wrench} (\path{src/shared/python/grip_contact/topology_contact.py}).
For a hand--hand pair $p = (s_a, s_b)$ with world site states $(\mathbf{p}_a, \mathbf{v}_a, \boldsymbol{\omega}_a)$ on phalanx $B_a$ and $(\mathbf{p}_b, \mathbf{v}_b, \boldsymbol{\omega}_b)$ on phalanx $B_b$, closest primitive points $(\mathbf{c}_a, \mathbf{c}_b)$, signed separation $g = d - (r_a + r_b)$, and unit normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ from $B_a$ toward $B_b$, the penetration depth and compression rate under nominal inter-hand preload $\delta_0 = F_{\mathrm{hh},0} / k_n$ ($F_{\mathrm{hh},0} = \alpha_{\mathrm{hh}} F_{\mathrm{sq}}$) are:
\begin{equation}
\delta = \max(0, -g + \delta_0), \qquad \dot{\delta} = -\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot (\mathbf{v}_b(\mathbf{c}_b) - \mathbf{v}_a(\mathbf{c}_a)).
\end{equation}
The unilateral Hunt--Crossley normal force, regularised Coulomb--viscous tangential force, and equal-and-opposite phalanx body wrenches transferred to body origins $\mathbf{x}_{B_a}, \mathbf{x}_{B_b}$ are:
\begin{equation}
f_n = \begin{cases} \max\!\left(0,\, k_n \delta^p + c_n \delta^p \dot{\delta}\right), & \delta > 0, \\ 0, & \delta \le 0, \end{cases}
\qquad
\mathbf{f}_t = -\left(\mu_s \tanh\!\left(\frac{\|\mathbf{v}_t\|}{v_{\mathrm{tr}}}\right) + \mu_v \|\mathbf{v}_t\|\right) f_n \frac{\mathbf{v}_t}{\|\mathbf{v}_t\|},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F}_{B_a} = -f_n \hat{\mathbf{n}} + \mathbf{f}_t = -\mathbf{F}_{B_b}, \qquad
\mathbf{M}_{B_a} = \mathbf{m}_s + (\mathbf{c}_a - \mathbf{x}_{B_a}) \times \mathbf{F}_{B_a}, \qquad
\mathbf{M}_{B_b} = -\mathbf{m}_s + (\mathbf{c}_b - \mathbf{x}_{B_b}) \times \mathbf{F}_{B_b}.
\end{equation}
Hence at every contact midpoint $\mathbf{c}$, $\mathbf{F}_{B_a} + \mathbf{F}_{B_b} = \mathbf{0}$ and $\mathbf{M}_{B_a}(\mathbf{c}) + \mathbf{M}_{B_b}(\mathbf{c}) = \mathbf{0}$.
In \texttt{contact\_mode="shared"}, \texttt{build\_grip\_lab\_mjcf} sets \texttt{contype="0" conaffinity="0"} on all geoms and strips MJCF \texttt{<contact>} pairs so MuJoCo's native collision detector generates zero grip contacts (\texttt{capabilities().native\_contact = False}, \texttt{supports\_physical\_pairs = True}, \texttt{supports\_wrench\_application = True}), and \texttt{apply\_wrenches} writes the COM-transferred wrenches into \texttt{data.xfrc\_applied}. In \texttt{contact\_mode="native"}, native MJCF collision geoms remain active (\texttt{native\_contact = True}, \texttt{supports\_wrench\_application = False}) and \texttt{apply\_wrenches} raises \texttt{SchemaFeatureUnavailableError}, preventing double application of native and shared contact on any pair.

\subsection{The \texttt{full-body-v2} Schema (HRG-D1, \#13170)}
The \texttt{full-body-v2} schema replaces the v1 trunk (\texttt{TorsoInput}
and \texttt{SpineInputX/Y}) with two regional 3-DOF joints, lumbar (L5/S1)
and thoracic (T12/L1), each ISB \texttt{isb\_spine} ZXY. Coordinates migrate
from v1 through a constrained rotation solve with the
\texttt{bruno2015\_regional} prior. The solve reports its residuals and its
non-uniqueness. \texttt{full-body-v1} stays the default. The definition,
equations and residual report are in the Simscape matching reference,
section ``The full-body-v2 Schema and Trunk Coordinate Migration''. The geometry
is HRG-D2.

\subsection{Landmark Regional Trunk Joints (HRG-D2, \#13173)}
HRG-D2 places L5/S1 at the HRG-C3 design offset and C7/T1 at the cervicale.
T12/L1 is placed at the Gray lumbar fraction $18/46$, and the neutral
curvature is checked against Yukawa et al.\ 2018. The de Leva trunk solids
are re-parented and the dummy body is dropped. The ROM becomes soft limits,
with hard ranges report-only. The back markers observe rank 5 of the six
trunk coordinates, so the split is regularised by
\texttt{bruno2015\_regional}. The details, the mass ledger, the cross-engine
results and the best-fit Hub residual (61.9 / 34.3~mm) are in the Simscape
matching reference, section ``Landmark Regional Trunk Joints for
full-body-v2''.
