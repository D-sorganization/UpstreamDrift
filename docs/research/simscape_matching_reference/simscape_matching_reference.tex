\begin{abstract}
This reference documents tour-reference and owner optical-capture matching with
the \texttt{GS3DX\_Human} ellipsoid model under MATLAB R2025b. It defines SI
capture transformations, joint-role mapping, closed-grip inverse kinematics,
marker-offset calibration, full foot orientation and model/runtime provenance.
The current shared package contains eight 1080p H.264 videos using the
refined Human ellipsoid geometry. Selected club-orientation fits have
mean derived-position RMS of 16.944 mm for the tour and 20.762 mm for
the owner, with mean club-orientation errors of 5.476 and 19.287 degrees.
Selection balances orientation error against joint-step regularity;
these values are fit diagnostics rather than anatomical accuracy.
An anchor-seeded native position/velocity audit accepts all 733 owner
anchor and midpoint states, while the tour accepts 1,306 of 1,307.
Between-sample branch continuity, dependent acceleration, calibrated
contact and independent full-swing forward dynamics remain unqualified.
The model lineage, intrinsic Euler and torque mappings, inertia
assumptions, controller experiments and rejected contact-load results remain
available for reproduction. A half-turn leg-orientation false acceptance was
reproduced and repaired; a historical receipt records 287 passing native software/parameter checks. Bounded
bilateral address corrections pass their geometry gates, and both feet
develop support in separately verified 20 ms gravity probes. Later one-second supported holds pass their stated gates with retained upper-body feedback and prescribed neck motion. Full-swing independent forward dynamics remain unqualified. Native execution, software checks, visual review
and physical acceptance are recorded separately. Gates \#11156, \#11160 and
\#11173 remain open, and cross-execution reproducibility concerns are explicit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Coordinate Frames, Units, and Sign Conventions}

\subsection{Global World, Capture, and Address Coordinate Systems}
The simulation environment enforces standard SI metric units: lengths in meters (m), masses in kilograms (kg), forces in Newtons (N), torques in Newton-meters (N$\cdot$m), angles in radians (rad) for mathematical formulations, and degrees ($^\circ$) for tabular reporting.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Simscape World Frame ($\mathcal{F}_W$):} Defined with $+Z_W$ vertical against gravity ($\mathbf{g} = [0, 0, -9.80665]^T$~m/s$^2$), $+X_W$ pointing forwards across the golfer's frontal plane at address (facing direction), and $+Y_W$ pointing laterally down the target line to the golfer's left.
    \item \textbf{Native Capture Coordinate Frame ($\mathcal{F}_C$):} Optical motion capture systems natively export trajectories with $+Y_C$ pointing upwards. The loader \texttt{gs3dx\_capture\_markers.m} performs an orthogonal mapping into the Simscape world frame:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{p}_W = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \mathbf{p}_C = \begin{bmatrix} x_C \\ -z_C \\ y_C \end{bmatrix}.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Address Frame ($\mathcal{F}_{\text{addr}}$) and Waist Origin:} In \texttt{gs3dx\_capture\_address\_transform.m}, the address origin $\mathbf{p}_{\text{origin}}$ is defined as the mean position of the \textbf{four waist markers} at frame 1 ($t = 0$):
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{p}_{\text{origin}} = \frac{1}{4} \left(\mathbf{p}_{\text{WaistLeft}} + \mathbf{p}_{\text{WaistRight}} + \mathbf{p}_{\text{WaistLBack}} + \mathbf{p}_{\text{WaistRBack}}\right)_{t=0}.
    \end{equation}
    The address origin is \textbf{not} the ankle midpoint or ground datum. Point positions transform to local address coordinates via the basis matrix $\mathbf{S} = [\mathbf{s}_{\text{facing}}, \mathbf{s}_{\text{target}}, \mathbf{s}_{\text{up}}]$:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{p}_{\text{addr}} = \mathbf{S}^T (\mathbf{p}_W - \mathbf{p}_{\text{origin}}).
    \end{equation}
    Under this change of frame, forces transform purely by rotation:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{f}_{\text{addr}} = \mathbf{S}^T \mathbf{f}_W,
    \end{equation}
    For a resultant moment $\mathbf{m}_W$ taken about the World origin,
    moments transform by incorporating the lever arm of the origin shift:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{m}_{\text{addr}} = \mathbf{S}^T \mathbf{m}_W - (\mathbf{S}^T \mathbf{p}_{\text{origin}}) \times \mathbf{f}_{\text{addr}}.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Ground Datum vs. Address Origin:} The waist origin sits at elevated vertical position. The ground height belongs to the selected model workspace and experiment. A recorded run used $Z_W \approx -1.00027$~m and ankle height $0.0702$~m; these are not universal constants or the capture address origin. Record the actual floor transform for each replay.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Joint Sign Conventions and Anatomical Definitions}
Joint rotations are parameterized as intrinsic transformations. For revolute and universal primitives, the anatomical angle $A_j$ is related to the raw Simscape generalized coordinate $q_j$ via:
\begin{equation}
    A_j = s_j \cdot \text{wrap}_{180}(q_j - q_{j,0}),
\end{equation}
where $s_j \in \{+1, -1\}$ is the structural joint direction sign, $q_{j,0}$ is the anatomical neutral angle, and $\text{wrap}_{180}(\theta) = \theta - 360^\circ \cdot \lfloor(\theta + 180^\circ)/360^\circ\rfloor$.

\subsection{Quaternion-to-Euler Mapping and Torque Duality}
\texttt{GS3DX\_Quat} replaces Gimbal joints with native Spherical Joints parameterized by unit quaternions $\mathbf{q} = [q_w, q_x, q_y, q_z]^T$. As documented by MathWorks~\cite{MathWorksSpherical}, the underlying joint primitive eliminates quaternion orientation singularities. However, to interface with legacy Euler-angle signal buses, \texttt{gs3dx\_xyz\_map.m} extracts intrinsic $X$-$Y$-$Z$ angles using the exact sequence:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{R} = \mathbf{R}_x(a) \mathbf{R}_y(b) \mathbf{R}_z(c).
\end{equation}
Let $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ denote the rotation matrix computed from unit quaternion $\mathbf{q}$. The extracted angles are evaluated as:
\begin{align}
    a &= \text{atan2}(-R_{23}, R_{33}), \\
    b &= \text{atan2}(R_{13}, \text{hypot}(R_{11}, R_{12})), \\
    c &= \text{atan2}(-R_{12}, R_{11}),
\end{align}
with $a$ and $c$ unwrapped to the continuous branch of reference angles $\mathbf{q}_{\text{ref}}$.
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Singularity Caveat:} The adapter mapping remains \textbf{singular where $\cos(b) = 0$ ($|b| = 90^\circ$)}. While the Spherical Joint itself does not lock, representing motion through this adapter re-introduces the kinematic singularity; full singularity removal is not achieved.
\end{quote}

The kinematic velocity mapping relates Euler angle rates $\dot{\mathbf{q}}_r = [\dot{a}, \dot{b}, \dot{c}]^T$ (in radians/s) to the follower-frame angular velocity $\boldsymbol{\omega}_F$:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\omega}_F = \mathbf{E}(b, c) \dot{\mathbf{q}}_r, \quad \text{where} \quad \mathbf{E}(b, c) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos b \cos c & \sin c & 0 \\ -\cos b \sin c & \cos c & 0 \\ \sin b & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
By the principle of virtual power, mechanical power is invariant under generalized coordinates: $\mathbf{T}^T \boldsymbol{\omega}_F = \boldsymbol{\tau}_q^T \dot{\mathbf{q}}_r$. The torque dual transformation mapping Gimbal axis torques $\boldsymbol{\tau}$ and joint damping to follower-frame torque $\mathbf{T}$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{T} = \mathbf{E}^{-T} \left(\boldsymbol{\tau} - \mathbf{D} \dot{\mathbf{q}}_{\text{deg}}\right),
\end{equation}
accounting explicitly for conversion between degrees and radians ($\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{\text{deg}} = \dot{\mathbf{q}}_r \times 180 / \pi$).

\subsection{Midfoot Joint Architecture and Mass Partitioning}
In \texttt{GS3DX\_Human}, a sprung revolute joint is introduced at the metatarsophalangeal (MTP) boundary, positioned at 73\% of foot length from the posterior heel and 25~mm above the sole:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\text{MTP}} = -K_{\text{midfoot}} \theta_{\text{MTP}} - C_{\text{midfoot}} \dot{\theta}_{\text{MTP}},
\end{equation}
where $K_{\text{midfoot}} = 2,000$~N$\cdot$m/rad and $C_{\text{midfoot}} = 0.5$~N$\cdot$m$\cdot$s/rad. Total foot mass (1.096~kg) is partitioned into a 0.250~kg forefoot solid carrying the toe contacts and a 0.846~kg rearfoot solid carrying the heel and ball contacts.

\section{Motion Capture Calibration, Inverse Kinematics, and Identifiability}

\subsection{Capture Pre-Processing and Trajectory Gaps}
The reference motion (\texttt{capture-A}) is sampled at 360~Hz over 654 frames ($T = 1.814$~s). Key temporal events are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Top of Backswing:} Frame 378 ($t = 1.050$~s), defined by pelvis yaw reversal.
    \item \textbf{Peak Clubhead Speed:} Frame 476 ($t = 1.319$~s), reaching 50.7~m/s approximately 16~cm before ball impact.
    \item \textbf{Ball Contact:} Frame 477 ($t = 1.322$~s), when the clubhead returns to address position along the target line; head speed drops to 41.4~m/s across contact.
\end{itemize}
Marker dropouts occur on the clubhead at frame 445, frames 519--551, and frames 653--654. The inverse kinematics algorithm omits gap-filled samples from the residual vector (\texttt{jc.gap}); fitting linearly interpolated points introduces severe tracking distortion ($> 0.5$~m) through follow-through.

\subsection{Levenberg--Marquardt Inverse Kinematics Formulation}
Inverse kinematics minimizes residual tracking errors over 33 independent coordinates $\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^{33}$ using \texttt{KinematicsSolver} as forward kinematics:
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\mathbf{q}_k} \frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{r}_{\text{marker}}(\mathbf{q}_k)\|^2 + \frac{w_p^2}{2} \|\mathbf{q}_k - \mathbf{q}_{\text{posture}}\|^2 + \frac{w_s^2}{2} \|\mathbf{q}_k - \mathbf{q}_{k-1}\|^2 + \frac{w_{\text{rom}}^2}{2} \|\mathbf{r}_{\text{rom}}(\mathbf{q}_k)\|^2,
\end{equation}
where $w_p = 0.01$~m/rad pulls trunk coordinates toward neutral, $w_s = 0.02$~m/rad penalizes frame-to-frame coordinate difference (note: this is a coordinate step penalty, not velocity without division by $\Delta t$), and $w_{\text{rom}} = 3.0$~m/rad penalizes joint range violations:
\begin{equation}
    r_{\text{rom}, j}(\mathbf{q}) = \max\left(0, A_j(\mathbf{q}) - A_{j,\max}\right) + \max\left(0, A_{j,\min} - A_j(\mathbf{q})\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Joint Angle and Standoff Identifiability Limits}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Lead Elbow Flexion Identifiability:} Fitting unconstrained skin markers yields an unphysiological lead elbow flexion of $51.8^\circ$--$65.9^\circ$. When marker offsets are calibrated against an unconstrained fit, they absorb marker error; enforcing an anatomical ROM penalty thereafter causes severe conflict, inflating tracking RMS from 23.3~mm to 43.2~mm. Offsets must be calibrated under the constrained prior.
    \item \textbf{Hand-on-Grip Standoff Identifiability:} Total hand separation across the shaft ($72$--$74$~mm) is identifiable; however, the lateral shift of the shaft relative to the hands is unidentifiable because sideways shaft movement is absorbed by clubhead marker offsets. The standoff is partitioned symmetrically ($1.46$~in on each side).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Control Architecture: Feedforward, Feedback, and Balance}

\subsection{Upper-Body Feedforward and PD Tracking}
Upper-body actuation operates under \texttt{UpperBodyTracking} with an unactuated 6-DOF pelvis (\texttt{ModelingMode} 0):
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{upper}}(t) = \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{ff}}(t) + \mathbf{K}_p (\mathbf{q}_{\text{ref}}(t) - \mathbf{q}(t)) + \mathbf{K}_d (\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{\text{ref}}(t) - \dot{\mathbf{q}}(t)).
\end{equation}
In \texttt{gs3dx\_track\_gains.m}, gains are critically damped ($\zeta = 1.0$) at natural frequency $\omega_n = 2\pi \times 6.0$~rad/s based on reflected segment inertia $I_j$ (in kg$\cdot$m$^2$). The continuous radian gains are converted to Simscape degree units by multiplying by $\pi / 180$:
\begin{equation}
    K_{p, j} = I_j \omega_n^2 \left(\frac{\pi}{180}\right), \quad K_{d, j} = 2 \zeta I_j \omega_n \left(\frac{\pi}{180}\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Iterative Learning Control (ILC) Formulation}
Feedforward torques $\mathbf{F}(t)$ are updated via a filtered P-D learning law:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{F}^{(k+1)}(t) = \mathcal{Q}\left(\mathbf{F}^{(k)}(t) + \gamma \cdot \left(\mathbf{K}_p \mathbf{e}^{(k)}(t) + \mathbf{K}_d \dot{\mathbf{e}}^{(k)}(t)\right)\right),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{Q}$ is a zero-phase 4th-order Butterworth low-pass filter (cutoff 12~Hz), $\gamma$ is the learning gain (default 1.0), $\mathbf{e}^{(k)} = \mathbf{q}_{\text{ref}} - \mathbf{q}^{(k)}$, and $\dot{\mathbf{e}}^{(k)} = \dot{\mathbf{q}}_{\text{ref}} - \dot{\mathbf{q}}^{(k)}$.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Convergence Caveat:} Because $\mathcal{Q}$ attenuates high-frequency content, an ILC fixed point $\mathbf{F}^* = \mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{F}^* + \gamma \mathbf{u}_{\text{pd}}^*)$ does \textbf{not} imply that PD torque vanishes. In historical tests, Iteration 2 exhibited the lowest observed PD torque ($10.3$~N$\cdot$m RMS), but is not a proven global optimum. Later iterations exhibited growing PD torque (14.7~N$\cdot$m at iteration 3; 20.7~N$\cdot$m at iteration 4).
\end{quote}

\subsection{Leg Servo and Damped Inverse-Jacobian Balance Feedback}
The lower body is actuated by twelve joint coordinates $\mathbf{q}_{\text{leg}} \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$. The balance gain $\mathbf{G}(t)$ is computed in \texttt{gs3dx\_balance\_gain.m} from the per-foot $6 \times 6$ foot pose Jacobian $\mathbf{J}$ (position in m, rotation vector in rad scaled by $\text{lever\_m} = 0.5$~m), using central finite differences in degrees ($h = 10^{-3}$ deg). Damped least-squares inversion with damping $\lambda = 0.002$~m/deg yields:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{G}_{\text{leg}}(t) = -\mathbf{J}^T \left(\mathbf{J} \mathbf{J}^T + \lambda^2 \mathbf{I}\right)^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{I}_D \\ \mathbf{0}_{6-D, D} \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $D = 3$ (or 2 for horizontal axes). In \texttt{gs3dx\_balance\_command.m}, the commanded torque vector is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{cmd} = \mathbf{C}_0 + \mathbf{K}_p \odot \left(\mathbf{A}(t) + \mathbf{G}(t) \mathbf{s} + \mathbf{foot}\right) + \mathbf{K}_d \odot \left(\mathbf{R}(t) + \mathbf{G}(t) \mathbf{r}_s\right),
\end{equation}
with shift $\mathbf{s} = \text{sat}_{\text{limit}}(-k_p \mathbf{e} - k_d \dot{\mathbf{e}})$, separately commanded rate $\mathbf{r}_s = -k_p \dot{\mathbf{e}}$, and separate foot displacement. This rate is not the exact derivative of the saturated shift: it omits acceleration and saturation derivatives. The implementation uses this approximation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{foot}_{\text{leg}} = \mathbf{G}_{\text{leg}}(t) \cdot \text{sat}_{\text{limit}}\left(k_f \mathbf{e}_{\text{foot}}\right).
\end{equation}
Builder defaults are $k_p = 3.0$, $k_d = 0.4$, $k_f = 1.0$, and $\text{limit} = 0.10$~m.

Here $\mathbf{C}_0$ is either a constant twelve-axis feedforward torque
vector or its sampled profile (N$\cdot$m). $\mathbf{A}(t)$ and
$\mathbf{R}(t)$ are twelve-axis leg reference angles and rates in
degrees and degrees/s. $\mathbf{e}$ is measured minus reference
whole-body COM position in the selected World axes (m), and its
rate uses the corresponding COM rate references (m/s).
$\mathbf{e}_{\rm foot}$ is measured minus reference World ankle
position, not contact clearance. The shift gains $k_p$ and $k_f$
are dimensionless, while $k_d$ has units seconds.
$\mathbf{G}$ has units deg/m, so angle offsets are in degrees
and rate offsets in degrees/s. The degree-adapted servo gains
$\mathbf{K}_p$ and $\mathbf{K}_d$ have units N$\cdot$m/deg
and N$\cdot$m$\cdot$s/deg. The command above is the reference
and feedforward part of the servo; the implemented net leg torque is
$\mathbf{u}_{\rm leg}=\mathbf{cmd}-\mathbf{K}_p\odot\mathbf{q}_{\rm leg}-\mathbf{K}_d\odot\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{\rm leg}$.
This distinction is required when auditing sensed actuator torque and power.

\section{Theoretical Caveats: Nullspaces and Under-Actuation}

\subsection{Closed-Loop Internal Force Nullspace Hypothesis}
Let $\mathbf{J}_c \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times n}$ denote the constraint Jacobian governing the grip closure. Internal constraint torques satisfy:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{int}} = \mathbf{J}_c^T \boldsymbol{\lambda} \in \text{range}(\mathbf{J}_c^T).
\end{equation}
Let $\mathbf{Z}$ denote a matrix whose columns span $\text{null}(\mathbf{J}_c)$ (so $\mathbf{J}_c \mathbf{Z} = \mathbf{0}$). For any constraint-compatible generalized velocity $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{Z} \boldsymbol{\nu}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{Z}^T \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{int}} = \mathbf{Z}^T \mathbf{J}_c^T \boldsymbol{\lambda} = (\mathbf{J}_c \mathbf{Z})^T \boldsymbol{\lambda} = \mathbf{0}.
\end{equation}
For quaternion configurations, $\dot{\mathbf{q}}=\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{q})\mathbf{v}$; configuration and velocity dimensions may differ. The constraint Jacobian acts on velocity. Full multibody dynamics include actuation $\mathbf{B} \mathbf{u}$ and ground contact wrenches:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{M}(\mathbf{q}) \dot{\mathbf{v}} + \mathbf{h}(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{v}) = \mathbf{B} \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{J}_c^T \boldsymbol{\lambda} + \mathbf{J}_{\text{contact}}^T \mathbf{w}_{\text{contact}}.
\end{equation}
To generate a selected internal torque using commanded actuators alone, that
torque must lie in the actuated image $\text{range}(\mathbf{B})$. Passive
constraint reactions are not independent control inputs.
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Theoretical Note:} The explanation of torque drift as accumulation within closed-loop kinematic nullspaces represents a \textbf{plausible scientific hypothesis}, supported by simulation observations, but is \textbf{not an established mathematical proof}.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Rank-Deficiency vs. Global Dynamic Infeasibility}
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Mathematical Caveat:} The instantaneous \textbf{rank-deficiency of contact wrench or kinematic Jacobian matrices does not prove the global infeasibility} of the forward-dynamics swing matching problem.
\end{quote}
Local Jacobian rank-deficiency demonstrates the limitation of static linear inversion schemes, but does not establish global dynamic infeasibility. Conversely, unproven claims of Lie algebraic nonholonomic controllability must be avoided without rigorous proof.

\section{Contact Wrench Mechanics, Support Hull, and Ground Reactions}

\subsection{Planar Support Hull and Point-Force Contact Diagnostic}
The point-force diagnostic in \texttt{gs3dx\_contact\_wrench.m} projects demanded center of mass accelerations onto the planar foot support hull under assumed Coulomb friction $\mu = 0.8$. Dynamic equilibrium equations are:
\begin{align}
    \sum_{i} \mathbf{f}_i + M \mathbf{g} &= M \mathbf{a}_{\text{COM}}, \\
    \sum_{i} (\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{r}_{\text{COM}}) \times \mathbf{f}_i + \sum_{i} \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{contact}, i} &= \dot{\mathbf{h}}_{\text{COM}}.
\end{align}
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Diagnostic Scope:} The frozen residuals (\textbf{21.863~N$\cdot$m RMS through impact; 81.427~N$\cdot$m at impact}) represent a \textbf{point-force diagnostic on a planar support polygon}, omitting ground reaction yaw couples and full 3D segment angular momentum rates. Passing this diagnostic does \textbf{not} establish complete dynamic admissibility.
\end{quote}
Contact normal force formulations in Simscape utilize internal smoothing; formulas $F_n = \max(0, -K_c z - C_c \dot{z})$ are schematic linear approximations.

\subsection{Measured Ground Reactions vs. Kinematic GRF Estimates}
\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Kinematic GRF Estimates:} Total GRF is estimated from segment accelerations:

    \end{equation}
    At address, $\mathbf{F}_{\text{kin\_GRF}} = 1.001$~BW. Prior to impact, filtered peak is $1.24$--$1.33$~BW (raw peak $1.853$~BW at $-55.6$~ms). Club acceleration contributes $+0.57$~BW. Contact compliance parameters remain uncalibrated assumptions.

\section{Refined Human Matching: Orientation and Identifiability}
The former Fit pose solver assumed 33 independent coordinates; the Human
model has additional neck coordinates. Joint roles must therefore be resolved
from unique native block paths and validated joint schemas. A graphics
replacement cannot substitute for a compatible kinematic model. The refined
exporter selects Human explicitly, fits its longitudinal ellipsoid dimensions
to the capture, and records the exact solve and rendering dependency hashes.

limitations. Native model tests are required in addition to pure helper tests.

The 14 position targets do not determine every segment orientation. In
particular, ankle position alone leaves foot rotation ambiguous; a forward
axis alone also leaves roll ambiguous. Raw ankle-to-toe direction includes
the height difference between markers and is not the shoe's native forward
axis. Let $a_f$ denote ankle position and $t_{i,f},t_{o,f}$ the toe markers.
Construct the proper marker triad
\[
 x_f=\frac{(t_{i,f}+t_{o,f})/2-a_f}{\|(t_{i,f}+t_{o,f})/2-a_f\|},\quad
 z_f=\frac{x_f\times(t_{i,f}-t_{o,f})}{\|x_f\times(t_{i,f}-t_{o,f})\|},\quad
 F_f=[x_f,\ z_f\times x_f,\ z_f].
\]
For explicitly measured address frames, obtain the proper polar mean
$F_0=U\operatorname{diag}(1,1,\det(UV^T))V^T$ from the SVD of their triad sum.
The target shoe orientation is
\[
 R_{t,f}=F_fF_0^TR_z(\psi_0).
\]
Here horizontal address yaw $\psi_0$ comes from the capture. A horizontal sole
at address is an explicit calibration assumption, not force or contact
evidence. Missing address calibration, degenerate triads and unmeasured
markers must fail closed or remain explicitly masked; interpolation must not
be described as measured orientation.

For each foot, append the nine-component residual
\[
 r_{R,f}=w_R\operatorname{vec}(R_{p,f}-R_{t,f})/\sqrt{2},\qquad
 \|r_{R,f}\|=2w_R\sin(\theta_f/2).
\]
The two feet contribute 18 residual components. The weight has units of
metres per normalized orientation error, so it is a modeling tradeoff with
the position residuals; it is not an anatomical accuracy threshold.
Backward yaw and an inverted sole both receive a nonzero penalty.
The reported 14-target position RMS excludes this orientation term, and
angular errors must be reported separately. Full foot orientation does not
resolve missing thorax, pelvis or head orientation observations.

\subsection{Robust Q-Filtered Upper-Body Iterative Learning and Configured Model Lifecycle}
\label{subsec:upper_body_iterative_learning}

Upper-body feedforward torques are identified via iterative learning control (ILC)
on the contact-supported model using \texttt{gs3dx\_track\_learn.m} and \texttt{gs3dx\_track\_state.m}.
At iteration $k$, the next feedforward profile $F_{k+1}$ across the 12 tracked upper joints is:
\begin{align}
  u_{\mathrm{PD},k}(t) &= K_p \odot \bigl(A(t) - q_k(t)\bigr) + K_d \odot \bigl(R(t) - \dot{q}_k(t)\bigr), \label{eq:ilc_pd_torque} \\
  F_{k+1}(t) &= \mathcal{Q}\Bigl(F_k(t) + \alpha \, u_{\mathrm{PD},k}(t)\Bigr), \label{eq:ilc_q_update}
\end{align}
where joint angles $A, q$ are in degrees ($\mathrm{deg}$), rates $R, \dot{q}$ in $\mathrm{deg/s}$, gains $K_p \ge 0$ in $\mathrm{N\cdot m/deg}$, $K_d \ge 0$ in $\mathrm{N\cdot m/(deg/s)}$, and feedforward torques $F, u_{\mathrm{PD}}$ in $\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. The scalar learning gain is $\alpha > 0$. Filtering the full updated signal rather than the increment alone ($\mathcal{Q}$-filtering via zero-phase 4th-order forward-backward \texttt{filtfilt}) attenuates high-frequency content in the full updated profile; it is not a brick-wall frequency cutoff.

Preflight contract verification requires:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Timing and Filter Bounds:} $T$ must be a finite, strictly monotonic, uniformly spaced 1D vector. The selected reference interval $T(\mathrm{use})$ must satisfy $N_{\mathrm{samples}} > 12$ for filter padding, and cutoff frequency must obey $0 < f_c < f_s / 2$ with $f_s = 1/\Delta t$.
  \item \textbf{Signal Dimensions:} For joint prefix $P$ with $n_{\mathrm{axes}} \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, reference arrays $A, R, F$ must be $[n_{\mathrm{axes}} \times N_T]$ with $N_T=\operatorname{numel}(T)$, and gains $K_p, K_d$ must be $[n_{\mathrm{axes}} \times 1]$. Tracking enable requires $\mathtt{UpperBodyTracking} == 1$.
  \item \textbf{Model Lifecycle Modes:} \texttt{initialization="legacy"} manages historical \texttt{GS3DX\_FitTrack}, applying \texttt{gs3dx\_drive}, zeroing workspace names containing \texttt{StartVelocity}, and overriding \texttt{TrackStart}. Conversely, \texttt{initialization="configured"} requires an explicit already-loaded model, such as \texttt{GS3DX\_Human}, preserving caller model workspace, dirty state, and pre-fitted geometry without drive or velocity overwrites.
  \item \textbf{Best-Used Semantics:} Returned \texttt{out.feedforward} is the profile $F_k$ that was \emph{actually simulated} in the iteration achieving minimal RMS PD torque, not the subsequent updated profile $F_{k+1}$.
  \item \textbf{Initial Sample Provenance:} \texttt{out.initial\_joint\_states} records state sampled at the first reference sample $T(\mathrm{use}_1)$; this does not imply physical $t=0$ if reference start time is non-zero.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Problem and Observability}
The hand-to-club chain leaves club roll to the fitted wrist coordinates. The
marker-only fit placed the three clubhead markers well, but it did not
constrain rotation about the shaft. The rendered face was therefore 29.5 deg
(driver) and 17.9 deg (7-iron) open at impact, while the capture head triad
was 8.8 and 8.4 deg open at its own impact. The triad
(\texttt{Marker\_2:2:1..3}) is attached to the spec frame \texttt{Clubhead}.
Its calibrated offsets $p_j$ define a capture rotation $R_{c,i}$ per frame by
a Kabsch fit of $p_j$ onto the observed positions. Frames with a missing or
degenerate triad (driver 36 of 654, iron 17 of 657) are unobserved and get no
target. They are never filled.

\subsection{Cost Term}
Let $a$ be the rendered face normal expressed in the \texttt{Clubhead} frame:
$a=R_F^{\top}\,c$, where $c$ is \texttt{clubface\_vector} of the club
assembly (including the address-square roll constant) and $R_F$ is the
frame's placement on the club body. Then for each observed frame $i$ the
Levenberg--Marquardt IK adds three residual components
\[
 r_{\mathrm{face},i}=\sqrt{w_f}\,\bigl(R_i(q)\,a-R_{c,i}\,a\bigr),
\]
where $R_i(q)$ is the model rotation of \texttt{Clubhead}. The cost is
$w_f\lVert R_i a-R_{c,i}a\rVert^2$, the squared chord between the model and
capture normals. Marker residuals are unit-weight in metres, so a 5 deg face
error (chord 0.087) costs as much as an 8.7 cm error on each of the three
head markers when $w_f=3$. The term uses the existing per-frame
\texttt{axis\_targets} mechanism of \texttt{BaseFullBodyIK} (the same mechanism
used for the elbow-pit targets), so every IK provider honours it without
engine-specific code. The residual applies in the full-capture IK, in the
consistency re-solve, and in the optional ZMP-filter and shooting re-solves.
\texttt{--face-weight 0} restores the marker-only fit.

\subsection{Impact and Top Definitions}
At about 2400 deg/s of face closure near impact, one 360 Hz capture sample
spans about 7 deg. A frame index is therefore too coarse. Impact is the
sub-sample ball passage (\texttt{club\_face.ball\_passage}). After the top of
the backswing, the face-centre path is taken as piecewise linear. Impact is
its closest approach to the address face centre, within 0.15 m and within
0.10 m of the address height. The face angle there blends the two
neighbouring normals (\texttt{face\_angle\_at}). The top is the slowest
face-centre sample between first motion and peak speed. Each swing is
measured on its own clock. The 1 kHz rollout lags the reference by a few
milliseconds, about 6 deg of face rotation at a fixed time. Comparing at a
common time would mistake that lag for a face error.

\section{Dynamics-Informed Motion Estimation (DIME): Shared Reports, GUI Strategy Selection and Methods}
\label{sec:dime_reports_methods}

This section specifies the cross-engine reporting, strategy selection, and analytical output contracts formalized under DIME-11 (\#11432) of epic \#11421. The estimator mathematics itself is now maintained in the separate research record \texttt{docs/research/model\_aware\_matching/model\_aware\_matching.tex} (MOSAIC), which also records the 2026-10-05 audit of the DIME implementation: the merged children delivered contracts and reporting, while the coupled estimator, learned initialisers and benchmark numbers remained placeholders. No DIME result qualifies any capture in this document.

\subsection{Strategy Selection and Explicit Graceful Unavailability}
Estimator strategy selection coordinates across inverse kinematics, inverse dynamics, forward dynamics, moving horizon estimation (DIME MHE), continuous replay, and experimental learning-based estimators. Capability states are reported truthfully separating method existence from qualification:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Status}(\mathcal{S}) \in \{\mathtt{implemented}, \mathtt{qualified}, \mathtt{unavailable}\}
\end{equation}
Unqualified engines cannot be offered or reported as validated; any attempt to request or claim validation on an unqualified engine fails closed with a typed precondition error. Experimental strategies, including neural estimators, remain explicitly labeled as unavailable with transparent diagnostic justifications pending dedicated comparative studies. As of the audit no provider in the repository reports \texttt{qualified}.

\subsection{Governing Dynamics, Counterfactual Decomposition and Units}
The constrained multibody equations of motion satisfy:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{q}) \dot{\mathbf{v}} + \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{v})\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{q}) = \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{J}_c^T \boldsymbol{\lambda}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{q}$ is the generalized configuration (a manifold when spherical joints or a floating base are present), $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_v}$ is the tangent velocity, $\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{q})$ is the mass matrix, $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}$ are Coriolis and centrifugal effects, $\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{q})$ are gravitational generalized forces, $\mathbf{B}$ is the actuator selection map, $\mathbf{u}$ are net controls, and $\mathbf{J}_c^T \boldsymbol{\lambda}$ are the contact reactions. The left-hand side is linear in the stacked inertial parameters, $\mathbf{M}\dot{\mathbf{v}} + \mathbf{C}\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{g} = \mathbf{Y}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{v},\dot{\mathbf{v}})\,\boldsymbol{\pi}$, which the MOSAIC record exploits.

The pointwise counterfactual accelerations are defined with the reactions recomputed from the same constraint equations under the counterfactual input:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{ZTCF}}(t) &= \mathbf{M}(\mathbf{q})^{-1} \left( -\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v} - \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{q}) + \mathbf{J}_c^T \boldsymbol{\lambda}_0 \right), \qquad \boldsymbol{\lambda}_0 = \boldsymbol{\lambda}\big|_{\mathbf{u}=0}, \\[4pt]
\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{ZVCF}}(t) &= \mathbf{M}(\mathbf{q})^{-1} \left( - \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{q}) + \mathbf{J}_c^T \boldsymbol{\lambda}_{00} \right), \qquad \boldsymbol{\lambda}_{00} = \boldsymbol{\lambda}\big|_{\mathbf{u}=0,\,\mathbf{v}=0},
\end{align}
so that the measured acceleration decomposes exactly as $\dot{\mathbf{v}} = \mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{ZTCF}} + \mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{J}_c^T(\boldsymbol{\lambda}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}_0)$. In free flight $\boldsymbol{\lambda}_0 = \boldsymbol{\lambda}_{00} = 0$. The ZTCF is the drift evaluated pointwise along the measured state; the \emph{ZTCF trajectory} is the distinct integral curve $\dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})$ started from $\mathbf{x}(t_0)$ and following its own flow, and the two must not be conflated. ZVCF means zero velocity \emph{and} zero input at each instant; the control-preserved zero-velocity acceleration is a different quantity and is never abbreviated ZVCF. A declared feedback servo is part of $\mathbf{u}$ and is removed by the ZTCF; declared passive joint impedance is part of the drift.

Generalized accelerations are mixed-unit vectors ($\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ on revolute coordinates, $\mathrm{m/s^2}$ on translational coordinates) and are reported per coordinate; no norm is taken across mixed units. All reported physical quantities adhere strictly to canonical SI units: length in metres ($\mathrm{m}$), angle in radians ($\mathrm{rad}$), time in seconds ($\mathrm{s}$), force in newtons ($\mathrm{N}$), and torque in newton-metres ($\mathrm{N\cdot m}$). Floating-base root coordinates (degrees of freedom 0 to 5) have strictly zero artificial actuator forces; their rows of the equations of motion carry the input-free information about the inertial parameters.

\subsection{Ground Reaction Force Provenance and Typed Missingness}
Ground reaction forces (GRF) maintain strict provenance tracking distinguishing directly measured, physically inferred, regularized, and unavailable data. In particular, missing native GRF is represented with typed unavailable missingness and is never silently rendered or substituted as numerical zeros.

\subsection{Visual Overlays and Geometry Model}
Visual overlays employ refined ellipsoid geometry (\texttt{GS3DX\_Human\_Refined\_Ellipsoid}) with rigorous timestamp and frame-index synchronization. Any timing drift or count discrepancy between video frames and estimation kinematics fails closed.

\subsection{Limitations and Interpretation Boundaries}
Kinematic agreement in inverse kinematics fitting does not demonstrate forward dynamic validity, neuromuscular plausibility, or physical ground support equilibrium. Reports must maintain clear distinctions between kinematic fits, inverse-dynamics effort attributions, and certified continuous forward simulations.

\section{Phase-Split Forward-Dynamics Marker RMS (\#12042)}
\label{sec:fd_phase_split}

\subsection{Motivation}
The whole-swing FD marker RMS combines two regimes. Before impact, the
tracked reference is stable. After impact, the frame-by-frame IK can settle
on different, nearly equal solutions in weakly observed coordinates: the
metatarsophalangeal toes, shoulder and forearm axial rotation, and right hip
rotation. That choice of branch moves the whole-swing FD by several
millimetres with no change to the swing itself. Matched-swing receipts
therefore also report the two phases separately. This is reporting only: no
gate, threshold or acceptance status reads the new values.

\subsection{Definition}
Let $e_{f,m}$ be the FD marker position error magnitude (m) at frame $f$ for
marker $m$, $v_{f,m}$ the marker validity mask, and $t_I$ the impact time. Then
\[
  \mathrm{RMS}_{\le I} = \sqrt{\operatorname{mean}\{e_{f,m}^2 : t_f \le t_I,\ v_{f,m}\}},
  \qquad
  \mathrm{RMS}_{> I} = \sqrt{\operatorname{mean}\{e_{f,m}^2 : t_f > t_I,\ v_{f,m}\}}.
\]
These are recorded as \texttt{dynamics.fd\_phase.fd\_rms\_address\_to\_impact\_m}
and \texttt{fd\_rms\_after\_impact\_m}, with the frame count of each phase. The
ledger carries them as \texttt{fd\_address\_to\_impact\_rmse\_m} and
\texttt{fd\_after\_impact\_rmse\_m}. A phase with no valid sample reports
\texttt{null}, never zero.

\subsection{Impact Rule}
No new impact detector is added. $t_I$ is the shared sub-sample ball passage
from GCV-14 (\texttt{club\_face.ball\_passage}; see the Impact and Top
Definitions subsection of the OSV-10 section), applied to the
\texttt{Clubhead} frame origin along the reference trajectory $q_\mathrm{ref}$.
One guard applies. Before the detected impact, the clubhead must have risen
by at least half of its full height range above address. When the reference
clubhead path is discontinuous, a branch jump can corrupt the top-of-backswing
search, and the detector can then accept a sample next to address. The block
then reads \texttt{status: unavailable} with a reason, and no value is
reported. One exploratory slice-7 run (capture-A, split weights 0.3/1) hit
this guard: its reference clubhead jumps at about 1.81 s, and the detector
returned 0.086 s.

\subsection{Canonical Values}
The canonical calibrated runs were regenerated with the documented commands
in \texttt{evidence/ground\_support/CANONICAL\_RUN.md}. Whole-swing FD, IK and
acceptance status are byte-identical to the receipts regenerated for the hip

\section{Thorax and Shoulder-Girdle Turn Split (Issue \#12042, Slice 7)}
\label{sec:turn_split}

Status: \emph{measured, not adopted}. The MuJoCo lane put the shoulder girdle's scapular share of the turn into the thorax: at the top of capture-A the upper trunk of the consistency-resolved reference $q_{\mathrm{ref}}$ over-turned by $+18.1^\circ$ while the shoulder girdle was $+9.3^\circ$ and the scapular protraction coordinates sat near $-2^\circ$ to $-6^\circ$. Acceptance for this slice is: the upper trunk and pelvis within $5^\circ$ of the markers at top and impact, and the shoulder girdle within $8^\circ$, on capture-A and capture-B, without making the inverse-kinematics (IK) marker RMS or the forward-dynamics (FD) replay worse. An IK change below meets the turn bounds on both captures, but every setting that met them made the FD replay worse. It is therefore committed as an opt-in option, off by default, and the canonical receipts are unchanged. All results are MuJoCo; this is turn measurement, not an engine qualification.

\subsection{Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Thorax yaw is weakly identified.} At the top of capture-A, changing the thorax yaw by $15^\circ$ (with the other coordinates re-solved) moves the whole-window marker RMS by under $1$~mm. The arm and club markers (16 of the tracked set) therefore decide the yaw of the thorax and the scapulae, and the two shoulder-girdle markers are outvoted.
\item \emph{The capture back triad is not rigid.} \texttt{BackLeft}--\texttt{BackTop} shortens from $209$ to $189$~mm at the top, so the rigid thorax cannot fit all three back markers exactly.
\item \emph{The scapula ranges do not bind.} \texttt{ScapInputY} (protraction, the yaw share) is limited to $\pm40^\circ$ and the solved value at the top is $-2^\circ$ to $-6^\circ$. Widening it to $\pm60^\circ$ changed no solution, so lever (b), the scapula ranges, was rejected and no range was widened.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Method}
Two terms are added to the trajectory, consistency, shooting and ZMP-filter re-solves (\texttt{pipeline/turn\_split.py}); the address calibration and segment scaling stay marker-only.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A thorax-orientation residual (lever (c)). Let $\mathbf{a}=\widehat{\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{BL}}-\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{BR}}}$ be the unit axis between the calibrated \texttt{BackLeft} and \texttt{BackRight} attachments in the \texttt{Spine} frame, and $\mathbf{d}(t)$ the unit capture direction \texttt{BackRight}$\to$\texttt{BackLeft}. The residual is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{thx}}(t)=\sqrt{w_{\mathrm{thx}}}\,\big(R_{\mathrm{Spine}}(q)\,\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d}(t)\big),
\end{equation}
dimensionless and added beside the metre-valued marker residuals, using the same axis-target interface as the club-face residual (OSV-10). Frames with a missing marker, or a capture line shorter than $0.05$~m, have no target.
\item Shoulder-girdle marker weights (lever (a)): \texttt{LShoulderBack} and \texttt{RShoulderBack} are weighted $w_{\mathrm{sg}}$ (all other markers $1$).
\end{enumerate}
The command-line options are \texttt{--thorax-weight} $w_{\mathrm{thx}}$ (default $0$) and \texttt{--shoulder-girdle-weight} $w_{\mathrm{sg}}$ (default $1$). The IK-passing setting is $w_{\mathrm{thx}}=0.3$, $w_{\mathrm{sg}}=5$; when either term is active the receipt carries an \texttt{ik.turn\_split} block.

\subsection{Follow-Through FD Diagnosis (Follow-Up)}
\label{sec:turn_split_fd}
This subsection resolves the first open item above: why the follow-through FD replay got worse with the split. The experiments were run on branch \texttt{claude/turn-split-impact-window} at commit \texttt{f81c1647b2}. That commit adds \texttt{--thorax-window}/\texttt{--shoulder-girdle-window} \{\texttt{full}, \texttt{impact}\}, \texttt{--split-taper-s} and per-frame marker weights. It is not merged, because no setting passed. The baselines are the canonical receipts, at FD $61.63$~mm (capture-A) and $46.97$~mm (capture-B).

\paragraph{Diagnosis.} The tracking controller is computed torque with an unactuated root and no actuator torque limits, so torque saturation cannot occur (zero saturated samples by construction). The peak joint torque after $1.35$~s was $681$~Nm without the split and $698$~Nm with it, both at the left hip flexion. On capture-A at $0.3/5$:
\begin{itemize}
\item The marker fit of $q_{\mathrm{ref}}$ after $1.35$~s is not worse ($52.5$~mm without and $52.2$~mm with the split), so the reference does not track the markers less well.
\item The FD increase is whole-body and largest at the club ($+41$~mm), left ankle ($+55$~mm) and knees ($+28$ to $+33$~mm). The actuated-joint tracking error stays near $1^\circ$--$1.5^\circ$ RMS. The error sits in the unactuated root: pelvis yaw $8.3^\circ\to10.6^\circ$ RMS and lateral root $10.5\to38.0$~mm RMS. Contact unloads (weight fraction reaches $0$) in both runs.
\item The reference itself differs where markers observe the model weakly. The toe (\texttt{mtp}) angles differ by up to $60^\circ$, shoulder axial rotation by $43^\circ$ and forearm rotation by $36^\circ$, from about $1.2$~s onwards. Changing the toe angles alters the foot contact geometry, which the root has to follow.
\end{itemize}
The trajectory IK solves frame by frame, warm-starting each frame from the previous solution with a prior toward it. In the follow-through the marker fit is about $55$~mm RMS and several near-equal solutions exist, so the branch it lands on depends on the solution path. Two observations confirm this:
\begin{enumerate}
\item With both split terms switched off from impact $+50$~ms onwards, the follow-through cost is identical to the baseline's. Yet $q_{\mathrm{ref}}$ there still differs by up to $20^\circ$--$60^\circ$ in the same coordinates.
\item A near-null perturbation ($w_{\mathrm{thx}}=0.01$, $w_{\mathrm{sg}}=1$) changes $q_{\mathrm{ref}}$ by at most $1.9^\circ$ before $1.0$~s, but by up to $39^\circ$ (A) and $46^\circ$ (B) after impact. FD moves from $61.6$ to $55.0$~mm on A (better) and from $47.0$ to $60.9$~mm on B (worse).
\end{enumerate}
The whole-swing FD RMS therefore has a sensitivity floor of roughly $\pm7$ to $\pm14$~mm, set by the IK branch chosen in the follow-through, not by the turn split or the controller gains. A $2$~mm FD criterion cannot be resolved against that floor.

A gain change could not be judged against a criterion below the $\pm7$--$14$~mm floor, so none was made.
