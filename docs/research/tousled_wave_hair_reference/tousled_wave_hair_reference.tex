\section{Purpose and Evidence}
This note defines the visual-only \texttt{tousled\_wave} and
\texttt{short\_curls} styles for the CC0 head's existing scalp shell. The
source-inspired targets are the short wavy hair visible in the contemporary
PGA TOUR portraits of Mac O'Grady and John Schlee. The 1984 PGA TOUR Guide
reproduces O'Grady's grayscale headshot on PDF page 126 (printed page 124);
the 1973 PGA TOUR Guide shows Schlee's grayscale portrait on PDF page 58.
These qualitative references do not provide calibrated head geometry, natural
hair color, or strand-level measurements.

\begin{itemize}
  \item O'Grady: \href{https://pgatourmedia.pgatourhq.com/static-assets/page/header/files/1984_pgatour.pdf}{1984 PGA TOUR Guide}
  \item Schlee: \href{https://pgatourmedia.pgatourhq.com/static-assets/page/header/files/1973_pgatour.pdf}{1973 PGA TOUR Guide}
\end{itemize}
The source images are retained as local research material only; neither image
is copied into this repository. Grayscale tone does not establish natural hair
color, and the headshots do not support a body-build inference.

\section{Coordinates and Inputs}
All length inputs are in head-length units, with the existing CC0 head's neck
plane at $z=0$ and vertex at $z=1$. In the canonical frame, $x$ points toward
the face, $y$ points to the subject's left, and $z$ points upward. The existing
closed scalp mesh supplies the vertices $\mathbf{p}_i$ and outward normals
$\mathbf{n}_i$. A style adds only a bounded shell around this surface.

Let $s\in\{-1,0,+1\}$ encode the hair part. The phase of the existing comb
ridges is
\begin{align}
 u(x,y) &= \frac{y-s\,q(x-x_c)}{\lambda_c},\\
 \theta(x) &= \frac{2\pi(x-x_c)}{\lambda_w},\\
 b(y) &= 0.18\sin\left(\frac{2\pi y}{\lambda_c}\right),\\
 \phi(x,y) &= 2\pi\left[u(x,y)+a_w\left(\sin(\theta+b)+0.20\sin(2\theta+0.7-b)\right)\right].
\end{align}
where $q$ controls ridge sweep and $b(y)$ varies ridge bending across the scalp. The style sets $a_w=0.42$ cycles and
$\lambda_w=0.26$ head lengths. The wave term bends the existing ridges across
the crown; it does not add disconnected locks, vertices, or a second shell.
For the other preset styles, $a_w=0$ and the phase-warp branch is skipped,
which preserves their previous vertex coordinates exactly. The short-curl
preset uses a smaller $a_w=0.12$ and a $0.18$ head-length bend wavelength.

The added thickness field is
\begin{equation}
 t_w(x,y,z)=A_c\,M_{\mathrm{crown}}(z)\cos(\phi(x,y)),
\end{equation}
with crown mask $M_{\mathrm{crown}}$ already used by the existing haircut
builder. The full thickness is the current base fade, fringe, and parting
field plus $t_w$. It is multiplied by the existing smooth hairline edge mask,
so added volume returns to zero at the cut boundary. The \texttt{tousled\_wave}
style-specific inputs are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Input & Value & Meaning \\
\midrule
$A_c$ & 0.015 & comb-ridge amplitude (head lengths) \\
$\lambda_c$ & 0.10 & ridge wavelength across the crown (head lengths) \\
$a_w$ & 0.42 & sinusoidal phase warp (cycles) \\
$\lambda_w$ & 0.26 & bend wavelength along the crown (head lengths) \\
$t_{\mathrm{top}}$ & 0.065 & top shell thickness (head lengths) \\
$t_{\mathrm{side}}$ & 0.018 & side shell thickness (head lengths) \\
$t_{\mathrm{fringe}}$ & 0.046 & soft front-hairline lift (head lengths) \\
front-edge wave & 0.006 & hairline-height amplitude (head lengths) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The base hairline control points retain a modest forehead and longer nape;
parting depth and heavy-side gain are zero. The hairline receives a bounded
0.006 head-length sinusoidal variation over the front arc; it is subtle and
does not change the closed rim. \texttt{tousled\_wave} remains nearly symmetric. Its top remains below $z=1.09$ in the unit-head geometry.

\section{Short Curls (Issue \#15928)}
The separate \texttt{short\_curls} preset gives Mac O'Grady and John Schlee a
shared stylized option with broad, dense curl relief. Its scalar field combines
two crossing pairs of gently warped wave trains:
\begin{align}
w_x &= x+0.018\sin\left(2\pi\frac{y}{0.17}\right)
 + 0.009\sin\left(2\pi\frac{x+y}{0.21}\right),\\
w_y &= y+0.016\sin\left(2\pi\frac{x}{0.19}\right)
 - 0.008\sin\left(2\pi\frac{x-y}{0.23}\right),\\
C(x,y) &= 0.70\cos\left(2\pi\frac{w_x}{0.115}+0.4\right)
 \cos\left(2\pi\frac{w_y}{0.13}-0.6\right)\\
&\quad + 0.30\cos\left(2\pi\frac{x+0.4y}{0.12}+0.9\right)
 \cos\left(2\pi\frac{y-0.35x}{0.11}-1.2\right),\\
t_{\mathrm{curl}}(x,y,z) &= A_{\mathrm{curl}}M_{\mathrm{crown}}(z)C(x,y).
\end{align}
The phase variation avoids parallel furrows and a circular swirl while
remaining smooth and bounded. The style sets $A_{\mathrm{curl}}=0.024$ head
lengths, the upper end of the accepted range
$0\leq A_{\mathrm{curl}}\leq0.024$. It uses $0.048$ top thickness,
$0.016$ side thickness, $0.038$ fringe thickness, and a $0.012$ front
hairline variation with a small secondary ripple. Two scalp refinement passes
make the relief visible at ordinary render sizes. With the crown field, the unit-head top remains below
$z=1.09$. Other presets use the exact zero default and skip the new relief
branch. A selected cap keeps the existing short-crop clearance path.

\section{Closed-Shell Construction and Invariants}
For each scalp point, the existing mesh builder evaluates the nonnegative
total thickness and offsets the outer surface along $\mathbf{n}_i$. It keeps
the hidden inner surface at the configured skin clearance and zips the shared
hairline rim to close the shell. The wave and curl fields change only scalar
thickness; they do not change the scalp cut, inner surface, face winding, or
rim index welding. The result must remain a finite, outward-wound, closed
manifold with positive signed volume.

When a cap is requested, the existing cap-specific path still builds the
legacy short crop and uses its cap-clearance geometry. The new style does not
alter that fallback. Appearance has no effect on the native body, joint,
contact, or dynamics arrays; the native MuJoCo regression compares those
arrays and forward-dynamics quantities across all named styles.

\section{Limits and Reproduction}
The portrait references motivate only the broad silhouette (fuller crown,
wavy ridges and short curls). This is not a reconstruction of any named
player's individual hair or a measured historical hairstyle. Colors remain an
artistic choice. To reproduce the checks and native previews from the
repository root, run:
\begin{verbatim}
python -m pytest tests/unit/model_appearance/test_hair_styles.py -q
python scripts/render_hair_styles.py --style short_curls --out /tmp/short-curls
\end{verbatim}
The native-model equality test is visual-only qualification: it verifies that
hair changes do not alter modeled dynamics. It is not a motion-fit or physical
validation of either golfer.
