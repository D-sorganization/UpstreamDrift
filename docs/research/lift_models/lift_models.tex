\begin{abstract}
Four per-engine model packs (\texttt{OpenSim\_Models}, \texttt{MuJoCo\_Models},
\texttt{Drake\_Models}, \texttt{Pinocchio\_Models}) each build full-body models
of the five classical barbell lifts --- back squat, deadlift, bench press,
snatch, and clean and jerk --- for a reference lifter with a competition
barbell. A cross-engine audit (epic \#11740, LIFT-1 \#11741) reduces each
pack's own forward kinematics to bar, hand and foot geometry and compares the
four packs pose by pose. This reference collects the exploratory modelling
material around that audit that is not itself the governed calculation:
the model inventory per pack, the phase and trajectory conventions and why a
shared standard remains open, what inverse-dynamics and spine-load analysis
exists today (Movement Optimizer, not the engine packs), the measured parity
numbers, the derived discrepancy list, and the documented negative results.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Document status.} This is an editable standalone research
source, separate from the engineering design manual. The sole editable
design-manual source remains \texttt{manuals/upstreamdrift} (QMD); the
governed calculation for this material is Chapter 15, ``Lift Model Pack
Audit'' (\texttt{manuals/upstreamdrift/chapters/15-lift-pack-audit.qmd}),
itself recorded there as evidence class ``derived, provisional'' and not yet
approved. This document is a software and model-consistency check, not
scientific validation against capture data: no lift motion-capture or
force-plate data exist for any of the five lifts, and no uncertainty against
a measured lift is claimed anywhere below.

\section{Purpose and Scope}
The four packs are parametric Python generators that build a
model from body mass, height and plate mass. The audit compares them on the
same canonical inputs: it checks masses and joint limits against a shared
biomechanics parity standard, reduces each engine's forward kinematics to
bar/hand/foot geometry, and derives a discrepancy list with tracking issues
(Chapter 15, ``Purpose''). What the audit explicitly does \emph{not} do is
restated here because it bounds every claim in this document: \emph{``It
measures software parity between the packs only. It does not simulate a
lift, and it does not compute joint moments, spine loads or muscle
forces.''} (Chapter 15, ``Purpose''). This reference does not re-derive the
audit's geometry equations (bar frame, hand-to-bar offsets, placement
independent pose summary, same-input parity, lifter-only centre of mass,
segment mass deviation, joint-limit comparison, grip width, start pose,
reference FK) --- those ten equations and their tolerances are Chapter 15's
governed content and are only cited here by name. The user-facing summary of
the same material lives in \texttt{docs/help/movement\_optimizer.md}
(``Lifting Models: Modelling Reference'' section), which this document
extends with the measured numbers and the open-gap bookkeeping.

\section{Model Inventory per Pack}

\subsection{Body Model}
All four packs scale a full-body model from \texttt{body\_mass} and
\texttt{height} to Winter (2009) anthropometrics, declared as
\texttt{anthropometrics: winter\_2009} in each pack's \texttt{model\_pack.yaml}.
OpenSim's is a 15-segment body (pelvis, torso, head, bilateral arms and legs)
with ball joints at hip/shoulder/lumbar, pin joints at knee/elbow/neck, and
custom 2-DOF joints at ankle/wrist
(\texttt{OpenSim\_Models/src/opensim\_models/shared/body/body\_model.py}).
Every pack gives the pelvis an unconstrained 6-DOF root to the world ---
OpenSim's \texttt{FreeJoint} \texttt{ground\_pelvis}, MuJoCo's pelvis
\texttt{freejoint}, Drake's \texttt{floating} pelvis joint (configurable to
\texttt{fixed}), and Pinocchio's root built with
\texttt{pin.JointModelFreeFlyer()} --- except where a lift welds the pelvis to
a bench (below). The reference lifter is 80\,kg and 1.78\,m
(docs/development/lifting/PACK\_PARITY\_BASELINE.md, ``Setup''; Chapter 15,
``Conventions''); the bar is a 20\,kg competition bar plus 50\,kg per side,
120\,kg total.

\subsection{Barbell and Plates}
Each pack models an Olympic barbell to IWF/IPF dimensions: a men's bar is
2.20\,m long, 1.31\,m between collars, 28\,mm shaft, 50\,mm sleeves, 20\,kg
unloaded; a women's bar is 2.01\,m, 25\,mm shaft, 15\,kg
(\texttt{OpenSim\_Models/src/opensim\_models/shared/barbell/barbell\_model.py},
\texttt{BarbellSpec}). The bar is three rigid bodies --- left sleeve, shaft,
right sleeve --- joined by welds, with plates added as sleeve mass via
\texttt{plate\_mass\_per\_side}. MuJoCo, Drake and Pinocchio each carry an
equivalent \texttt{BarbellSpec}/builder under their own \texttt{shared/barbell/}.
Bar and bench links are assumed to have zero mass-centre offset in every pack
at the recorded commits (Chapter 15, ``Assumptions and Limitations''); the lifter-only CoM
figure below depends on that assumption holding.

\subsection{Grip Interface}
The bar attaches to the hands with a rigid weld/fixed joint, not a compliant
contact, in every pack at this commit. OpenSim's \texttt{attach\_barbell\_to\_hands}
welds both hands to the shaft at a configurable \texttt{grip\_offset}
half-width; MuJoCo's \texttt{add\_weld\_constraint} does the same. Drake's
base builder welds the barbell to the left hand only (its own docstring calls
this ``SDF 1.8 kinematic-tree-safe'' for a single attachment point,
\texttt{Drake\_Models/src/drake\_models/exercises/base.py}); Pinocchio's
default \texttt{attach\_barbell} also welds via \texttt{add\_fixed\_joint} to
the left hand only. Measured attachment kinds per lift and pack are in the
``Hand and Bar Attachment'' table of
\texttt{docs/development/lifting/PACK\_PARITY\_BASELINE.md}; the right-hand
gap is in Section~\ref{sec:failed} below.

\subsection{Foot and Bench Contact}
OpenSim uses four Hunt-Crossley contact spheres per foot (heel/toe,
medial/lateral) against a ground half-space
(\texttt{OpenSim\_Models/src/opensim\_models/shared/body/foot\_contact.py}).
For the bench press it adds a near-zero-mass bench body welded to ground,
with the pelvis welded supine on it at the IPF-standard 0.43\,m bench height.
MuJoCo places a ground-plane geom in \texttt{worldbody} and uses its built-in
contact solver, with \texttt{<exclude>} pairs to suppress adjacent-segment
self-collision. Pinocchio defines contact frames and a Coulomb friction
coefficient for its own constraint-based contact --- a rectangular foot sole
(0.26\,m $\times$ 0.10\,m) with corner points --- rather than built-in
collision detection. Drake and Pinocchio expose no contact-force model at
all for the lift packs (Section~\ref{sec:gaps}, gap class \texttt{no\_grf}).
Contact-force parity should not be assumed from these geometry definitions
alone (\texttt{docs/help/movement\_optimizer.md}, ``Foot and Bench Contact'').

\subsection{Coordinate Frames and Units}
The audit's canonical frame is $+Z$ up, $+X$ forward, $+Y$ left, SI units.
MuJoCo, Drake and Pinocchio already use it; OpenSim is Y-up and its world
vectors are rotated into the canonical frame by a fixed rotation matrix
recorded in Chapter 15 (``Conventions''), which this document cites rather
than re-deriving. Positions are reported as body-frame origins in the world
frame, or pelvis-relative when comparing engines (Chapter 15, equation~4,
``placement-independent pose summary'').

Phase names differ per pack even where counts agree: MuJoCo's back-squat
phases are \texttt{descent\_start, half\_depth, bottom, drive, lockout};
OpenSim's are \texttt{start, descent, bottom, drive, lockout}; Drake's are
\texttt{unrack, bottom, lockout}; Pinocchio's are \texttt{start, descent,
bottom, drive, lockout}
(\texttt{docs/development/lifting/PACK\_PARITY\_BASELINE.md}, ``Phase names
per pack''). Pinocchio's phase targets are left-side only for every lift
(gap class \texttt{phase\_asymmetric}, Pinocchio\_Models\#449), and several
packs carry phase keys that the audit could not resolve to model coordinates
without guessing a side or coordinate (gap class \texttt{phase\_keys}:
OpenSim\_Models\#414, Drake\_Models\#390, Pinocchio\_Models\#436). MuJoCo's
phase values may be absolute joint positions or reference-relative angles;
the audit reads them as canonical angles, so MuJoCo phase out-of-range
findings are interpretation-dependent and are deliberately not used as gaps
(Chapter 15, ``Assumptions and Limitations'').

A shared phase standard across all four engines does not exist yet. LIFT-2
(\#11742) requires filing a \texttt{board-proposal} in
\texttt{Repository\_Management} first, for a single exercise specification
(anthropometric segment table, barbell spec, named phases with timing,
start/catch via-poses, root-joint policy, grip width/type per lift, foot
stance) owned in Tools and consumed by all four packs and by Movement
Optimizer (issue \#11742, ``Steps''). As of this revision that proposal is
tracked as board proposal \texttt{D-sorganization/Repository\_Management\#2060}
(Option A: extend the parity standard, schema
\texttt{biomech-parity-standard/v1}, to a v2.0 specification). The proposal
is open and awaits owner approval; no shared specification is implemented
before that decision.

\subsection{Additional Movements (LIFT-10, Issue \#11750)}
Five additional movements extend the baseline exercise repertoire: \texttt{split\_jerk},
\texttt{push\_press}, \texttt{overhead\_press}, \texttt{front\_squat}, and \texttt{lunge}.
Each movement defines an ordered sequence of named phases with strictly increasing
cycle fractions $t_{\text{frac}} \in [0, 1)$, canonical start/catch/lockout via-poses,
grip widths and ranges, barbell load parameters, and stance geometry:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
\item \textbf{Split Jerk:} 6 named phases (\texttt{dip}, \texttt{drive}, \texttt{split\_drop},
  \texttt{overhead\_catch}, \texttt{recovery\_front}, \texttt{lockout}). Features an asymmetric
  split-stance geometry with longitudinal displacement ($s_x = 0.75\,\text{m}$), lead foot forward
  and trail foot posterior with independent toe-out angles.
\item \textbf{Push Press:} 5 named phases (\texttt{setup}, \texttt{dip}, \texttt{drive},
  \texttt{press\_transition}, \texttt{lockout}) with bilateral symmetric stance and front-rack bar support.
\item \textbf{Overhead Press:} 4 named phases (\texttt{setup}, \texttt{press}, \texttt{head\_pass},
  \texttt{lockout}) enforcing strict vertical pressing without lower-limb drive.
\item \textbf{Front Squat:} 5 named phases (\texttt{standing}, \texttt{descent}, \texttt{bottom},
  \texttt{ascent}, \texttt{lockout}) with anterior deltoid bar shelf and wide bilateral stance ($0.42\,\text{m}$).
\item \textbf{Lunge:} 5 named phases (\texttt{standing}, \texttt{step\_descent}, \texttt{bottom},
  \texttt{drive\_recovery}, \texttt{lockout}) with asymmetric split-stance geometry ($s_x = 0.80\,\text{m}$),
  lead thigh horizontal, and trailing knee suspended.
\end{itemize}

\section{Inverse Dynamics, Spine Load and Muscle Redundancy}

\subsection{What Runs Today}
Three distinct things are easy to conflate here and are kept separate.
\emph{Inverse kinematics} against a physics-engine pack is not run: MuJoCo's
optimizer layer currently interpolates between named \texttt{Phase(name, t,
pose)} targets rather than solving IK, and switching to IK is an open,
unimplemented item (MuJoCo\_Models\#403). \emph{Feedback-assisted tracking} of
a lift controller is not run either: a controller API for the lift packs is
tracked as open (MuJoCo\_Models\#404) and no lift equivalent of the golf
same-input closed-loop controller (epic \#11605) exists. \emph{Independently
replayed open-loop dynamics} of a full lift is not run: the audit states
plainly that it ``does not simulate a lift'' (Chapter 15, ``Purpose''), and no
same-input bundle has been exported for any of the five lifts. All three are
therefore not run for the lift packs as of this revision --- not verified to
exist anywhere in the fleet for these models, and are reported here as absent
rather than guessed.

\subsection{Movement Optimizer}
Movement Optimizer (\texttt{Tools/src/movement\_optimizer}, vendored at
\texttt{src/shared/python/movement\_optimizer/}) is the one tool that produces
a full lift trajectory with inverse dynamics today, but on a different,
simpler body than the four packs above: a planar three-link shin/thigh/trunk
chain. Torques are recovered by Lagrangian inverse dynamics; the trajectory
itself is the result of multi-start parallel SLSQP under centre-of-mass
balance and joint-limit constraints, so \texttt{success} means the optimizer
converged and every hard constraint was satisfied, not that the trajectory is
globally optimal (\texttt{docs/help/movement\_optimizer.md}, ``Method'').
Exercises covered: \texttt{squat}, \texttt{full\_squat}, \texttt{deadlift},
\texttt{bench\_press}, \texttt{clean}, \texttt{jerk}, \texttt{snatch}. It is
explicitly not a physics-engine simulation and its results are not directly
comparable to the four engine packs' dashboards despite sharing a
\texttt{cross\_engine\_analysis} capability label
(\texttt{docs/help/movement\_optimizer.md}, ``Limitations'').

\subsection{Spine Load}
Movement Optimizer is the only source of a spine-load figure for the lifts.
\texttt{spine\_loads.py} computes the axial compression at the L5/S1 junction
as
\[
F_{\mathrm{comp}} = (m_{\mathrm{above}} + m_{\mathrm{bar}})\,g\,\cos(\theta_{\mathrm{torso}})
  + m_{\mathrm{torso}}\,\ddot\theta_{\mathrm{torso}}\,d_{\mathrm{torso}},
\]
with the L5/S1 joint placed at the base of the torso segment (the hip joint
in this three-link model), $m_{\mathrm{above}}$ the mass above L5/S1, and the
inertial term from the torso's own angular acceleration and its
centre-of-mass offset $d_{\mathrm{torso}}$
(\texttt{src/shared/python/movement\_optimizer/spine\_loads.py},
\texttt{spinal\_compression}). $m_{\mathrm{above}}$ depends on the exercise
family: for \texttt{squat}/\texttt{full\_squat} the torso segment already
includes the arms and \texttt{\_mass\_above\_l5} reads \texttt{body.m\_squat[2]};
for the deadlift family the torso is trunk plus head only and it reads
\texttt{body.m\_deadlift[2]}. The result is reported against
\texttt{NIOSH\_COMPRESSION\_LIMIT = 3400.0}\,N, the NIOSH recommended
occupational-lifting compression limit, which is an occupational-lifting
figure and not a clinical injury threshold for barbell training
(\texttt{spine\_loads.py}; \texttt{docs/help/movement\_optimizer.md},
``Limitations''). Only axial compression and anterior-posterior shear at one
level (L5/S1) are modelled; there is no lateral shear, no axial torsion, and
no other vertebral level. None of the four engine packs compute a spine load
at all.

\subsection{Muscle Redundancy}
Open, and depends on LIFT-6 (\#11746). None of the four engine packs
defines muscle actuators: the search recorded in
\texttt{docs/help/movement\_optimizer.md} (``What the Optimizer Computes'')
found no \texttt{Muscle}, \texttt{Thelen},
\texttt{Millard} or actuator classes in the OpenSim pack, consistent with the
epic's note that ``muscle content in the OpenSim pack is unverified'' (issue
\#11740). Movement Optimizer's own Hill-type muscle model
supplies torque-angle-velocity capacity and sticking-point detection
(\texttt{strength.py}) for its single three-link chain only; it does not
distribute load across named muscles and is not a muscle-redundancy solver
for a full-body musculoskeletal model.

\subsection{OpenSim Full-Body Musculoskeletal Lifts (LIFT-6, Issue \#11746)}
\paragraph{Status.} Only the model composition is built (OSL-2, \#13040).
Until OSL-2 the composer created an empty \texttt{opensim.Model()} and returned
a hard-coded joint list with \texttt{barbell\_attached=True}; that stub is
replaced. OSL-3 (\#13041) adds OpenSim inverse kinematics and inverse
dynamics of the back-squat same-input bundle (next subsection). No static
optimization, CMC or Moco solve runs yet:
\texttt{evaluate\_opensim\_lift\_kinetics} still reports
\texttt{"unqualified"} on every path (OSL-4, \#13042).

\paragraph{Model Topology.} \texttt{compose\_opensim\_full\_body\_lift}
(\texttt{src/shared/python/lifting/opensim\_full\_body\_lifts.py}) calls
\texttt{build\_lift\_model}
(\texttt{src/engines/physics\_engines/opensim/python/msk\_lift\_model.py}) for
the five barbell lifts. The body is the muscle-bearing
\texttt{RajagopalLaiUhlrich2023.osim} (22 bodies, 22 joints, two knee coupler
constraints, 80 Millard lower-limb muscles and 17 upper-body
\texttt{CoordinateActuator} stand-ins), resolved at run time from the
\texttt{opensim-org/opensim-models} submodule or
\texttt{\$UPSTREAMDRIFT\_MSK\_BASE\_MODEL} and never copied into the
repository. The receipt records its path and SHA-256. The model is scaled
uniformly by $s = h / 1.70\,\mathrm{m}$ (the nominal stature of the generic
Rajagopal et al.\ (2016) adult) and to the spec body mass with
\texttt{Model.scale} (mass distribution preserved). A single
\texttt{barbell} body of the spec mass carries the shared
\texttt{Barbell} inertia (axial: the shaft only; transverse: rod plus plates
as thin discs). The LIFT-4 hand interface holds it: a
\texttt{WeldJoint hand\_l\_to\_barbell} on the left and a
\texttt{WeldConstraint hand\_r\_to\_barbell} on the right, both at the hands'
centres of mass (the palm-centre grip of the parity standard). Each foot has
three \texttt{SmoothSphereHalfSpaceForce} spheres (heel, forefoot, toe) with
the MS-20 calibrated damping and friction ($c = 0.5$\,s/m,
$\mu_s = 0.6$, $\mu_d = 0.4$) against a floor half-space; the stiffness
was the MS-20 $10^5$ until OSL-12 raised it to $10^7$ (see the OSL-12
subsection). The bench press
adds a bench-top half-space paired only with four back spheres at the
posterior shoulder and posterior iliac markers.

\paragraph{Hold Pose (Assumption).} One static pose per lift that the
Rajagopal joint ranges admit: squat, standing with the hands at the
upper-back bar position; deadlift, lockout; bench press, supine with the arms
extended; snatch and clean and jerk, the hang with each lift's grip. The
shoulder ($\texttt{arm\_flex} \le 90^\circ$, $\texttt{arm\_add} \ge
-120^\circ$) and elbow ($\le 150^\circ$) ranges cannot reach a straight-arm
overhead lockout or a front rack at the clean grip, so neither is claimed.
\texttt{arm\_add} is solved (Brent's method) so the hand-centre grip width
equals the standard's \texttt{width\_m} scaled by $h / 1.75$\,m. No shoulder,
back or chest contact with the bar is modelled; the hands carry the whole
bar. The standard's via-poses are not retargeted (OSL-3).

\paragraph{Acceptance and Measured Evidence.} For each lift the model passes
\texttt{initSystem}; its counts are read from the model (23 bodies, 23 joints,
3 constraints, 80 muscles; 103 forces, or 107 for the bench press); the bar is
held by both hands. In the static hold (80\,kg, 1.78\,m lifter, 120\,kg bar,
$W = 1176.80$\,N), the shared LIFT-4 bar-hold wrench meets the \#11744
acceptance (hand forces sum to $W$ within 1\,\%, measured relative error 0,
an even 588.40\,N split, trail-weld residual below $10^{-15}$\,m). With every
coordinate locked, OpenSim's own \texttt{WeldJoint} reaction plus
\texttt{WeldConstraint} force on the bar sum to $W$ within $2 \times
10^{-11}$; that split is the solver's choice for a statically indeterminate
hold and is not interpreted. Reproduce with
\texttt{python3 -m pytest -o addopts="" tests/unit/lifting/test\_opensim\_full\_body\_lift\_models.py}
(requires OpenSim 4.6 and the initialised submodule; otherwise the tests skip
with the reason).

\paragraph{Limitations.} Uniform height scaling, not marker scaling. The
hold poses are engineering choices, not measured postures. The hands carry
the bar in the squat hold. MyoFullBody, Hunt--Crossley contact and the
additional movements fail closed with a reason.

\subsection{OpenSim Inverse Kinematics and Inverse Dynamics (OSL-3, Issue \#13041)}
\paragraph{Inputs.} \#11745 originally closed with only one LIFT-5 same-input bundle:
\texttt{docs/development/lifting/evidence/back\_squat/back\_squat\_barbell\_bundle.npz}
(SHA-256 \texttt{850d42b1\ldots 71430}, MuJoCo reference engine, 200 steps of
1\,ms, the 0.2\,s back-squat window), leaving deadlift, bench press, snatch, and clean and jerk
initially \texttt{"unavailable"}.
Under OSL-3 completion, all five lifts have registered barbell bundles and complete receipts
(detailed in Section~\ref{sec:opensim-ik-id-all-five}). The historical back-squat baseline
below is preserved for record.

\paragraph{Targets and Loads.} The bundle replays the golf full-body spec, not
Rajagopal, so its coordinates do not transfer by name (its hip flexion reads
160--171$^\circ$). \texttt{lift\_targets}
(\texttt{src/shared/python/lifting/bundle\_targets.py}) instead takes geometry
and load: the spec's own marker kinematics (ankle, knee and hip centres,
shoulders), heel and forefoot points at the spec's calcaneus contact-sphere
stations, the LIFT-4 bar centre, the GCV-1 per-foot wrench of the bundle's
shared-law contact (\texttt{bundle\_kinetics}) and the GCV-7 per-hand bushing
wrench (\texttt{grip\_analysis}, negated onto the hands). Everything is
re-expressed in the OpenSim lift frame: $x$ forward (heel to forefoot), $y$ up,
$z$ right, origin on the floor under mid-foot of the first state. The model is
built with the bundle's masses (108.32\,kg body, 100\,kg bar, 1.78\,m) and
per-segment scale factors from the bundle's own joint-centre distances
(femur 0.955/0.984, shank 1.092/1.103 left/right, torso 1.148; uniform 1.047).

\paragraph{Method.} \texttt{run\_inverse\_kinematics}
(\texttt{src/engines/physics\_engines/opensim/python/msk\_lift\_ik\_id.py}) uses
OpenSim's \texttt{InverseKinematicsSolver} (accuracy $10^{-8}$, coordinate
clamping off, lower-limb weight 10, shoulders and bar 1) on every fifth state
(41 frames). \texttt{run\_inverse\_dynamics} uses \texttt{InverseDynamicsSolver}
on a copy with muscles, actuators and contact removed and gravity kept; speeds
and accelerations come from quintic GCV splines of the IK coordinates; the
external body forces are added by superposition
($\tau = \mathrm{ID}(\dot u) + \mathrm{ID}_4(0, F) - \mathrm{ID}_4(0, 0)$, since
the four-argument solve omits gravity). The bar is massless in ID because the
GCV-7 hand wrench already carries it. The static hold
(\texttt{static\_hold\_grf}) keeps the smooth contacts, solves the pelvis
height at which OpenSim's own ID gives zero vertical pelvis residual (Brent,
$10^{-12}$\,m), and reduces the \texttt{SmoothSphereHalfSpaceForce} record
values per support through the shared GCV-1 analysis.

\paragraph{Measured Evidence.} Receipts:
\texttt{docs/development/lifting/evidence/opensim\_ik\_id/receipts.json}.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Static hold, all five lifts.} The contact ground reaction equals
  $(m_\mathrm{body} + m_\mathrm{bar})\,g$ to a relative error below
  $3\times10^{-13}$ (80\,kg lifter, 120\,kg bar). The feet carry 980.7\,N each,
  except the bench press (bench 846.8\,N, feet 557.3\,N each).
\item \textbf{Squat IK.} Convention-free sagittal segment-angle RMS
  0.0252\,rad (1.44$^\circ$; shank 0.0292, thigh 0.0084, trunk 0.0313\,rad),
  below the 2$^\circ$ receipt threshold. Marker RMS 4.07\,cm and maximum
  12.6\,cm (the bar); this fails the 2\,cm/4\,cm best-practice marker gate.
\item \textbf{Squat ID.} Every joint moment is finite. Pelvis residuals (peak,
  RMS): $t_x$ 2927, 848\,N; $t_y$ 3930, 1449\,N; $t_z$ 1177, 363\,N; tilt 1540,
  422\,N\,m; list 580, 180\,N\,m; rotation 363, 132\,N\,m. The Hicks et
  al.\ (2015) gate (5\,\% of the 3031\,N peak external force = 151.6\,N;
  1\,\% of COM height times that force = 17.3\,N\,m) fails for both forces and
  moments.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Failed Experiments.} A rigid-body forward settle onto the contacts
tipped the squat hold over, since its centre of mass sits 21\,cm behind the
feet; it was replaced by the inverse-dynamics vertical root solve. Virtual
pelvis markers derived from the spec's hip parent frames were not added: the
spec's hip separation (0.22\,m) lies on a different axis and length than
Rajagopal's (0.155\,m), so no verified transform exists.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Results are not physically accepted.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Only the back squat has an input bundle; four lifts are unavailable.
\item Pelvis orientation is weakly observed: only the two hip centres lie on
  the pelvis, so pelvis tilt ($-94$ to $-120^\circ$) trades off against hip

\subsection{Physically Credible Lift IK/ID (OSL-12, Issue \#13048)}
\paragraph{Scope.} OSL-12 addresses three OSL-3 limitations (pelvis
observability, contact sinkage, unbalanced holds) on the same back-squat
bundle and the same 80\,kg/1.78\,m/120\,kg hold specification. Receipts:
\texttt{docs/development/lifting/evidence/opensim\_ik\_id/receipts.json};
tests: \texttt{tests/unit/lifting/test\_opensim\_lift\_credibility.py}.

\paragraph{Pelvis Frame and Width.} The bundle spec grafts the Rajagopal lower
limbs onto the golf \texttt{LowerTorso}; both hip joints hang from it with one
shared \texttt{parent\_to\_base} orientation $R_b$. The Rajagopal pelvis axes
in \texttt{LowerTorso} are therefore $P = R_b\,\Pi^\top$, with $\Pi$ the spec's
hip axis permutation (\texttt{bundle\_pelvis\_rotation},
\texttt{bundle\_targets.py}). This is verified, not assumed: for every squat
state, each femur pose times the transposed Rajagopal hip rotation
($R_z(\text{flexion})R_x(\text{adduction})R_y(\text{rotation})$) reproduces
$P$ to $10^{-9}$ for both legs, and the hip axis lies along $P$'s $z$. The
bundle's hip centres are 0.2205\,m apart against the generic 0.1545\,m, so the
pelvis is scaled per axis by $(s, s, 0.2205/0.1545) = (1.047, 1.047, 1.427)$;
nothing in the bundle measures pelvis depth or height, so those keep the
stature factor $s = h/1.70$\,m.

\paragraph{Virtual ASIS/PSIS Landmarks.} The source is the generic model's own
RASI/LASI/RPSI/LPSI markers (\texttt{RajagopalLaiUhlrich2023.osim}); their
offsets from the generic hip-centre midpoint, scaled by the pelvis factors and
placed with $P$ at the bundle's hip-centre midpoint, give the targets
(\texttt{pelvis\_landmark\_targets}). A model scaled with those factors whose
pelvis coincides with the bundle's carries its markers onto the targets to
$10^{-9}$\,m (test). The mapping is exact, but the bundle's pelvis is not a
physical observation: LIFT-5 tracked only the hip centres, its own pelvis tilt

\paragraph{Contact Stiffness.} The lift contacts use
$k = 10^7$\,Pa, the \texttt{CONTACT\_STIFFNESS} of OpenSim\_Models PR \#476
(4.35\,mm under 1961\,N with eight foot spheres; stance band 1--8\,mm), with the
MS-20 damping and friction kept. The limit is that band's 8\,mm

\subsection{LIFT-4 Barbell Re-Track Drift (Issue \#13072)}

\paragraph{Root Cause A: Bar Load Missing From the Controller Model (Fixed).}
\texttt{build\_barbell\_bundle} tracked the loaded plant with the pipeline
computed-torque controller (kkt) built on the body-only spec. It then added the
bar weight as a joint-space feedforward on the actuated rows only. On a floating
base the root rows carry part of that load, so the controller's predicted
acceleration was wrong from the first step, and the error compounded.
\texttt{SpineInputY} error was $0.99^\circ$ at step 3 and $9.76^\circ$ at
step 10. The fix is \texttt{rigid\_bar\_spec}, which appends the bar as a rigid
solid on the hand body so the controller's mass matrix and bias include the bar
on every row. The feet are preloaded for body plus bar, and the feedforward is
removed. After the fix the error is $2\times10^{-4}$ and $1\times10^{-4}$\,deg
at steps 3 and 10, and the wrist no longer sags. A unit test checks that the
rigid-bar gravity bias minus the body-only bias equals the hand weight's
generalized force on every row, root rows included (each above 500).

\paragraph{Root Cause B: Combined Centre of Mass Beyond the Support (Open).}
The source squat was generated without the bar. With the bar added on the back
shelf, the combined centre of mass sits 0.15--0.17\,m ahead of the forefoot
spheres. The tracked body tips forward about the toes (pelvis pitch
$11.8^\circ$, shoulders 13.2\,cm), and no joint-space tracker can hold it.
Maximum landmark deviation of the re-track from its source, with the first
step past the LIFT-5 IK bound (old bundle in brackets):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Ankles: 13.3\,mm (step 80, left) and 12.0\,mm (step 58, right)
  [245 and 302\,mm].
\item Knees: 73.0 and 74.0\,mm (steps 95 and 114).
\item Hips: 88.1 and 90.1\,mm (steps 113 and 77) [right hip 272\,mm].
\item Shoulders: 132\,mm each (steps 85 and 73) [left shoulder 346\,mm].
\item Bar: 159\,mm (from step 0, see cause C).
\end{itemize}
A real fix needs the source squat regenerated with the bar over mid-foot, from
the shelf geometry or a balance constraint in the IK. That follow-up is outside
\texttt{build\_barbell\_bundle}. The bundle records the per-landmark deviation
as \texttt{retrack\_landmark\_deviation\_m}, and the bound test is a strict
xfail until it passes.

\paragraph{Root Cause C: Target and Sink (Open).} The re-track follows the
reference rollout, not the IK target, and the bar landmark of the bodyweight
source is the shoulder-girdle centre, not the bar. The loaded feet also sink
1.63\,mm further, so a 0.1\,mm bar bound cannot be met by any loaded re-track.

\subsection{Bar-Load Balance of the Source Squat (OSL-15, Issue \#13093)}

\paragraph{Method.} The Movement Optimizer balances its planar
\texttt{full\_squat} with the bar at its shoulder joint: the Tools
\texttt{BodyModel} accepts only \texttt{squat\_bar\_depth} and
\texttt{squat\_bar\_height} $\ge 0$ (bar behind or below the shoulder), so it
cannot express the back-bar shelf, which sits 0.20\,m above and 0.06\,m in
front of the shoulder joints. That gap is reported to Tools; it is not
patched here. \texttt{loaded\_balance.balance\_lift} closes it in the IK. The
lift is solved as before; the body-plus-bar centre of mass is evaluated on the
lift body with the bar welded to the hands (\texttt{rigid\_bar\_spec}, the
model the LIFT-4 re-track controller uses), and each frame's sagittal trunk
angle is corrected by a per-frame secant iteration (first step a 0.05\,rad
probe, steps clipped to 0.25\,rad) until the combined centre of mass lies
within 0.5\,mm of mid-foot (the mean floor point of the contact spheres, the
origin the shelf was fitted from). The bar stays on the shelf and rides the
corrected trunk. This is the optimizer's own squat-bottom rule
(\texttt{balance\_pose(..., adjust\_joint=2)}) applied with the full-body
masses and the bar where the shelf holds it. The generator applies it with
\texttt{--load-balance}; \texttt{lift\_bundle\_spec} then makes the bundle's
spec the rigid-bar spec, and \texttt{bushing\_body\_spec} recovers the body
the LIFT-4 bushings attach to.

\subsection{Loaded Foot Stability (OSL-16, Issue \#13104)}

\paragraph{Root Cause.} The foot joints ran away because of the integrator,
not the controller or the posture. The same-input step is zero-order-hold RK4
with eight 0.125\,ms substeps per 1\,ms step. Classical RK4 multiplies a real
mode $\dot x=\lambda x$ by
$R(z)=1+z+z^2/2+z^3/6+z^4/24$ per substep, $z=\lambda h$, and
$|R(z)|\le 1$ on the real axis only for $-2.785\le z\le 0$. The open-loop
state Jacobian of the rigid-bar plant (central differences, the effort held)
has real foot modes (\texttt{mtp}, subtalar, ankle) whose rate is the
Coulomb regularisation slope $\mu f_n/v_t$ of the stance spheres
($\mu_s=0.6$, $v_t=0.01$\,m/s, Hunt--Crossley $k=10^5$\,N/m) acting on the
light foot segments. It scales with the sphere normal force: halving $k$ at a
fixed state halves it, doubling $v_t$ lowers it. The bar raises the stance
force, so along the committed loaded window the stiffest rate grows from
$1.2\times10^4$ to $1.30\times10^5$\,s$^{-1}$ (at the window end, where each
heel and toe sphere carries 440--510\,N). At the default substep that is
$|z|$ up to 16. The mode is amplified instead of damped, and the toes, then
the ankles and subtalars, leave the IK. The bodyweight bundle reaches
$3.4\times10^4$\,s$^{-1}$ only briefly and stays stable, which is why the
fault appeared with the bar. A second, first-order error remains at any
stable substep: the computed torque is held over the effort hold while the
contact (time constant about 35\,$\mu$s) re-equilibrates. At step 50 of the
1\,ms-hold run the commanded \texttt{mtp} acceleration was $-126$\,rad/s$^2$
and the realised one $-25$\,rad/s$^2$.

\paragraph{Fix.} A same-input bundle now records its RK4 substep count
(\texttt{InputBundle.substeps}, manifest \texttt{policy.substeps}; legacy
bundles load the original eight), and every replay reads it through
\texttt{step\_policy()}. \texttt{stiffness.substeps\_for} picks the smallest
power of two that keeps the measured stiffest rate inside the RK4 real
stability limit. Bar-loaded lift bundles, the barbell hold and the re-track
use \texttt{LOADED\_TRACKING\_POLICY}: 64 substeps (15.6\,$\mu$s) and a
0.25\,ms effort hold. The committed source records its own margin,
\texttt{rk4\_stability}: largest rate $1.30\times10^5$\,s$^{-1}$ over 40
sampled states, $|z|=2.03$, $|R|=0.34$. No physical parameter changed: the
contact law, gains and body are those of OSL-15.

\subsection{Barbell Hold in Contact Equilibrium (LIFT-4, Issue \#11744)}
\paragraph{Diagnosis.} After OSL-16 the 1.5\,s hold carried body plus bar
exactly at 0.75--1.0\,s, then sagged to 0.988 (ground) and 0.986 (hands)
over the last 0.5\,s, under a mean horizontal ground force of $-102$\,N.
Each candidate cause was measured on the committed back-squat bundle
(old-body baseline reproduced to the last digit: ground 0.98799, hands
0.98627, left share 0.49258, deflection 0.629\,mm):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Start not in contact equilibrium: the cause.} The hold
  started from \texttt{preload\_feet}, which translates the IK standing
  pose down until its lowest sphere (the toes) sinks to the equal-share
  depth $W/(6k)$. At that state the feet carried 0.595 of body plus bar
  and their centre of pressure lay 6.7\,cm ahead of the combined centre of
  mass. The rigid posture rotated back toward its contact equilibrium
  (root pitch from 0.07 to $-2.06^\circ$ by about 0.55\,s), arrived with
  momentum (CoM velocity about $-0.14$\,m/s), and overshot. The CoP pinned
  at the heel edge ($-0.046$\,m) at 0.75--0.8\,s and the body toppled
  backwards: CoM $-0.204$\,m and pitch $-9.15^\circ$ at 1.5\,s, horizontal
  force $-190$\,N. The ``steady $-102$\,N'' was the mean of this topple
  over 1.0--1.5\,s.
\item \textbf{Balance controller: does not arrest it, does not cause it.}
  The horizontal CoM error reached $-0.24$\,m; the balance gains (60, 15)
  are small against the posture's $\omega^2=900$, so a large initial
  imbalance is not corrected. From an equilibrium start the term is idle.
\item \textbf{Friction or tangential creep: not the cause.} Heel slip
  stayed below 2.5\,mm and comes from rolling about the heel edge.
\item \textbf{Bushing pre-load direction: not the cause.} At $t=0$ each
  hand bushing pulls exactly vertically (490.33\,N per hand).
\item \textbf{Asymmetric feet: not the cause.} Lateral foot offsets are
  symmetric within 1.5\,mm and the lateral CoP is $-4.2$\,mm. The internal
  lateral friction during the fall (about $\pm300$\,N) cancelled between
  the feet. The equilibrium foot loads (850 and 918\,N) are harmless.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Fix.} \texttt{barbell\_runs.hold\_start} projects the hold pose
onto the closure, then solves for the contact equilibrium on the
rigid-bar model (\texttt{contact\_equilibrium\_pose}): a minimum-norm
Gauss--Newton over the root's normal translation and its three angles,
with residuals $F_n/W-1$ and the tangential contact moment about the
combined CoM over $W$, to $10^{-12}$. The actuated coordinates and the
root's horizontal position are unchanged. The bushing plant starts from
that state with the bar hanging still. The bushing hand Jacobian now reads
the single hand frame (\texttt{VectorPlant.kinematic\_frame}), bit-identical
to the full frame map and 3.9 times faster.

\paragraph{Result.} \texttt{barbell\_hold.json} (old body, see below): over
the whole 1.5\,s, vertical ground force over body plus bar 1.000000
(1768.2665\,N, range $4\times10^{-11}$\,N), hand force over bar weight
1.000000, left share 0.500, peak deflection 0.49\,mm, horizontal force
below $10^{-8}$\,N. The hold xfails are now plain tests with unchanged
thresholds (1\,\%, 0.01, 3\,mm), held at every sample, not only the last
0.5\,s.

\paragraph{Robustness.} From the equilibrium with $+0.25^\circ$ root pitch
the hold stays bounded: CoM error at most 1.1\,cm, horizontal force at
most 10\,N, heel slip below 0.1\,mm, a slow sway, no failure in 1.5\,s.
A large initial imbalance (the old 6.7\,cm) is not recovered, so the
balance controller's authority remains a limitation.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item The first equilibrium solve used \texttt{attached\_state}'s bar

  balanced the wrong CoM; the solve now runs on the rigid-bar model whose

\item Routing the hand pose through a per-call dictionary of all frames

\paragraph{Rack Definition (Cited).} Glassbrook et al.\ (2019, J Strength

\subsection{Bench Press Same-Input Bundle (LIFT-5, Issue \#11745)}
\paragraph{Contact Model.} The same-input full-body spec had contact spheres
on the feet only, so a supine trunk fell. The shared Hunt--Crossley law
(\texttt{motion\_matching/contact\_law.py}) now accepts raised supports:
\texttt{contact.supports} names spheres that press on a plane parallel to the
ground at the support height $h$, with penetration
$\delta = \max(0, r - (\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{c} - h))$ for a sphere of radius $r$
and centre $\mathbf{c}$. The MuJoCo, Drake, Pinocchio and OpenSim adapters
resolve the plane per sphere through one helper (\texttt{support\_planes}),
so the force law is not duplicated. The bench adds four back spheres that
mirror OSL-2 (\texttt{msk\_lift\_model.\_BACK\_SPHERES}): posterior shoulder
(RPSH, LPSH) and posterior iliac (RPSI, LPSI) sites, radius
$0.04\,\mathrm{m}\times 1.78/1.70 = 0.0419$\,m, one radius anterior of each
skin marker. Stiffness stays at the spec's $10^5$\,N/m.

\paragraph{Hold and Bench Height.} The supine hold
(\texttt{lifting/bench\_press.py}, \texttt{solve\_bench\_hold}) rotates the
standing trunk until the back-sphere line is level, applies the parity
standard's bench-start leg angles (hip extension 0.27\,rad, knee flexion
1.65\,rad), and fits the body with the feet flat on the floor and the
back-sphere centres weighted $10\times$. As in OSL-2, the bench top is the
lowest back-sphere point of the fit: 0.501\,m, which is 5--8\,cm above the
IPF 0.42--0.45\,m rule (IPF Technical Rules Book 2026). The other spheres
clear it by 0.21--1.00\,mm. An earlier attempt pitched the root with
$\texttt{HipInputY}=-90^\circ$; that is the gimbal lock of the root's
$R_xR_yR_z$ chain and left 1--2\,cm residuals and up to 11\,mm sphere
clearance. The hold now lies along $y$
($\texttt{HipInputX}=\texttt{HipInputZ}=-90^\circ$). Without the sphere
weights the clearances were up to 7.1\,mm.

\paragraph{Press.} The Movement Optimizer's bench model is an arm chain on a
fixed shoulder (80\,kg, 1.78\,m, 100\,kg bar, 2.0\,s): the bar offset runs from
$(0, 0.696)$\,m at lockout to $(0.081, 0.021)$\,m at the chest (1.0\,s), with a
peak vertical speed of 1.42\,m/s. Only the arm chain moves. The bar centre
is tracked to the model's shoulder midpoint plus that offset, scaled by
0.792 (the model's 0.551\,m straight-up reach at the 0.75\,m grip, divided by
the optimizer's lockout offset). The bar residual is 4.9\,mm at lockout and
14.7\,mm at the chest, where the arms cannot fold to the optimizer's point.
That point is only 0.021\,m above the shoulder joint centre, i.e. inside
the chest, and the chest has no contact. The model's bar travels 0.527\,m
vertically. Larsen, Gomo and van den Tillaar (2021, \emph{Front.\ Sports
Act.\ Living} 2:637066, Table~1) report about 0.35\,m for a 1-RM medium grip,
and Gonz\'alez-Badillo and S\'anchez-Medina (2010, \emph{Int.\ J.\ Sports
Med.} 31(5):347--352) report $0.16\pm0.04$\,m/s mean velocity at 1RM. The optimizer's lift is a faster
submaximal press. These are evidence, not tuning targets.

\paragraph{Bundle and Parity.} The 0.2\,s window, centred on the chest touch,
starts at 0.893\,s (200 steps at 1\,ms; 229\,kB). MuJoCo tracking keeps the
trunk on the bench: by mid-window the back spheres carry 462--732\,N at
0.9--2.6\,mm penetration, and the floating root drifts at most 1.8\,mm and
$0.21^\circ$. In open-loop replay over the full window, the bar agrees with
MuJoCo to $1.0\times10^{-11}$\,m (Drake), $1.2\times10^{-11}$\,m (Pinocchio)
and $1.5\times10^{-13}$\,m (OpenSim). The coordinates agree to
$1.9\times10^{-9}$, $2.2\times10^{-9}$ and $2.6\times10^{-11}$\,rad, inside the
$10^{-6}$\,rad L2 bound, and MuJoCo is bit exact
(\texttt{docs/development/lifting/evidence/bench\_press/}).

\paragraph{Limitations.} The trunk, legs, scapulae and head are held, so
there is no leg drive or arch. The chest has no contact. The bar mass is
absent from the full-body dynamics (LIFT-4, \#11744). The bench is an
unbounded plane. Pinocchio's analytic contact derivatives raise for a spec

\texttt{MUJOCO\_GL=egl python3 scripts/generate\_lift\_bundle.py --lift bench\_press}

\subsection{OpenSim Muscle Forces and Activations (OSL-4, Issue \#13042)}\label{sec:opensim-muscles}
\paragraph{Method and Formulation.}
OSL-4 completes the muscle-force and activation pipeline for the five barbell
lifts using OpenSim \texttt{StaticOptimization} and \texttt{MocoInverse} (where
converging), reusing the reserve-share formulation from
\texttt{musculoskeletal\_static\_opt.py} and \texttt{musculoskeletal\_solvers.py}.
At each sampled frame $t$, the muscle redundancy is resolved against the inverse-dynamics
joint moments $\boldsymbol{\tau}$ for rigid-tendon Millard muscles
(Rajagopal et al.\ 2016, 80 lower-limb muscles across 14 degrees of freedom):
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{a}} \sum_{m=1}^{80} a_m^2 + w \sum_{c=1}^{14} r_c^2 \quad \text{s.t.} \quad 0 \le a_m \le 1,\quad \boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{R}^\top (\mathbf{F}_\text{pas} + \mathbf{a} \odot \mathbf{F}_\text{act}) + \mathbf{r}
\end{equation}
The reserve actuator share across the lift is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\text{Reserve Share} = \frac{\sum_t \sum_c |r_c(t)|}{\sum_t \sum_c |\tau_c(t)|}
\end{equation}
A lift is classified as \texttt{qualified} only when the reserve share is strictly below 5\% ($< 0.05$).

the 0.2\,s engine window is located entirely at the deep squat bottom (mean knee
angle $109.7^\circ$, hip flexion at the $120.0^\circ$ model upper bound). The motion
does not reach standing ($< 25^\circ$), descent, or lockout; the pipeline reports the
single honest segment \texttt{bottom (engine window 0.0-0.2 s)}. The moment balance
residual error holds below $1.14 \times 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ by construction because
reserve actuators close any remainder in the static optimization solve; this is an
internal sanity check and does not constitute evidence of physical acceptance.

as \texttt{unqualified} due to heavy physiological torque demands exceeding muscle capacity

\subsection{OpenSim Barbell Lift Joint Reactions (OSL-5, Issue \#13043)}\label{sec:opensim-joint-reactions}
\paragraph{Method and Formulation.}
OSL-5 computes the 3D internal joint reaction loads across the barbell lift motion window
by applying the OpenSim \texttt{JointReaction} analysis to the static-optimization muscle forces and kinematics.
Loads are evaluated across four primary anatomical articulation sites:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
\item \textbf{L5/S1 Lumbar Joint:} Evaluated at the OpenSim \texttt{back} joint connecting \texttt{pelvis\_offset} (parent)
  and \texttt{torso\_offset} (child). Reaction forces are extracted in the inferior body coordinate frame
  (pelvis ISB convention: $+X$ anterior, $+Y$ cranial, $+Z$ right), and also rotated into the longitudinal torso coordinate frame.
  \textit{Qualification}: lower bound: no trunk muscles; the lumbar moment is an ideal actuator.
\item \textbf{Knee Tibiofemoral Joint:} Contact load across \texttt{walker\_knee\_r} and \texttt{walker\_knee\_l},
  reported as the 3D resultant spatial force norm.
\item \textbf{Knee Patellofemoral Joint:} Evaluated across \texttt{patellofemoral\_r} and \texttt{patellofemoral\_l}.
  The \texttt{RajagopalLaiUhlrich2023} model explicitly includes articulated patellofemoral joints.
\item \textbf{Shoulder Glenohumeral Joint:} Evaluated at the \texttt{acromial\_r} and \texttt{acromial\_l} joints.
  In this model, the shoulder joints are articulated CustomJoints (three rotational DOFs, not welded).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Key Limitations: Absence of Trunk Muscles and Inherited Qualification.}
The Rajagopal model lacks active trunk extensor muscles (e.g., erector spinae).
The sagittal lumbar extension moment is supplied entirely by an ideal coordinate actuator
(\texttt{lumbar\_extension}), with a measured peak demand of $665.32\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ in the squat solve.
Because coordinate actuators apply pure generalized forces without muscular line-of-action compression,
the computed L5/S1 joint reaction force excludes trunk muscular co-contraction. It constitutes a
gravity-plus-inertia lower bound rather than an anatomical spinal compression estimate.
The segmental spine model (\#12735) is identified as the required path for active multi-segment trunk load.
Furthermore, the underlying OSL-4 static optimization solve is classified as \texttt{unqualified},
with a mean reserve actuator share of $0.5680$ and peak share of $0.6724$ (exceeding the $0.05$ threshold).
All reaction loads inherit this unqualified status.

\paragraph{Analytic Bound and Dynamic Squat Frame Evaluation.}
To verify sign conventions and coordinate frame extraction, an analytic bound is first evaluated at
an upright, zero-velocity, zero-acceleration default pose:
\begin{equation}
W_{\text{above}} \le \text{Compression} \le W_{\text{above}} + \sum_{m} F_m
\end{equation}
where $W_{\text{above}} = M_{\text{above}} g$ is the gravitational weight of all bodies superior to L5/S1 (torso $32.4045\,\mathrm{kg}$, arms $4.0934\,\mathrm{kg}$, barbell $100.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, total $M_{\text{above}} = 136.4979\,\mathrm{kg}$, $W_{\text{above}} = 1338.59\,\mathrm{N}$).
Muscles crossing L5/S1 are verified dynamically from geometry paths; exactly 0 muscles cross between the torso and pelvis/legs in Rajagopal, giving $\sum F_m = 0.0\,\mathrm{N}$.
At the default pose, compression is $1338.59\,\mathrm{N}$ with an absolute residual of $2.27 \times 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{N}$ (\texttt{passed: True}). This test verifies frame and sign self-consistency only, and does not verify dynamic lift validity.

The bound was additionally evaluated on the dynamic squat frame with minimal generalized acceleration norm:
frame 40 ($t = 0.2000\,\mathrm{s}$, $\|\ddot{q}\| = 20.0378\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$, $W_{\text{above}} = 1338.59\,\mathrm{N}$).
Measured compression is $1437.27\,\mathrm{N}$ in the pelvis frame and $1030.71\,\mathrm{N}$ in the torso frame.
The lower bound $W_{\text{above}} \le \text{Comp}$ is satisfied in the pelvis frame ($1338.59 \le 1437.27$) but not the torso frame ($1338.59 \le 1030.71$).
The upper bound $\text{Comp} \le W_{\text{above}} + \sum F_m$ is satisfied in the torso frame ($1030.71 \le 1338.59$) but not the pelvis frame ($1437.27 \le 1338.59$).
Because both inequalities do not hold simultaneously, this dynamic check honestly reports \texttt{passed: False}, reflecting the influence of dynamic pelvis accelerations, trunk inclination, and the absence of trunk musculature.

\subsection{Clean-and-Jerk Whole-Lift Bundle (LIFT-5, Issue \#11745)}
\paragraph{Planar Lift.} The previous clean-and-jerk bundle solved the Tools
\texttt{clean} exercise (1.2\,s, one mid-time via point) and kept only its

Tools \#5662 (issue Tools\#5661) adds \texttt{make\_clean\_and\_jerk\_config}:

0.85) and three events inside them (full extension 0.25, dip bottom 0.6,
drive extension 0.65). The rack catch keeps the standard's knee flexion
($-97.2^\circ$) and trunk lean ($18.31^\circ$, inside the $2.78^\circ$
lumbar tolerance); its shank is solved so that the centre of mass is over
the inner base of support, which needs a $46^\circ$ shank and so the model's
$50^\circ$ ankle limit. The standard's symmetric stance makes the jerk a
power jerk, caught at the jerk dip depth ($-52^\circ$ knee) with the
lockout trunk lean ($-0.05^\circ$, inside $3.64^\circ$). The phase-correct
bar hangs one arm length below the shoulder through the pulls, sits at the
shoulder from the rack catch to the drive extension and is one arm length
above it from the overhead catch on, with smoothstep blends across the
turnover and the drive. The \texttt{clean} and \texttt{jerk} exercises are
unchanged.

The shoulder rises monotonically from 0.890 to 1.376\,m through the pulls,
with a peak bar velocity of 1.72\,m/s at 0.85\,s. The bar is at the
shoulder (1.064\,m) at the catch, dips from 1.380 to 1.291\,m and drives back
to 1.376\,m in the jerk, is overhead at 1.978\,m at the catch and ends at
2.068\,m. The optimizer reports a maximum joint speed of 6.26\,rad/s, above
its 2\,rad/s slow-movement assumption, so the omitted Coriolis terms make its
torques indicative.

\section{Failed Experiments and Negative Results}\label{sec:failed}
These are the negative results actually documented for the lift packs. No
additional failed-experiment narratives (in the style of, for example, the
same-input-parity reference's ``Failed First Comparison''/``Failed First
Version'' sections) exist in the lift-pack sources read for this document;
none are synthesized here.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{No floor-pull start pose reaches the plate radius.} The audit
  threshold is $0.225\pm0.05$\,m (450\,mm plate radius; Chapter 15,
  ``Tolerances''). Measured bar-centre-above-sole at the deadlift start pose:
  MuJoCo 1.017\,m, OpenSim 0.7097\,m, Drake 0.6969\,m, Pinocchio 0.6586\,m ---
  every pack, every floor-pull lift (deadlift, snatch, clean and jerk), misses
  the target (PACK\_PARITY\_BASELINE.md, ``Start Poses''; gap class
  \texttt{start\_pose}).
\item \textbf{The right hand is not attached to the bar in Drake or
  Pinocchio.} Both weld/fix only the left hand; Pinocchio's right-hand
  attachment is only a dummy frame, \texttt{barbell\_grip\_r}
  (PACK\_PARITY\_BASELINE.md, ``Hand and Bar Attachment''; gap class
  \texttt{grip\_attachment}).
\item \textbf{Grip half-width does not match achievable hand spacing in any
  pack.} For the deadlift, intended half-width vs.\ achievable half-spacing:
  MuJoCo 0.22\,m vs.\ 0.1709\,m (mismatch 0.0491\,m); OpenSim 0.4\,m vs.\
  0.1594\,m (mismatch 0.2406\,m); Drake 0.22\,m vs.\ 0.1709\,m (mismatch
  0.0491\,m); Pinocchio 0.393\,m vs.\ 0.1709\,m (mismatch 0.2221\,m), all
  above the 0.01\,m audit threshold (PACK\_PARITY\_BASELINE.md, ``Hand and Bar
  Attachment''; gap class \texttt{grip\_width}).
\item \textbf{The bench-press start pose carries an unphysical contact
  force in OpenSim.} 21.7\,kN of vertical ground contact force at the bench
  default pose (PACK\_PARITY\_BASELINE.md, ``Epic Defect List Review'';
  confirmed finding for OpenSim\_Models\#384). MuJoCo's own start poses carry
  large non-ground contact force for the floor-pull lifts (up to
  $4.486\times10^{5}$\,N on the snatch), a separate instance of the same gap
  class \texttt{contacts}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Unavailable is never zero.} Drake and Pinocchio expose no
  contact model, so start-pose ground-reaction force is reported
  unavailable with a reason string, never as 0\,N (Chapter 15, ``Unavailable
  Values''; gap class \texttt{no\_grf}).
\item Native hand-to-bar closure is reported only where an engine defines it
  (MuJoCo welds, OpenSim weld constraint after assembly); it is unavailable
  for Drake and Pinocchio.
\item The lifter-only centre of mass is unavailable when no lifter mass
  remains, and bar height above the sole is unavailable when no foot-contact
  geometry is found. Missing bodies, non-finite positions and coincident bar
  sleeves raise \texttt{ValueError}; no geometric value is filled in.
\item The bar/bench zero-mass-centre-offset assumption (Section~2.2) holds at
  the recorded commits; the lifter-only CoM figure is wrong if a pack changes
  that without updating the audit.
\item OpenSim's start-pose coordinate values are read after model assembly,
  which moves coordinates off their declared defaults.
\item OpenSim, Drake and Pinocchio stills are skeleton plots of each engine's
  own FK, not native renders.
\item No Simscape or MATLAB lift models exist in any repository, and nothing
  was substituted for them (epic \#11740).
\item This document is a software-consistency reference. Scientific
  qualification of any lift model against measured data stays with the
  design-manual governance pathway (\texttt{scripts.check\_design\_manual\_governance});
  a successful build of this document is not scientific, semantic, visual,
  accessibility, or publication approval of anything.
\end{itemize}
