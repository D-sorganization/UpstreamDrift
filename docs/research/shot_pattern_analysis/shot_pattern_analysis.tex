\textbf{Research Question}\par
Does deliberately curving a shot produce a tighter or more valuable outcome
when nominal targets are matched and impact delivery varies?\par
\vspace{0.18in}
\textbf{Principal Finding}\par
There is no universal winning shot shape in these simulations. Dispersion,
carry, and conditional scoring depend on the club, delivery variability, and
geometric assumptions. A matched nominal target does not force equal carry
on every shot or isolate curvature as a cause.

\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
This study compares straight, draw, and fade patterns for three assumed club
geometries. The matched-delivery experiment solves nominal face, path, and
speed for a common raw landing target, then applies paired variations in
shaft rotation, path, speed, strike, and covariance. It covers shaft-rotation
standard deviations of $1^\circ$ and $2^\circ$, ordinary and doubled curve
strength, and three forward-lean settings. The full original and supplemental
inventories contain 10.44 million attempted shot rows. Supported populations,
nominal-search failures, and invalid outcomes remain explicit. We examine
endpoint density, lateral and longitudinal spread, illustrative flight paths,
and conditional strokes gained. The selected scoring estimates do not identify one pattern that is best
across these modeled clubs and delivery distributions. Face closure through shaft rotation lowers delivered
loft locally, but its effect on carry is club dependent. Each headline
comparison carries two uncertainty statements: a paired Monte Carlo interval
from \BootstrapResamples{} resamples and a wider band that also spans assumed
clubhead centre-of-gravity depth, inertia, driver face curvature and an
alternative contact law. Only \BandDirectionChanges{} of the 48 headline
comparisons changes direction under that band. The estimates are model
sensitivities, not measured golfer performance; neither statement includes
course-model or player-distribution uncertainty.

\section{Executive Findings}
\textbf{Curving the ball is not a universal accuracy advantage.}
The selected zero-lean scoring comparisons favor different shapes for different
clubs, with some intervals spanning zero. Neither a small scoring difference
nor a narrow sampling interval establishes a golfer's preferred pattern.

\textbf{The shots do not all travel the same distance.}
The solver matches the zero-error nominal endpoint. Noisy shots retain both
lateral and downrange variation; changing variability can also change mean
carry. The fixed-target controls reduce nominal distance mismatch but rematch
face, path, and speed, preventing a causal attribution to curvature alone.

\textbf{Face closure and loft are coupled under the shaft-rotation assumption.}
Closing locally lowers loft. The selected shaft-only iron and wedge cases exhibit
long-left/short-right association. The selected shaft-only low-loft driver cases show the
opposite association; carry also depends on launch, spin, and ball speed,
so the geometric derivative alone does not determine its direction.
Shaft lean changes the delivered geometry and its face--loft coupling.

\textbf{Scoring effects are conditional and small in the selected examples.}
Across eight selected zero-lean conditions per club, Draw minus Straight
strokes gained per 100 shots ranges from \DriverDrawMin{} to \DriverDrawMax{}
for the driver, \SevenIronDrawMin{} to \SevenIronDrawMax{} for the 7-iron and
\PitchingWedgeDrawMin{} to \PitchingWedgeDrawMax{} for the pitching wedge. Fade
minus Straight ranges from \DriverFadeMin{} to \DriverFadeMax{},
\SevenIronFadeMin{} to \SevenIronFadeMax{} and \PitchingWedgeFadeMin{} to
\PitchingWedgeFadeMax{} respectively. Even the largest is about one stroke per
100 modeled shots. These are ranges of condition estimates, not pooled
intervals or per-round improvements.

\textbf{The conclusions survive the assumed clubhead uncertainty.}
Re-simulating every headline condition with each clubhead variant moves the
driver estimates by at most about 0.11 strokes per 100 shots and the irons by
less. Widening every interval to cover those variants changes the direction
of \BandDirectionChanges{} comparison: the pitching-wedge Fade at curve
$2\times$, twist SD $1^\circ$, combined noise (C22) moves from a small positive
estimate to an interval spanning zero. The 7-iron's Fade advantage and Draw
penalty hold in all eight conditions under both statements.

\subsection{How to Read the Figures}
The report graphics use the conventional plotting layout: reported lateral
distance is on the horizontal axis and downrange distance is on the vertical
axis; reported lateral is positive right. The native right-handed model frame
is $(X_n,Y_n,Z_n)=$ forward/target, left, up. Reported endpoint coordinates are
$(X,Y)=(X_n,-Y_n)$, so the plotted lateral sign differs from native $Y_n$.
Contact and club-frame vector equations use the native frame; endpoint and
scoring tables use the reported frame. Density plots show endpoint
populations, with 50\%, 80\%, and 95\% binned empirical highest-density regions;
these are not confidence intervals for a mean. Dots and flight paths are
labelled samples. The baseline club plates use zero lean, curve strength
$1\times$, shaft-twist SD $1^\circ$, and the combined2 profile: path SD
$0.5^\circ$, speed SD 2 mph, and strike SD 0.25 in in each face-plane axis.
Shaft-twist SD is distinct from delivered-face SD. The initial fixed-path
face-SD experiment is retained separately in the historical appendix.

\subsection{Units and Conventions in the Tables}
Distances are in metres with yards in parentheses. Relative strokes gained
(SG) is a pattern's mean minus Straight's on the same shots and is quoted per
100 modeled shots, so $+0.50$ means half a stroke saved per 100 attempts.
Absolute mean SG in the dispersion tables stays per modeled opportunity.
Intervals are 95\% paired percentile intervals. ``Sampling'' intervals carry
Monte Carlo resampling uncertainty only; ``With Head Band'' intervals also
span the clubhead-assumption variants of
Section~\ref{sec:head-band}. Direction is Positive or Negative when the whole
interval lies on one side of zero and Spans 0 otherwise. Each condition is
named by club, curve scale ($1\times$ or $2\times$), shaft-twist SD and noise
profile; ``Shaft Only'' varies shaft twist alone, ``Combined'' adds path,
speed and strike variation. Keys C01--C24 index those conditions.

\clearpage\section{Model, Methods, and Numerical Results}
The current phase-two study supersedes the aimed, fixed-delivery comparisons
recorded below. Its calculation-level design is maintained in
\href{phase2/DESIGN.md}{the Matched-Delivery Design}; its accepted execution,
independent audits, limitations, and remaining publication work are tracked in
\href{phase2/TURNOVER.md}{the Study Turnover}. Reproduction instructions are in
\href{phase2/REPRODUCING.md}{the Saved-Data Reproduction Guide}.

For each feasible straight, draw, and fade condition, nominal face, path, and
clubhead speed are solved so that the unadjusted zero-error landing reaches the
same physical target within 0.05\,m. No post-flight aim rotation supplies that
match. The reference static lofts are $10^\circ$, $34^\circ$, and $46^\circ$
for driver, 7-iron, and pitching wedge, respectively. Each is evaluated at
$0^\circ$, $5^\circ$, and $10^\circ$ forward shaft lean, with Gaussian shaft
rotation standard deviations of $1^\circ$ and $2^\circ$, and two curve scales.
These are geometric modeling assumptions, not measured player distributions.
The earlier delivered-loft presets and lean sensitivity values below describe
phase one only.

Nine delivery-noise profiles include shaft rotation alone, path SD
$0.5^\circ$, speed SD 1 or 2\,mph, horizontal and vertical strike SD
0.25\,in each, combined perturbations, and signed shaft-rotation/path
covariance. The interactive tool supports covariance across all seven modeled
delivery variables. Each feasible condition uses 10,000 paired shot indices
per pattern. Unsupported impacts and infeasible nominal matches remain explicit;
they are not discarded from the experiment inventory.

Driver scoring uses a hypothetical tee-shot fairway/rough context, while
7-iron and wedge scoring use hypothetical approaches. First ground contact is
treated as final lie. Individual-supported dispersion, common-three-pattern
scoring, and common-six-population paired contrasts have different denominators
and must not be conflated. The fixed-target control separates comparisons at a
common distance from within-lean matching; re-matching delivery still prevents
a causal claim that curvature or shaft lean alone improves accuracy.

\subsection{From Delivery Assumptions to Scored Endpoints}
The calculation has five linked stages. A nominal shot is first matched by
forward simulation; a paired error vector is then added to the club delivery;
rigid-body impact computes ball and club post-impact motion; the resulting ball
state is integrated to first ground contact; and a stated course scenario maps
the endpoint to a conditional score. This order matters: endpoint location and
scoring emerge from delivery, contact, and flight together. There is no direct
rule that assigns extra carry to a closed face or extra curvature to a draw.

\textbf{Paired Delivery Errors}\quad$\longrightarrow$\quad
\textbf{Rigid Club--Ball Impact}\\[2pt]
$\longrightarrow$\quad\textbf{RK4 Ball Flight to First Contact}\quad$\longrightarrow$\quad

\end{center}

\textbf{Nominal Matching and Paired Sampling.} For each club, lean, and curve
scale, the study forward-simulates candidate deliveries and solves nominal
face and clubhead speed. The prescribed face-to-path difference sets path from
face, while the target remains $(X_T,0)$ in the native forward/left/up frame.
Acceptance is based on the raw two-coordinate endpoint residual
$\sqrt{(X-X_T)^2+Y^2}\leq0.05$\,m. No post-flight rotation or per-shot aim
correction is used. Each pattern therefore begins at the same nominal point,
but its matched face, path, and speed can differ. A failed bounded search is
recorded as unavailable; the optimizer's exit alone neither proves
reachability nor physical impossibility.
For prescribed face-to-path difference $\delta$, path is $p=f-\delta$.
The bounded least-squares problem is
\[
 (f_*,v_*)=\arg\min_{(f,v)\in\mathcal B}
 \tfrac12\|(X_n(f,v;\delta)-X_T,\;Y_n(f,v;\delta))\|^2.
\]
The original search box uses reference face $f_{\rm ref}\pm3^\circ$ and
speed $[0.75,1.25]v_{\rm ref}$, initialized at the reference face and speed
clipped to the bounds. The reference face is $0^\circ$ for Straight and
$\pm1.5^\circ$ times curve scale for Draw/Fade; their face-to-path
differences are respectively $0^\circ$, $-1.5^\circ$ and $+1.5^\circ$
times curve scale. SciPy's bounded least-squares solver uses
$\texttt{xtol}=\texttt{ftol}=\texttt{gtol}=10^{-11}$ and
$\texttt{max\_nfev}=80$. Both a successful solver termination and the
independently evaluated endpoint tolerance are required. This local search
provides neither a uniqueness guarantee nor a global feasibility proof.
Within-lean targets use the zero-error straight reference at that lean;
fixed-target controls use its zero-lean target. The explicit expanded face and
speed bounds of the supplement are retained in the across-lean controls below.

For each executed condition a single seeded multivariate Gaussian sample is
drawn and reused by shot index for Straight, Draw, and Fade. Its ordered
components are shaft rotation, path, speed, toe/heel strike, high/low strike,
forward lean, and attack angle. The modeled units are degrees, degrees,
metres per second, metres, metres, degrees, and degrees. In selected protocols,
shaft-rotation SD is $1^\circ$ or $2^\circ$, path SD is $0.5^\circ$, speed SD
is 1 or 2 mph, and strike SD is 0.25 inch on each face axis. Paired indices
make contrasts less sensitive to Monte Carlo noise; they do not represent
repeated measurements of the same golfer. The Gaussian distributions and any
correlations are user-specified sensitivity inputs: no empirical golfer
dataset was available to estimate them. Shaft rotation is converted
nonlinearly into delivered face and loft, so the imposed rotation/path
covariance is not itself the realized face/path covariance.

The executed matrices use seed 20261009. With $D$ the diagonal matrix of
primitive SDs and $C$ their correlation matrix, the sampler implements
\[
 \boldsymbol\epsilon_i=D U\Lambda^{1/2}\mathbf z_i,\qquad
 C=U\Lambda U^T,\quad \mathbf z_i\sim\mathcal N(\mathbf0,I_7),\quad
 \Sigma=DCD.
\]
NumPy's default generator supplies the independent standard-normal rows;
correlation eigenvalues are clipped only for validated numerical roundoff.
The same seed and field ordering preserve pairing across the comparisons.
Forward-lean and attack-angle SDs are zero in all nine executed profiles,
although the interface supports them. Each row below also includes the chosen
shaft-twist SD of $1^\circ$ or $2^\circ$.

\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}

only among supported shots are distinct quantities; the reported delivered
face/path diagnostic is conditioned on each pattern's supported shots.

\textbf{Club and Contact Assumptions.} Static loft and lie are taken from the
cited model-year specifications; all remaining club properties are explicit
hybrid research assumptions, not a reconstruction of those products. The

Here CG denotes depth behind the face; $h_{\max}$ and $v_{\max}$ are the

sets pre-impact club angular velocity and ball velocity/spin to zero; shaft
twist specifies club orientation, not a closure angular rate. Club angular
velocity generated by impact is retained in the contact solution.

More precisely, let the central delivered face frame be
$B=[\mathbf n_0,\mathbf h,\mathbf u]$, with $\mathbf h$ heelward and
$\mathbf u$ face-up. For toe-positive offset $a$, high-positive offset $b$,
CG depth $d$, and curvature radii $R_B,R_R$, the adapter uses a local
\emph{parabolic} surface:
\[
 \mathbf r_c=\left[d-\tfrac12(a^2/R_B+b^2/R_R)\right]\mathbf n_0
              -a\mathbf h+b\mathbf u,
\]
\[
 \mathbf n=\frac{\mathbf n_0-(a/R_B)\mathbf h+(b/R_R)\mathbf u}
 {\|\mathbf n_0-(a/R_B)\mathbf h+(b/R_R)\mathbf u\|},\qquad
 I_c=B\,\mathrm{diag}(I_n,I_h,I_u)B^T.
\]
A zero radius denotes a flat direction: its reciprocal terms are omitted.
The normal used in contact is the local $\mathbf n$, while inertia is oriented
by the central frame $B$; the radii do not define an exact spherical face.
The rectangular supported face requires $|a|\le h_{\max}$ and
$|b|\le v_{\max}$. For right-positive path $p$, attack angle $\gamma$,
and speed $v_c$, the pre-impact translational velocity is
\[
 \mathbf v_c^-=v_c(\cos\gamma\cos p,-\cos\gamma\sin p,\sin\gamma)^T.
\]
All trigonometric evaluations use radians after converting the degree-valued
public inputs.

The primary impact law is a two-body rigid-body impulse calculation with
Coulomb friction coefficient $\mu=0.4$ and \emph{energetic restitution}
$e_E=0.83$. In compact form, let $\mathbf c$ be the club-minus-ball relative
velocity at contact, $\mathbf J$ the impulse on the ball, $m_c,m_b$ the club
and ball masses, $\mathbf r_c$ and $\mathbf r_b$ the CG-to-contact levers,
and $I_c,I_b$ the world-frame inertia tensors. The ball is modeled as a solid
sphere with mass $m_b=0.04593$\,kg, radius $R=0.021335$\,m, and
$I_b=(2/5)m_bR^2$ (the scalar multiplying the identity tensor).
The contact-relative velocity and its impulse update are
\[
 \mathbf c=\mathbf v_c+\boldsymbol\omega_c\times\mathbf r_c
             -\mathbf v_b-\boldsymbol\omega_b\times\mathbf r_b.
\]
The relative contact velocity changes as
\[
 \mathbf c^+=\mathbf c^- - K\mathbf J,\qquad
 K=\left(m_c^{-1}+m_b^{-1}\right)I
 -[\mathbf r_c]_\times I_c^{-1}[\mathbf r_c]_\times
 -[\mathbf r_b]_\times I_b^{-1}[\mathbf r_b]_\times.
\]
The impulse updates both bodies' linear velocities and creates angular
velocity through its moment arm:
\[
 \Delta\mathbf v_b=\mathbf J/m_b,\qquad
 \Delta\mathbf v_c=-\mathbf J/m_c.
\]
\[
 \Delta\boldsymbol\omega_b=I_b^{-1}(\mathbf r_b\times\mathbf J),\qquad
 \Delta\boldsymbol\omega_c=-I_c^{-1}(\mathbf r_c\times\mathbf J).
\]
For the ball, $\mathbf r_b=-R\mathbf n$, so tangential impulse contributes
spin about the ball center; for the club, an off-center impulse changes head
rotation through the finite inertia tensor. Contact is integrated in
normal-impulse increments. At each increment the tangential
impulse is selected by a maximum-dissipation Coulomb-disk solve, with
$\|d\mathbf J_t\|\leq\mu\,dJ_n$; normal compression stops when approach
velocity reaches zero. During rebound the accumulated returned normal work
is set to $e_E^2$ times compression work. This is why the primary coefficient
is an energy restitution and not a prescribed ratio of normal velocities when
normal and tangential motion are coupled.
For an orthonormal tangent basis $T$, current relative velocity $\mathbf c$,
and positive normal increment $dP$, the implemented disk problem is
\[
 \mathbf q_* =\arg\min_{\|\mathbf q\|\le\mu dP}
 \left\{\tfrac12\mathbf q^T(T^TKT)\mathbf q
 -[T^T(\mathbf c-dP K\mathbf n)]^T\mathbf q\right\},\qquad
 d\mathbf J=dP\mathbf n+T\mathbf q_*.
\]
The positive compression and rebound work are
\[
 E_c=\int_{\mathrm{compression}}c_n\,dP,\qquad
 E_r=-\int_{\mathrm{rebound}}c_n\,dP=e_E^2 E_c,
 \qquad c_n=\mathbf n^T\mathbf c.
\]
The nominal normal-impulse resolution is $N=128$, defining
$\Delta P_0=c_n^-/[N(\mathbf n^TK\mathbf n)]$; it is not a guarantee of
exactly 128 compression increments. Work uses trapezoidal quadrature, and
48 bisection iterations locate the final compression and rebound increments.
An eligible frictionless or block-diagonal sticking case uses the analytical
fast path instead. The disk solve likewise uses a 48-iteration multiplier
bisection when its unconstrained solution exceeds the Coulomb radius.
Refinement evidence at $N=256,512$ is linked by the design reference.

A separate maximum-dissipation/Newton comparator enforces an instantaneous
normal velocity restitution condition with the same numeric value 0.83 and a
Coulomb impulse cone. That value has a different law definition there. The
comparator is a sensitivity contrast, not the primary contact history and not
a second empirical calibration. Conservation, passivity, and refinement
checks support numerical consistency only. The rigid point-contact model does
not represent calibrated face compliance, shaft flexibility, grooves, local
friction variation, or measured ball/club launch behavior; external empirical
validation remains open.

\textbf{Flight and Endpoint.} The computed post-impact ball velocity and spin
initialize the same frozen Rust flight kernel documented for the historical
study, with the phase-two contact model supplying the launch state. Its state
is $(\mathbf r,\mathbf v,\omega)$, with constant spin-axis direction
$\widehat{\boldsymbol\omega}$ and angular-speed magnitude $\omega$:
\[
 \dot{\mathbf r}=\mathbf v,\qquad
 m_b\dot{\mathbf v}=m_b\mathbf g+\mathbf F_D+\mathbf F_M,\qquad
 \dot\omega=-\lambda\omega,\quad \lambda=0.05\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}.
\]
The launch adapter converts impact spin magnitude from rad/s to RPM at the
Python launch boundary and the native wrapper converts it back to rad/s.
The ODE has exponential spin decay; its seventh state is integrated by RK4,
not an exact exponential update. The actual Python-supplied ball radius is
$R=0.021335$\,m, with the impact mass above and area $A_b=\pi R^2$.
Still-air parameters are $\rho=1.225\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$,
$\mu_a=1.81\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$, and
$\mathbf g=(0,0,-9.80665)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. For relative air velocity
$\mathbf v_a=\mathbf v-\mathbf w$ ($\mathbf w=0$ here), $V=\|\mathbf v_a\|$,
and $\boldsymbol\omega=\omega\widehat{\boldsymbol\omega}$, the active forces are
\[
 \mathbf F_D=-\tfrac12\rho C_D A_b V\mathbf v_a,\qquad
 S=R\|\boldsymbol\omega\|/V,\quad C_L=\min(0.26,0.70S^{0.645}),
\]
\[
 \mathbf F_M=\tfrac12\rho C_L A_b V^2
 \frac{\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf v_a}
 {\|\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf v_a\|}.
\]
Only this single spin-induced force is added. Define
\[
 \mathrm{Re}_c=\operatorname{clip}(\rho V(2R)/\mu_a,10^3,10^7),\quad
 H(z;z_0,w)=\tfrac12[1+\tanh((z-z_0)/w)].
\]
For the supplied base drag coefficient 0.25, the exact active drag curve is
\[
 \begin{split}
 C_D=\operatorname{clip}\bigl(&0.50-0.25H(\log_{10}\mathrm{Re}_c;
                                    \log_{10}(70000),0.16)\\
                         &+0.05H(\log_{10}\mathrm{Re}_c;
                                    \log_{10}(700000),0.45),\;0.15,0.55\bigr).
 \end{split}
\]
These coefficients are the frozen implementation's empirical assumptions;
they are not a new calibration to the modeled ball. The spin force uses total
spin magnitude rather than its component perpendicular to air velocity.
Its magnitude therefore does not decrease smoothly with axis alignment except
at the near-zero cross-product cutoff. The source returns zero spin force
when speed, spin magnitude, or the spin--velocity cross-product norm is below
$10^{-6}$ in their respective SI numerical units; drag is zero below the same
speed threshold. Arbitrary off-center spin predictions inherit this model
limitation. Internal contact conservation does not validate the aerodynamic law.

All seven states use fourth-order Runge--Kutta at $\Delta t=0.02$\,s with a
nominal 12\,s horizon. The wrapper allows $\lfloor12/\Delta t\rfloor+1$
steps, giving a nominal upper bound of 12.02\,s at the production step.
The native ground-stop test is enabled only after $t>0.05$\,s to exclude the
initial zero-height state. Failure to return a landing is a numerical failure,
not a substituted airborne endpoint. Launch position is the origin at zero
height. Between the last
above-ground state and first nonpositive-height state, landing is
\[
 \mathbf r_{\rm land}=\mathbf r_j+
 \frac{z_j}{z_j-z_{j+1}}(\mathbf r_{j+1}-\mathbf r_j).
\]
The startup guard is an implementation qualification for extremely short
flights: it can pass an earlier ground crossing before termination is enabled.
This linear event interpolation does not inherit a fourth-order landing-time
accuracy guarantee merely from RK4 state integration. First ground contact is
treated as the hypothetical final lie for scoring. Bounce, roll, turf response,
obstacles, wind variation, and elevation are not simulated.
Unsupported off-face, nonapproaching, or nonairborne samples are kept with
their failure status, never clipped or resampled. Endpoint distributions are
therefore conditional on supported airborne shots when failures occur.

\textbf{Conditional Scoring and Interpretation.} The driver case assumes a
flat 400\,m hole with a 30\,m fairway corridor; its carry endpoint is assigned
a fairway or rough lie. Iron and wedge cases assume a fairway approach to a
centered 15\,m-radius green, with rough outside. A source-backed historical
PGA expected-strokes benchmark is interpolated at those assumed lie and
distance states. Fairway and rough values come from the historical published
table; short-putt values use an anchor-reconciled approximate putting fit. The
result is an approximate 2003--2010 population benchmark, not a current
player-specific model. SG is consequently conditional on supported
endpoints and these geometry/baseline choices; it is not measured performance
or an expected per-round improvement. Within-condition paired SG contrasts
use shot indices supported in all three patterns; between-condition contrasts
use the common supported indices across all six populations. Bootstrap
intervals quantify sampling variation for those simulated populations only. They do not include uncertainty
in club properties, contact law, flight physics, human delivery distributions,
course geometry, or the scoring benchmark.

\textbf{Endpoint Statistics and Binned Density.} For a pattern's $n$ supported
reported endpoints $\mathbf z_i=(X_i,Y_i)^T$ and target
$\mathbf z_T=(X_T,0)^T$, let
\[
 \bar{\mathbf z}=n^{-1}\sum_i\mathbf z_i,\quad
 \widehat\Sigma_z=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_i
 (\mathbf z_i-\bar{\mathbf z})(\mathbf z_i-\bar{\mathbf z})^T,
 \quad\mathbf b=\bar{\mathbf z}-\mathbf z_T.
\]
Lateral and downrange SDs are the square roots of the corresponding diagonal
entries; covariance describes orientation, while target RMSE includes bias:
\[
 \mathrm{RMSE}^2=n^{-1}\sum_i\|\mathbf z_i-\mathbf z_T\|^2
 =\|\mathbf b\|^2+\frac{n-1}{n}\operatorname{tr}(\widehat\Sigma_z).
\]
Radial target quantiles use $\|\mathbf z_i-\mathbf z_T\|$, and containment is
the fraction within the stated radius. These are distinct from radial carry
$\|\mathbf z_i\|$. A narrow one-dimensional endpoint locus is not evidence of
a calibrated two-dimensional player dispersion ellipse.

The unsmoothed histogram uses common spatial bin edges across the compared
patterns. For bin $j$ of area $a_j$ and count $n_j$, density is
$\widehat f_j=n_j/(n a_j)$, so $\sum_j\widehat f_j a_j=1$ separately for each
supported pattern population. Bins are ordered by density and retained through
the threshold needed for 50\%, 80\%, or 95\% cumulative mass; all tied bins at
the cutoff are included. Achieved mass can therefore exceed the named level.
Bin resolution and achieved mass are reporting quantities, not physical
precision; these regions depend on the grid and are not smooth population
density estimates or confidence regions for a mean. No Gaussian width or KDE
is imposed on singular or nearly one-dimensional samples.

\subsection{Rigid Shaft Twist, Delivered Loft, and Forward Lean}
Phase two pitches a fixed physical club, unlike the historical phase-one
axis-only lean control. Let static loft be $L$, lie be $\beta$, forward lean
be $\ell$, nominal right-positive face direction be $f$, and shaft twist be
$\theta$. In the native forward/left/up frame,
\[
 \mathbf n_0=(\cos L,0,\sin L)^T,\qquad
 \mathbf s_0=(0,\cos\beta,\sin\beta)^T,
\]
\[
 Q=R_z(-f)R_y(\ell),\qquad \mathbf s=Q\mathbf s_0,\qquad
 \mathbf n=R_{\mathbf s}(\theta)Q\mathbf n_0.
\]
Here $R_{\mathbf s}$ is Rodrigues rotation about the delivered shaft axis.
Zero twist gives delivered loft $\lambda=L-\ell$. The local sensitivities are
\[
 \frac{df}{d\theta}=-\frac{\sin\beta\cos L}{\cos(L-\ell)},\qquad
 \frac{d\lambda}{d\theta}=-\cos\beta,\qquad
 \frac{d\lambda}{df}=\cot\beta\frac{\cos(L-\ell)}{\cos L}.
\]
Thus closing the face by this assumed rotation tends to reduce loft. Fixed
shaft-twist SD has the same first-order loft SD across lean, but not the same
face-angle SD. Twist is an orientation error, not clubhead closure angular
velocity. Finite-angle geometry and impact/flight determine the endpoints;
these derivatives do not require every closed-face miss to travel farther.
The \href{phase2/DESIGN.md}{Phase-Two Design} and
\href{phase2/validation/GEOMETRY_DERIVATIVES.md}{Independent Derivative Checks}
provide the equations and their numerical checks.

\subsection{Distance Confounding and Scoring Interpretation}
Nominal landing matching removes an initial target mismatch; it does not
force equal realized carry distributions. Within-lean targets may differ
across lean. Fixed-target controls hold the physical reference distance while
rematching face, path and speed. Consequently, a change in spread or SG between
these conditions cannot be uniquely attributed to shaft lean, curvature or
distance alone. Lateral SD, downrange SD, bias and target error must be read
together. Marginal statistics cannot partition their causal contributions.

\subsection{Uncertainty in the Headline Comparisons}
\label{sec:head-band}
The deterministic check above perturbs single deliveries. The headline
comparisons instead quote two statements for each condition and pattern.

\textbf{Sampling interval.} Relative SG is the mean of rowwise differences,
pattern minus Straight, on the shots that all three patterns support. The
interval is the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile of \BootstrapResamples{} paired
bootstrap means drawn from those rows with the condition's recorded seed. The
frozen edition used 500 resamples; recomputing at 500 from the saved shots
reproduces every frozen interval to floating-point precision, so the larger
count changes only the
precision of the interval endpoints. No flight is re-simulated for this step.

\textbf{Head-assumption band.} Each headline condition is re-run under each
one-factor clubhead variant: centre-of-gravity depth $\times0.8$ and
$\times1.2$; all three principal inertias together $\times0.8$ and
$\times1.2$; for the driver, bulge and roll radii together $\times0.8$ and
$\times1.2$; and the Newton velocity-restitution contact law in place of the
energetic restitution law. A variant run repeats the complete protocol:
nominal face, path and speed are rematched to the variant's own Straight
landing, then the same seeded delivery errors, scoring and bootstrap are
applied. This gives \BandVariantRuns{} runs (seven per driver condition, five
per iron condition) and \BandShots{} simulated shots; \BandUnavailableRuns{}
runs left the model domain. Every variant kept the scoring status of its
nominal condition. The band is the smallest interval containing the nominal
sampling interval and every variant's sampling interval. The $\pm20\%$ ranges
are declared engineering assumptions, not measured distributions, so the band
is a sensitivity envelope rather than a probability statement.

\textbf{Reproduction check.} Before the variants were trusted, the same
runner re-simulated all \ReproCells{} headline conditions with the nominal
head and compared them shot by shot with the saved study:
\ReproShots{} shots, \ReproStatusMismatches{} support-status mismatches and
a largest endpoint difference of \ReproMaxEndpointDifference{}\,m. The band
therefore measures the head assumptions, not drift in the code.

\section{Corrected Central-Contact Impact Equations}
The experiment calls the public \texttt{ImpactSolverAPI} rigid-body model.
For unit face normal $\mathbf n$, relative centre velocity
$\mathbf v_r=\mathbf v_c^- -\mathbf v_b^-$, ball mass $m_b$, club mass $m_c$,
restitution $e$, ball radius $R$, and inertia $I_b=2m_bR^2/5$:
\[
 m_*=(m_b^{-1}+m_c^{-1})^{-1},\qquad
 J_n=(1+e)m_*\mathbf v_r\cdot\mathbf n.
\]
The ball contact point is $-R\mathbf n$ from its centre. Tangential surface
slip, including initial ball spin, and its effective inverse mass are
\[
 \mathbf u_t=\mathbf v_r-(\mathbf v_r\cdot\mathbf n)\mathbf n
                 +R(\boldsymbol\omega_b^-\times\mathbf n),\qquad
 D=m_b^{-1}+m_c^{-1}+R^2/I_b.
\]
For positive normal approach, Coulomb friction supplies
\[
 \mathbf J_t=\min\{\mu J_n,\|\mathbf u_t\|/D\}
                 \frac{\mathbf u_t}{\|\mathbf u_t\|},\qquad
 \mathbf J=J_n\mathbf n+\mathbf J_t.
\]
The tangential impulse is zero at zero slip or zero friction. Both linear
velocities and ball spin receive their corresponding impulse:
\[
 \mathbf v_b^+=\mathbf v_b^-+\mathbf J/m_b,\quad
 \mathbf v_c^+=\mathbf v_c^- -\mathbf J/m_c,\quad
 \boldsymbol\omega_b^+=\boldsymbol\omega_b^-+
                 (R/I_b)(\mathbf J_t\times\mathbf n).
\]
Earlier result bundles omitted $\mathbf J_t$ from both translational updates
and used the infinite-club sticking cap $(2/7)m_b\|\mathbf u_t\|$.
Those bundles are superseded historical controls. Regression tests first
failed against those defects, then passed with the correction. Independent
arbitrary-3D tests check linear and angular momentum, normal restitution,
nonincreasing kinetic energy, sticking contact, initial spin, and rotation
covariance. These establish internal consistency of central contact with a
translating club; off-centre club rotation, shaft response, and measured launch
accuracy remain unqualified.

\section{Geometric Face--Loft Coupling and Shaft Lean}
This is an explicitly assumed rigid geometry, not observed player data.
The native right-handed frame is $+X$ forward, $+Y$ left, and $+Z$ up.
User face angle is positive right. At zero twist, the delivered face normal is
$\mathbf n_0=(\cos L,0,\sin L)$ for nominal delivered loft $L$.
Given assumed shaft elevation $\ell$ and forward lean $a$:
\[
 s_x=\sin\ell\tan a,\qquad
 \mathbf s=(s_x,\sqrt{\cos^2\ell-s_x^2},\sin\ell).
\]
This construction is valid only when its square-root argument is nonnegative.
Rodrigues rotation about the unit shaft axis gives
\[
 \mathbf n(\theta)=\mathbf n_0\cos\theta+
 (\mathbf s\times\mathbf n_0)\sin\theta+
 \mathbf s(\mathbf s\cdot\mathbf n_0)(1-\cos\theta).
\]
Delivered face and loft are extracted as
\[
 F=\operatorname{atan2}(-n_y,n_x),\qquad
 L_d=\operatorname{atan2}(n_z,\sqrt{n_x^2+n_y^2}).
\]
Near zero twist,
\[
 \frac{dF}{d\theta}=-(s_z-s_x\tan L),\quad
 \frac{dL_d}{d\theta}=-s_y,\quad
 \frac{dL_d}{dF}=\frac{s_y}{s_z-s_x\tan L}.
\]
With zero lean, $dL_d/dF=\cot\ell$: closing tends to reduce loft in this
specific rotation mode. Rotation about the vertical axis instead preserves
loft. Face angle alone therefore does not identify delivered loft.

The main comparison applies this geometry to the face error $F-F_0$ around
each pattern's nominal face $F_0$, then rotates the axis and normal together
by the nominal target yaw. All shapes share the same zero-error delivered
loft, speed, attack angle, and strike. This isolates sensitivity to the assumed
face--loft coupling without giving one shape a different baseline loft.
Fixed-loft trials provide the corresponding control. Separate shaft-lean
sensitivity holds nominal loft fixed and changes only the axis; it does not
represent pitching a fixed physical club forward. That latter operation
changes nominal loft as well and must be analyzed separately.

Less delivered loft does not necessarily increase carry. Launch, spin, speed,
and aerodynamic trajectory jointly determine carry, so long-left/short-right
behavior is measured from the modeled endpoint covariance rather than imposed.
Static manufacturer lie is used only as an assumed delivered shaft elevation;
dynamic lie, shaft bending, and golfer covariance are unmeasured.

\section{Flight Integration and Landing}
Each shot calls the existing \texttt{BallFlightSimulator}, backed by the
\texttt{upstream-physics} Rust kernel. Launch velocity uses metres per second,
launch and azimuth angles radians, and spin magnitude RPM at the Python
boundary, converted to radians per second inside the kernel. The spin axis
is normalized. The solver integrates position, velocity and decaying spin
with RK4, drag and spin-induced aerodynamic force:
\[
 \dot{\mathbf r}=\mathbf v,\qquad
 m_b\dot{\mathbf v}=m_b\mathbf g+\mathbf F_D+\mathbf F_M,\qquad
 \dot\omega=-\lambda\omega.
\]
The production step is $0.02\,\mathrm{s}$ with a $12\,\mathrm{s}$ horizon.
Landing is linearly interpolated between the last above-ground and first
nonpositive-height samples. Failure to land raises an error; it never
silently treats the final airborne sample as a landing.
The Rust coefficient path is distinct from the constant-spin Waterloo/Penner
model in \texttt{flight\_models.py}; their calibrations must not be mixed.
The kernel applies a single spin force (not separate additive backspin lift
and sidespin Magnus forces). With $A=\pi R^2$ and relative airspeed $V$:
\[
 \mathbf F_D=-\tfrac12\rho C_D A V\mathbf v,\qquad
 S=R|\boldsymbol\omega|/V,\qquad
 C_L=\min(0.26,\;0.70 S^{0.645}),
\]
\[
 \mathbf F_M=\tfrac12\rho C_L A V^2
 \frac{\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf v}
 {|\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf v|}.
\]
The zero-cross-product case returns zero spin force. The shared drag-crisis
curve evaluates Reynolds number $\rho V(2R)/\mu$ with
$\mu=1.81\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$ and base drag coefficient $0.25$,
using the exact source-bound constants in \texttt{aerodynamics.rs}.
Spin decay uses $\lambda=0.05\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$.

\section{Aiming and Outcome Statistics}
Raw outcomes keep the requested target-relative angles. A second comparison
rotates each entire pattern by a single angle computed from its nominal,
zero-error landing, placing that nominal landing on the target line. This is
an aiming adjustment, not per-shot correction or a sample-mean fit. The
fixed target distance is the straight nominal carry; differences in curved
carry remain in the error metric.
\[
 \alpha=\operatorname{atan2}(y_0,x_0),\qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}x'\\y'\end{pmatrix}=
 \begin{pmatrix}\cos\alpha&\sin\alpha\\-\sin\alpha&\cos\alpha\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Lateral variance is the unbiased sample variance with denominator $n-1$;
standard deviation is its square root. Central 90\% width is the empirical
95th minus 5th percentile. Target RMSE is
\[\operatorname{RMSE}=\sqrt{n^{-1}\sum_i[(x_i-x_T)^2+y_i^2]}.\] Target-hit probability counts
landings within a $15\,\mathrm m$ radius circle, not a fairway strip.
Paired percentile-bootstrap variance-ratio intervals resample common shot
indices 500 times. These intervals describe Monte Carlo error only; they
exclude model discrepancy and parameter uncertainty.

\section{Distance Control}
Carry is not fixed: face error changes impact and spin, and curved patterns
have shorter mean carry. The supplementary common-range diagnostic maps each
nominal-aimed endpoint to
\[
 (x'',y'')=\frac{x_T}{\sqrt{x'^2+y'^2}}(x',y').
\]
It preserves landing bearing and sets every radial range to the same straight
nominal distance. It is geometric normalization, not a new flight simulation,
and cannot isolate a causal physical mechanism. The superseded historical controls suggested a small residual effect after
normalization. Their values do not establish the corrected-model result; all

\section{Club-Appropriate Scoring Estimates}
The driver scenario is a tee shot on a hypothetical flat $400\,\mathrm m$
hole with a fairway corridor of total width $30\,\mathrm m$. Endpoints inside
that corridor use the fairway benchmark; outside use rough. Remaining distance
is $d_i=\sqrt{(400-x_i)^2+y_i^2}$. Hole distances 350 and 450 m and corridor
widths 20 and 40 m provide layout sensitivities. The tee starting benchmark
must come from the published tee table, not a fairway approach substitute.

For the 7-iron and pitching wedge, the scenario is an approach from fairway at
the straight nominal carry, with the hole at that target and a circular green
of radius $15\,\mathrm m$. Inside the green the benchmark uses putting distance;
outside it uses unobstructed rough distance. Green radii 10 and 20 m provide
layout sensitivities. There are no bunkers, water, out-of-bounds, slopes,
bounce, or roll. Carry endpoints are assumed final resting locations in both
contexts; actual driver total distance and wedge stopping behavior are not
modeled. The original approximately 195 m driver-to-green experiment is only
a superseded historical control.

For historical-tour expected strokes $J(d,\ell)$ at distance $d$ and lie
$\ell$, the existing source-backed Tools analysis computes
\[
 SG_i=J(d_{\rm start},\ell_{\rm start})-1-J(d_i,\ell_i).
\]
All patterns share the starting state, so their relative benefit depends only
on the difference in expected strokes remaining. Reported benefits are per
shot, not per round. Multiplying by an assumed number of identical
approaches is an illustration, not a full-course simulation.
The green benchmark represents an \emph{unholed} putting state. With valid
one-, two-, and three-putt probabilities,
\[
 J_{\rm putt}=p_1+2p_2+3p_3=2-p_1+p_3\ge1,
 \qquad \lim_{d\downarrow0}J_{\rm putt}(d)=1.
\]
A separately explicit already-holed terminal state has zero strokes remaining.
No endpoint in this carry-only study is assumed holed; hole capture is not
modeled. The original baseline incorrectly placed terminal zero in the same
putting interpolation table. Between zero and its first positive knot at
$5.3975\,\mathrm{cm}$, it awarded partial hole-outs. At $1\,\mathrm{cm}$ this
produced approximately $0.815$ artificial extra SG. In the fixed-loft one-degree
controls it inflated straight means by approximately $0.0053$ strokes for
7-iron and $0.0103$ for PW. Direct API/cache parity reproduced this error and
therefore did not establish physical validity. The corrected baseline uses the
unholed tap-in anchor of one, a new version/digest, and rescoring of all saved
endpoints. Pre-correction scoring artifacts are retained as failed experiments.

The historical fairway and rough values come from Broadie's published
benchmark table. The putting approximation and its source reconciliation are
recorded in the scoring provenance. In the 2011 preprint, literal evaluation
of the displayed three-putt formula with the listed coefficient order gives
negative probabilities and conflicts with the stated anchors; that literal
experiment was rejected. A reconciled approximation must not be represented
as an independently verified exact reproduction of the author's fit.
The implemented approximate three-putt expression uses putt distance $d_f$
in feet:
\[
 \widetilde p_3=\frac{1}{1+\exp(5.49-0.106d_f+0.000563d_f^2)}-0.00398,
 \qquad p_3=\min\{\widetilde p_3,1-p_1\}.
\]
The upper bound prevents a negative two-putt probability for tap-ins; the
published one-putt expression uses yards. The baseline supports only its
explicit distance range and rejects unsupported queries rather than extrapolating.
Sampling intervals exclude putting-model uncertainty, green-layout error,
and impact-model discrepancy.

For the corrected matrix, the public Tools API evaluates every explicit
benchmark distance knot for each fixed starting state and finishing lie.
Endpoint SG is reconstructed by bounded piecewise-linear interpolation of
those returned SG values. This reuses the public API's outputs without
copying its expected-strokes formula. Knots, midpoints, and random supported
queries are checked against direct API evaluation to $10^{-12}$ strokes.
Receipts distinguish shots scored from API-evaluated states. Unsupported
states are reported unavailable; neither starts nor endpoints are extrapolated.

\section{Interpretation and Observability Limits}
The relevant question is whether curved patterns reduce spread or target
error after equal aiming treatment, not whether their nominal balls curve
back toward the line. Fixed-loft centered still-air delivery is mirror symmetric. Keeping the same
right-handed shaft axis in coupled mode does not preserve that reflection:
$L_d(-\delta)\ne L_d(\delta)$ in general. Thus conditional draw/fade
differences can arise structurally from assumed face--loft covariance and aiming
as well as finite sampling; they do not establish a player-specific preference. A nonzero face-to-path mean also does
not eliminate opposite curvature: a $1.5$-standard-deviation offset leaves
about $6.68\%$ probability of crossing zero face-to-path under the assumed
normal error distribution. At two degrees SD, the theoretical crossing
probability rises to about $22.66\%$. Real golfers may have correlated face/path
errors, different variability by intention, strike-dependent gear effect,
wind, varying speed or loft, and asymmetric hazards. Those require measured
inputs and additional experiments.
With only one uncertain input, the landing set is a narrow one-dimensional
curve in the horizontal plane, not a measured two-dimensional dispersion
ellipse. No ellipse-area advantage is inferred from this experiment.
For intuition, a local linear landing model
$Y\simeq aF+b(F-P)$ with fixed path gives
$\operatorname{Var}(Y)\simeq(a+b)^2\sigma_F^2$, independent of the mean
face and path if the sensitivities are constant. Any simulated spread
difference therefore arises from changing nonlinear sensitivities, not the
mere existence of a nonzero curvature mean. This linearization explains the
comparison; it is not used to generate any trajectory or landing.

\section{Evidence and Reproduction}
The adjacent README and checked-in result bundle record numerical results,
validation commands, dependency/source hashes and graphic provenance.
The graphics show a limited set of representative flights while the
dispersion and statistics use all 10,000 shots per pattern. Full fresh reproduction uses a new output directory so preserved historical
controls are never overwritten. Existing corrected bundles may instead be
validated and summarized by the overview command.
\begin{verbatim}
python3 -m src.tools.shot_pattern_analysis.matrix \
  shot_pattern_reproduction/corrected_results
python3 -m src.tools.shot_pattern_analysis.overview \
  shot_pattern_reproduction/corrected_results \
  --output-dir shot_pattern_reproduction/overview
python3 -m src.tools.shot_pattern_analysis.sensitivity \
  --output shot_pattern_reproduction/sensitivity.json
python3 -m src.tools.shot_pattern_analysis.numerics \
  --output shot_pattern_reproduction/numerics.json
\end{verbatim}
The native Rust dependency must be installed from this checkout. Compilation
of this reference is a document check, not scientific acceptance.

\section{External Context}

path and relates it to curvature; see
\url{https://www.trackman.com/blog/face-to-path} and
\url{https://www.trackman.com/blog/spin-axis}.
Historical strokes-gained methodology and benchmarks are described in
Broadie, \emph{Assessing Golfer Performance on the PGA TOUR},
\url{https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/4996/assessing_golfer_performance.full.pdf}.
These definitions support the input interpretation, not validation of this
repository's numerical predictions.
