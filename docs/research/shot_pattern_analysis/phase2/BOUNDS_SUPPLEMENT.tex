\section{Nominal Matching Bounds
Supplement}\label{nominal-matching-bounds-supplement}

\subsection{Scope and Scientific
Question}\label{scope-and-scientific-question}

This is a separate calculation-level reference for the phase-two
matched-shot study and
\href{https://github.com/D-sorganization/UpstreamDrift/issues/12000}{issue
\#12000}. It records an adapted search protocol after the original
324-cell and 72-cell plans were frozen. Those results retain their
original bounds and source receipts. A bounded optimizer returning no
match does not prove that a shot is physically impossible. No
supplemental Monte Carlo result is reported here.

The original nominal matching search gave each pattern a face-angle
interval centered on its reference face, with half-width \(3^\circ\),
and club speed between \(0.75v_0\) and \(1.25v_0\). The match solved raw
landing, without rotating a trajectory after flight. Strong
pitching-wedge curves at zero lean were outside that face interval even
though a wider bounded search found matches. This supplement keeps the
original model, seed, target definitions, noise profiles, and
\(0.05\,\mathrm{m}\) nominal-landing tolerance.

\subsection{Geometry, Units, and
Calculation}\label{geometry-units-and-calculation}

The positive face and path directions are right of the target for a
right-handed player; forward landing is \(x\) and right lateral landing
is \(y\). The draw has face-to-path \(-3^\circ\) at curve scale two, so
\(p=f+3^\circ\); the fade mirrors that relation. For each candidate, the
solver chooses face \(f\) and club speed \(v\) such that the physical
first-ground-contact endpoint \((x(f,p,v),y(f,p,v))\) is within
\(0.05\,\mathrm{m}\) of the stated \((x_\mathrm{target},0)\). Static
loft, shaft lean, attack, head properties, contact law, flight
integration, and scoring remain those in the parent
\href{DESIGN.md}{matched-study design} and the exact source-bound
receipt.

The absolute face interval for all three supplemental protocols is
\([-6^\circ,+6^\circ]\). The pitching-wedge protocols retain speed
factors \([0.75,1.25]\), which yield \([24,40]\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) for
the illustrative \(32\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) preset. The separate driver
speed sensitivity uses \([0.75,1.30]\), or
\([33.75,58.5]\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) for the \(45\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
preset. These are \emph{optimizer bounds}, not observations of a
golfer\textquotesingle s achievable delivery. A Gaussian perturbation
outside a physical simulation\textquotesingle s domain remains an
unsupported sample rather than being clipped or resampled.

Each cell has three physical patterns and, if accepted for the final

treating them as aliases in a comparison.

These protocols were specified \textbf{after} the feasibility audit and
\textbf{before} any supplemental Monte Carlo. They are sensitivity
analyses, not part of the original preregistration. The exact proposed
26-cell names and four references are in
\texttt{review\_followups/SUPPLEMENTAL\_EXECUTION\_INVENTORY.json} in

numerical bounds in immutable \texttt{matrix\_plan.json} files under
separate output roots.

\subsection{Probe Evidence and Numerical
Method}\label{probe-evidence-and-numerical-method}

Read-only native nominal probes found a PW zero-lean strong draw at face
\(-0.260459639^\circ\), path \(+2.739540361^\circ\), and speed
\(32.045914261\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) for a \(77.402477249\,\mathrm{m}\)
target. The mirrored fade also matched; fine-step replay residual was
\(0.000211396\,\mathrm{m}\). At \(5^\circ\) lean, PW strong draw matched
at face \(-0.076707602^\circ\), path \(+2.923292398^\circ\), speed
\(29.161308145\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), fine residual
\(0.000289371\,\mathrm{m}\). The driver \(5^\circ\) lean strong draw
required \(56.345074274\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), only
\(0.095074274\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) above the original \(56.25\) upper
bound; its fine residual was \(0.000943089\,\mathrm{m}\). These are
deterministic model probes, not observed outcomes or Monte Carlo
results. Detailed inputs, solver starts, and source hashes are retained
in the three probe receipts in the main study\textquotesingle s
\texttt{review\_followups} directory.

The implementation uses the existing native contact and ball-flight
solver, SciPy nominal optimizer, \texttt{ExperimentConfig},
\texttt{matched\_matrix\_cells}, and \texttt{run\_matched\_matrix}. The
supplement selector supplies only a reviewed cell set and bounds. The
existing runner preserves common random samples, full unsupported rows,
immutable cell manifests, source snapshots, and a separate reviewed
final-stage acceptance gate. No numerical tolerance or contact parameter
changed. A native integration test checks a zero-lean PW raw match and
exact default-bound parity with the original explicit bounds.

\subsection{Acceptance and
Reproduction}\label{acceptance-and-reproduction}

Before any 10,000-shot supplemental run, a 32-shot source-bound pilot
must establish all selected cells\textquotesingle{} status, numerical
refinement, raw-landing and figure checks, independent review, and an
acceptance receipt for this source snapshot. The supplemental auditor
must check its own 26-cell allowlist and four alias proofs; it must not
relax the original 324/72-cell auditor. A nominal matching exception
means only that the solver did not find a match under its stated finite
search box and starts. Runtime physics errors remain errors and are
never silently reclassified as bounded unavailability.

\subsection{Limitations and Failed
Search}\label{limitations-and-failed-search}

The original strong PW zero-lean bounded search failed to find a nominal
draw; the widened face search found one. The original strong driver
\(5^\circ\) lean fixed-target search remained about
\(0.635\,\mathrm{m}\) short at its speed cap, and the separately labeled
speed extension found a deterministic match. These changes reveal
sensitivity to arbitrary optimizer boxes, not evidence of real-world
shot controllability. The entire study remains an illustrative
rigid-body, no-wind, first-ground-contact simulation with assumed club
geometry, contact properties, and Gaussian delivery variation. It does
not include roll or measured golfer covariance, and conditional strokes
gained is scenario scoring rather than empirical player performance.
